\begin{abstract}
Automated theorem proving can be viewed as navigation through an implicit directed maze: proof states are vertices, legal inference steps are edges, and verifier-accepted certificates are absorbing exits. This viewpoint separates two problems that are often conflated. Shortest-path algorithms are efficient on many explicit graphs, but a compact logical problem may induce an enormous proof-state graph. The practical objective is therefore not merely to choose a path well after the maze is exposed, but to expose as little of the maze as possible while preserving certificate correctness.

The paper develops this program in three layers. First, it prioritizes exact or conservative reductions---including quotienting, partial-order reduction, admissible abstractions, verified landmarks, proof-horizon bounds, and related graph reductions---whenever their hypotheses apply. Second, when exact reduction is insufficient, it treats automated reasoning as a feedback-control problem in which learned quantities such as drift, variance, Bellman values, hazard, motif scores, and resource allocations guide search but never replace the independent verifier. Third, it formalizes discovery as a verifier-backed event and introduces graded discovery-state quantities for experiments in which a binary success/failure endpoint is too coarse.

A central mathematical connection in Version 0.4 is the Bellman--Abel correspondence for discovery. If an optimal Bellman value represents expected time-to-discovery and $A^*=-V^*$, then before discovery the optimal recursion induces an Abel-type translation law; with nonnegative rational action costs, the translation is cost adjusted. Approximate Abel coordinates, Bellman--Abel residuals, drift, variance, and action-gap stability then provide measurable signals for successor selection and experimental diagnosis.

The empirical methodology is deliberately forward chained and certificate first. Historical solutions are hidden at evaluation time, models are trained only on earlier audited rungs, and the rule is \emph{learn between experiments; freeze within experiments}. The first strict nine-rung H2 study is retained as a negative control: under a matched budget of $R=8$ verifier calls per target per action, all five intervention families reached the same two of nine rungs. Thus the current paper makes no end-to-end ATP speedup claim. Instead, that result identifies a measurement limitation of binary discovery gain and motivates the next transition-level experiment using graded Abel-state logging.

Semi-ideal computers, ATPs, automated logical deciders, automated metalogical deciders, reflective search, absorbing fixed points, semigroup and IFS descriptions, and DATA as a distinguished implementation are developed as compatible layers of the same certificate-preserving framework. Their role is to enlarge the control vocabulary without changing the authority condition: settlement is accepted only when an independent verifier accepts the corresponding certificate.
\end{abstract}
