\section{Conclusion}
The central proposal is that theorem proving and conjecture settlement can be studied as exact rationally weighted maze solving over verifier-backed state graphs. The internal search process may be nonmonotone, stochastic, learned, or self-reflective, while its cumulative history of verified information forms a monotone SIC. Under additional target-search conditions that SIC is an ATP; with typed proof/refutation/independence certificates it becomes an ALD; one logical level higher it becomes an AMLD.

The graph-theoretic perspective matters because it imports a large body of route-finding ideas subject to explicit hypotheses. For finite nonnegative rational weights, Dijkstra-style minimum-cost search is exact. With admissible lower bounds, A*-style and branch-and-bound pruning can reduce the region below an incumbent. Novelty DAGs eliminate verified-state cycles, conservative compilation replaces repeated verified paths by macro edges, and shared lemma banks turn repeated proof structure into reusable landmarks. These reductions do not solve undecidability or guarantee practical access to every fixed point. They narrow the ocean in which the machine must search.

The discovery layer adds a different measurable question. A search may reach a certificate without learning a reusable mechanism, or it may first acquire a verified novelty that changes what later search can reach or how cheaply it can settle targets. By separating weak discovery from operational discovery, and by measuring discovery hazard and intervention gain on sealed earlier rungs, the framework makes this distinction experimentally testable. A forward-chaining controller may learn from previous rung trajectories while remaining frozen with respect to the rung it is currently attempting.

The Abel layer addresses the cases in which the strict binary endpoint is scientifically clean but statistically coarse. When optimal time-to-discovery satisfies Bellman recursion, its negative is an Abel coordinate: under unit cost an optimal pre-discovery action advances expected Abel position by one, while exact rational transaction costs produce the corresponding cost-adjusted translation law. In realistic search this coordinate must be approximated. The relevant questions are therefore whether held-out Abel drift is positive, whether Bellman-Abel residual and variance remain controlled, whether approximation error is small relative to the action gap, and whether the action chosen by the approximate coordinate improves future verifier-backed discovery rather than merely correlating with retrospective success.

The first leak-free nine-rung H2 discovery experiment is deliberately retained as a negative control. Ordinary search, motif development, residual-goal repair, representation modulation, and counterpoint search all reached exactly the same two of nine targets at $R=8$. Thus binary matched discovery gain was zero for every nonbaseline arm, and a hindsight oracle over the five arms could not exceed the ordinary-search reachable set. That failure is informative: it says that the next iteration should record graded state transitions and test an Abel coordinate, not rescue the result by tuning a global exploration parameter after the fact.

The experimental-design principle is therefore cumulative but strict: learn between experiments and freeze within experiments. A completed run may suggest a new state feature, representation move, motif grammar, loss function, or controller; the change receives a new protocol version. Pre-reveal predictions, verifier gates, budgets, dependencies, seeds, and treatment grammars are preserved so that each rung can be audited as a prospective test. Transition-level data are clustered within rungs rather than counted as independent theorem replications, and a controller is promoted toward a live frontier only after sealed cross-family evidence supports transfer.

The motif-semigroup picture adds a disciplined vocabulary for structured variation. Instead of injecting a fixed fraction of noise, a controller can develop, transpose, invert, combine, quotient, decompose, or modulate a proof motif when earlier controlled experiments indicate that such a mechanism has positive discovery or Abel advantage in a comparable state. The transformed proposal remains untrusted until verification. This keeps creativity, discovery, functional iteration, and control inside the same certificate-first architecture as the rest of the paper.

The further reflective possibility is that the induced ATP can itself reason about a coded source ATP, ALD, AMLD, or controller. If such meta-theorems can be verified and fed back into search, the machine does not merely learn statistically from its trajectories; it can sometimes use proved facts about its own frozen search architecture. DATA is a distinguished laboratory for this general theory, not its definition.
