One exceptionally small initial hypothesis

The test did not begin with a muscle infarction, an aneurysm, or knowledge that the patient was Gregory House. It began with the words “Leg pain.” Brian knew the television plot; Sol was given a staged presentation rather than the concealed ending, with DATA MIND discovery control supporting the exploration. The original prompt alone did not determine a diagnosis.

The ordinary differential and the search basin

Further evidence described sudden unilateral mid-thigh pain, eventually characterized as severe enough to prevent weight-bearing. The leg looked essentially normal and distal pulses were preserved. There was no trauma or exercise trigger, and both muscle contraction and stretching worsened the pain. There was no swelling, tense compartment, or anticoagulant use.

Conventional suggestions included arterial ischemia, compartment syndrome, deep-vein thrombosis, muscle injury, radiculopathy, and infection. The clues weakened familiar explanations but did not satisfactorily explain the combination of excruciating focal pain and scant visible findings. The search had entered what the experiment called a basin: many plausible nearby ideas but diminishing explanatory gain.

The evidence-conditioned Eureka move

Only then did the instruction request exactly one novel mechanism outside the ordinary differential, with a discriminating test. Sol proposed spontaneous skeletal-muscle infarction / ischemic myonecrosis and suggested muscle-focused MRI. The tissue-level mechanism could explain focal, intense pain despite preserved distal pulses and a relatively normal external appearance.

Brian confirmed that this was the mechanism in the concealed plot. The result was more interesting than a jump from a nearly stated final cause to its next consequence: the move followed several rounds of genuine differential search and was conditioned by evidence already collected.

The later television reveal is not the Eureka hypothesis

In “Three Stories,” the third patient is ultimately revealed to be House himself. The fuller fictional causal explanation is a leg aneurysm with thrombosis, arterial obstruction, ischemia, muscle infarction, and necrosis. Sol had proposed the matching tissue-level pathological class, not the entire upstream vascular explanation. This difference is essential: matching evidence does not guarantee explanatory completeness.

Diagnoses as conjectures

The case illustrates scientific hypotheses as conjectural candidates under constraints: evidence accumulation, ordinary generation, recognition of stagnation, a deliberate mechanism-changing conjecture, and subsequent checking. The television narrative provided a concealed fictional answer, not independent proof of a general medical diagnostic capability. The working principle is methodological: do not depth-charge too early. The unsuccessful ordinary alternatives made the later creative step more constrained and interpretable.
