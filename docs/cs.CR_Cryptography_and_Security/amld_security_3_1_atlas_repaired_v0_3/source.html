\chapter*{Companion statement}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Companion statement}
\noindent\textbf{Series identity.} This manuscript is \emph{AMLD Security 3.1},\ a direct sequel to \emph{AMLD Security 3}.\par\medskip
\emph{AMLD Security 3} established eight defensively oriented application sketches for
verifier-gated target settlement \cite{security3}. Its account had four active settlement
roles $P,R,I,C$ and an explicit $\mathsf{UNKNOWN}$ outcome. The present sequel completes
the five-role vocabulary by introducing $D$ (Dependence), assigns to each settlement
role an entire specified FORGE-6 federation, and installs a distinct global
Player-0 $B^\star$. It does not replace the older propositions. It develops the
additional obligations that follow from this larger, nested architecture.

In this sequel ``five FORGE-6 federations'' has a literal interpretation:
$\F^P,\F^R,\F^D,\F^I,\F^C$ are five role-local FORGE-6 instances. Each has one internal
coordinator $B_0^r$ and five mathematical ATPs $B_1^r,\ldots,B_5^r$. Thus there
are $25$ mathematical search ATPs, five internal coordinators, and one global
Player-0 $B^\star$: $31$ distinguished components. This count describes an
architecture, not a claim that 31 executable processes were launched.

\chapter*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
A verifier-gated architecture can insulate security claims from speculative
search, but the addition of assumption dependence, nested federations, and an
adaptive controller creates new composition and scheduling problems. We define
$\mathsf{AMLD}^{+0}_{5\times\mathrm{FORGE6}}$ with settlement roles
$\{P,R,D,I,C\}$, each realized by a FORGE-6 federation, and Player-0 $B^\star$.
We distinguish semantic truth, checker acceptance, scoped dependence, and
resource-bounded nonsettlement; separate a frozen BANK baseline from a provisional
certified ledger; and formalize service authority, typed communication, and
cost accounting. The proved results include scoped deterministic report arbitration, nonvacuous guard atlases, multi-federation noncontamination,
context-safe transfer, finite guard-basis and guard-criticality theorems,
passive emulation, two-level round-robin bounds under committed replay,
conditional certificate-sharing gains, game-aware quotient preservation,
and generator-independent promotion. An ATLAS-style repair pass corrected guard consistency, outcome arbitration, service-macrostep horizons, latency units, and report revocation. Counterexamples rule out automatic
speedup, unsafe state quotienting, and universal safety claims from finite
regression tests. Applications to eight security and engineering domains and a
preregistered experimental program identify what remains open.

\chapter{Purpose, lineage, and claims}
\section{What changes and what does not}
The underlying problem remains the same: a powerful search process may propose
objects, but acceptance into trusted knowledge is a separate act. This sequel
makes that principle robust under five simultaneous settlement disciplines,
internal FORGE-6 cooperation, and external Player-0 control. We adopt the
established FORGE-6 interpretation of an internal $B_0$ coordinator and five
different mathematical searchers \cite{forge}, and the established
AMLD+0 convention $P_0=B^\star$ with role-local authority \cite{depths}.
The main claims are formal, conditional statements about this defined protocol.
They are not discoveries of weaknesses in real cryptographic schemes or a proof
of global AI safety.

\section{Truth, certificates, and three possible sources of confusion}
Three distinctions govern everything that follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A verifier accepting a byte string is not by itself a proof that the verifier,
its model, or its axioms are sound. Semantic conclusions below explicitly assume
soundness for the relevant certificate type and scope.
\item $D$ is an active settlement role; $\mathsf{UNKNOWN}$ is a report of
nonsettlement under the prescribed stopping conditions, not a search agent or
a certificate that the target is independent.
\item An assertion true under an augmented context is not thereby true in a
weaker, unscoped or differently labeled context.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions and audit posture}
The paper supplies a complete process decomposition, new guarded statements and
proofs, cost accounting, and a test design. Where a conclusion needs fairness,
faithful replay, decidable model checking, or a perfect lifting obligation,
those hypotheses are stated rather than silently presumed. No theorem herein
asserts unconditional settlement, polynomial cryptanalysis, alignment, or
practical security of a deployed implementation.

\chapter{The thirty-one-component architecture}
\section{Five settlement roles and six outcome categories}
\begin{definition}[Role and outcome alphabets]
Let
\[
\Rls=\{P,R,D,I,C\},\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O=\{&\mathsf{PROVED},\mathsf{REFUTED},\mathsf{DEPENDENCE},\\
&\mathsf{INDEPENDENCE},\mathsf{CONTRADICTION},\mathsf{UNKNOWN}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The five members of $\Rls$ are roles that generate candidate evidence. The sixth
member of $\mathcal O$ is a bounded no-certificate outcome. The settlement
report must identify the accepted certificate and its scope whenever it reports
one of the first five categories.
\end{definition}

$P$ seeks a proof of $T$; $R$ seeks a refutation of $T$; $D$ seeks certified
assumption-sensitive behavior; $I$ seeks metatheoretically certified
independence in a fixed formal setting; and $C$ seeks a certified inconsistency
of the declared assumptions. Roles may produce overlapping diagnostic evidence,
but acceptance is always typed and priority rules are declared before a run.
In particular, acceptance of a $D$ certificate is not an unconditional proof
of $T$ or $\neg T$.

\section{FORGE-6 as an exact role-local template}
\begin{definition}[A role-local FORGE-6 instance]
For $r\in\Rls$, set
\[
\F^r=(B_0^r,B_1^r,B_2^r,B_3^r,B_4^r,B_5^r,
\mathcal M^r,\sigma^r,\kappa^r,\mathcal L^r).
\]
Here $B_0^r$ is the internal coordinator, $B_1^r$ backward ATP,
$B_2^r$ forward ATP, $B_3^r$ adversarial ATP, $B_4^r$ proof-geometry/zipper
ATP, and $B_5^r$ counterfactual ATP. $\mathcal M^r$ is a typed message
bus, $\sigma^r$ an internal scheduler, $\kappa^r$ a cost ledger, and
$\mathcal L^r$ the role-local record of candidates and certified receipts.
All five mathematical ATPs may use different search tactics but none can
alter the target, context, verifier, or admission rule.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Role constraints without suppressing creative search}
The outer role dictates the \emph{type of certified result}, not a single
internal search style. For example, $B_3^P$ may search for weaknesses in a
proposed proof and $B_5^R$ may hypothesize a counterexample, but only the
correctly typed receipt makes an outer $P$ or $R$ settlement. Similarly,
$B_1^D$ searches backward for a minimal dependency, $B_2^D$ mines useful
conditional lemmas, $B_3^D$ challenges spurious assumptions, $B_4^D$
navigates assumption lattices, and $B_5^D$ explores conditional alternatives.
This is a design assignment, not a report that these processes have run.

\begin{proposition}[Typed cardinality]
If every $\F^r$ has one internal non-proving coordinator and five distinct
mathematical ATP components, and if $B^\star$ is distinct from all of them,
then the architecture has $5\cdot5=25$ mathematical ATPs, five internal
coordinators and one global Player-0, hence $31$ components.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}Five disjoint role tags each index five ATPs and one coordinator;
$B^\star$ is a separately tagged controller. Sum the disjoint sets.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Not thirty-one votes]
No count of components confers proof authority: certificates are accepted by
$V$, not by majority or agreement among any of the $31$ components.
\end{corollary}

\section{The outer board-player $B^\star$}
The symbol $B^\star$ names the \emph{active global Player-0}, not BANK,
not a stored board state, and not any $B_0^r$. At its legal turn $B^\star$
may choose $\Serve(r)$ for enabled $r\in\Rls$ or a permitted delegation
$\varepsilon$. The chosen federation and its internal $B_0^r$ then choose
legal internal work according to their policies. $B^\star$ may reason about
which permitted task to pursue, but does not choose an ATP's internal proof
step or assert a role certificate on its own authority.

An extended protocol may allow board/federation activation only upon
an explicitly authorized $B^\star$ turn and subject to credits, frozen
rules, and fully charged resources. Activation is \emph{not} automatic,
and the $31$-component core does not require extra spawned instances.

\section{Typed semantic bus and authority}
Legal packet types are $\mathsf{NEED}$, $\mathsf{HAVE}$,
$\mathsf{CANDIDATE}$, $\mathsf{MATCH}$, $\mathsf{STATUS}$,
$\mathsf{RESULT}$, and $\mathsf{WITHDRAW}$ (and versioned extensions
such as $\mathsf{FAIL}$). Every packet carries a role tag, originating
ATP, target, context, dependency digest, trust status, destination and
resource charge. Internal buses are initially OFF; $B^\star$ can
explicitly authorize a bus in the declared mode and can disable it.
Cross-federation packets pass a typed gateway and cannot self-promote.

\chapter{Formal states, receipts, and admissible transitions}
\section{Frozen instance and working evidence}
\begin{definition}[Frozen instance]
A run is parameterized by
\[
\mathcal I=(T,\Gamma_0,E_0,V,\mathfrak C, K_0,\mathcal A_0,
\mathbb B,\sigma_{\rm base},\rho,\Pi_0),
\]
where $T$ is the target; $\Gamma_0$ is the admitted theory; $E_0$ is
the environment; $V$ the fixed verifier (including certificate formats);
$\mathfrak C$ the allowed context, assumption and label family;
$K_0$ the frozen certified BANK; $\mathcal A_0$ the frozen transition
authority; $\mathbb B$ the global charged resource budget;
$\sigma_{\rm base}$ the baseline scheduler; $\rho$ the seed or
other replay-determining data; and $\Pi_0$ the immutable, scoped
settlement-reporting and revocation policy defined below. No component
may silently revise these.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Accepted tuple and provisional ledger]
A checked object is a tuple
\[
 z=(\text{type},c,\gamma,e,\lambda,\pi,V\text{-version},
 \mathrm{deps},\mathrm{origin},\mathrm{cost}) .
\]
The tuple's $\gamma$ identifies its exact assumption context, $e$ its
environment and $\lambda$ its authority or secrecy label. An object is
\emph{certified} when the fixed typed verifier accepts it and all
referenced dependencies are certified in compatible scopes. Let $L_t$
be an append-only \emph{provisional certified ledger}; its records do
not overwrite $K_0$. Unchecked material belongs to $U_t$.
The effective read-only verified view, where access is authorized, is
$K_0\cup L_t$, not an unlogged mutation of $K_0$.
\end{definition}

A deployment profile can authorize separately gated write-back to a
versioned persistent BANK, but is not assumed here. This distinction
allows a frozen experimental baseline and learning during a run without
silently changing its admitted premises.

\begin{definition}[Rule-complete global state]
A state consists of all thirty-one local control/search states, the
frozen instance, $L_t,U_t$, bus-enable bits, role permissions, pending
checker calls, provenance DAG, accepted typed outcomes, clocks,
resource accounts and scheduler/seed state sufficient to determine
legal successors. A \emph{legal move} is a transition allowed by
$\mathcal A_0$ that preserves the immutable instance and obeys its
verification and budget checks.
\end{definition}

\section{Frozen scoped arbitration and revocable reports}
\begin{definition}[Frozen report arbiter and scope]
A \emph{scope} $\gamma$ fixes theory, target, model class, environment,
versioned verifier, authority label and relevant dependency identifiers.
The frozen policy $\Pi_0$ includes (i) exact-scope matching and verified
transfer rules; (ii) certificate eligibility, including a separate
satisfiability receipt before display of a \emph{nonvacuous safety}
claim; (iii) a deterministic precedence rule; (iv) terminal and
stopping conditions; and (v) quarantine, supersession and revocation.
Let $\mathcal E_t(\gamma)$ be the time-stamped vector of admitted
$P,R,D,I,C$ receipts eligible for scope $\gamma$, \emph{not} a single
truth value. All receipts remain in the append-only evidence ledger.
For definiteness, this paper freezes the primary-display order
\[
 C\succ R\succ P\succ D\succ I,
\]
with accepted-event order followed by canonical receipt digest as
its deterministic tie-breaker. This order is an administrative display
convention, not a logical implication. The public record additionally
contains the full eligible vector and each receipt's exact scope.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Reporting, pending, unknown and quarantine]
Define $\operatorname{Report}_{\Pi_0}(\mathcal E_t,\gamma,t)$ by:
(a) a verified $C$ receipt at $\gamma$ or in a certified weaker
context whose theory extends into $\gamma$ marks the relevant scope
\textsf{QUARANTINED}, and revokes every previous \emph{safety display}
in that affected scope; (b) otherwise eligible receipts are shown as
a full vector with a primary display chosen by the frozen order;
(c) if none is eligible and either the stop rule has not fired or
mandatory checker calls remain pending, return \textsf{PENDING};
(d) if none is eligible, the stop rule has fired, and no mandatory
checker call remains pending, return \textsf{UNKNOWN}. Quarantine is a reporting status,
not a seventh settlement role. A revoked report remains in the
historical log tagged \textsf{REVOKED}, never silently erased. Scope
propagation requires a checked extension relation with the same
logic and environmental meaning; unrelated scopes remain untouched.
The protocol creates versioned replacement reports or revocation
notices, but cannot retract copies already exported to ungoverned
external systems.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Deterministic scoped report and revocation]
\label{thm:arbiter}
If the accepted ledger, immutable scope-compatibility data, event
sequence, stop status and $\Pi_0$ are identical, then the report vector,
primary display, quarantine and revocation flags are identical.
An accepted $C$ receipt cannot, by this rule, revoke reports in an
unrelated scope, but it invalidates all affected prior safety displays.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every predicate, precedence comparison and digest tie-break is a
fixed function of those inputs. Quarantine is computed from checked
scope extension; affected historical safety entries receive a
revocation tag. Unrelated entries fail that extension predicate.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Unknown is neither a role nor a timeout theorem]
\textsf{UNKNOWN} is reported only for the specified scope after its
stop rule fires, with no eligible certificate; it implies neither
independence nor falsity. Pending checks instead give
\textsf{PENDING}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{remark}[Inconsistency and certificate validity]
A $C$ receipt does not erase proofs from the immutable evidence
ledger. It may render them unsafe for \emph{nonvacuous safety reporting}.
A semantic soundness failure or changed checker is not repaired
merely by report precedence; such incidents require separate trust
reassessment.
\end{remark}

\section{What does a certificate establish?}
The verifier is a family of typed checks $V_{\tau}(\pi,c\mid\gamma,e)$.
For $P$, a receipt proves the target in the specified context.
Displaying it as nonvacuous security additionally requires the
declared model-satisfiability obligation. For $R$,
a proof of its negation or an admitted semantically sufficient
counterexample is checked. For $I$, the proof or pair of models must
establish the declared independence claim in the fixed metatheory;
failure of $P$ and $R$ searches is not evidence of $I$. For $C$, a
receipt certifies inconsistency of the specific context. For $D$, a
receipt certifies stated context sensitivity, defined precisely in
Chapter 5. The checker must not confuse these types. Raw $V$ checking
uses the full immutable input; ledger admission additionally needs
currently certified dependencies, an unexpired scope key and the
frozen $\Pi_0$ eligibility rules.

\begin{definition}[Soundness hypothesis]
A verifier is \emph{sound on an admitted scope family} if every accepted
receipt supports exactly its stated semantic or metatheoretic
conclusion in the declared model and scope. We do not assume that
acceptance by an arbitrary implementation implies this hypothesis.
\end{definition}

\section{The governing noncontamination theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Federated noncontamination]\label{thm:noncontam}
Suppose (i) $L_0$ is certified; (ii) the only ledger insertion operation
appends a $V$-accepted, scope-valid, support-closed tuple; and (iii)
all other moves, including all $31$ components' actions and packet
transmissions, leave $L$ unchanged. Then every $L_t$ contains only
certified tuples, and no member of $U_t$ enters $L_t$ merely by being
transmitted, repeated, scored highly or proposed by $B^\star$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on legal global transitions. The base case is assumption (i).
The only transition that enlarges the ledger satisfies (ii). Every other
transition preserves the inductive invariant by (iii). The provenance
of the proposal is irrelevant to the acceptance check.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Twenty-five-ATP immunity to voting]
Even unanimous agreement of the twenty-five mathematical ATPs cannot
promote an unsupported candidate in this protocol.\end{corollary}
\begin{corollary}[Conditional semantic soundness]
If, additionally, $V$ is sound on all accepted scopes and the meaning of
each accepted tuple is interpreted in its declared scope, every member
of $L_t$ has its certified typed meaning. This does \emph{not} imply
that the admitted environment models the deployed world.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Verifier-neutral controller replacement]\label{thm:neutral}
Suppose two runs differ only in $B^\star$'s scheduling policy, a
FORGE-6 internal search policy, or bus routing, while $V$, the
certificate language, frozen contexts and ledger insertion rule are
the same. For every \emph{identical candidate tuple} presented to $V$,
its raw verification result is identical in both runs if the complete
checker input, including referenced content hashes, is identical.

differ because dependency availability, budget and quarantine differ.
The runs may also differ in candidates and resource use.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}The typed verifier is a fixed function of the complete
frozen verification input, not its proposing policy. Ledger admission
may depend on an additional changing dependency-availability predicate.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Policy improvement is not truth improvement]
A changed schedule can improve certificate discovery rates but cannot
make an invalid certificate valid under the same frozen checker.
\end{corollary}

\chapter{Scoped evidence and the Dependence federation}
\section{Why dependence needs its own certificate}
A claim that changes with an assumption is not the same as a claim
independent of an axiomatic theory. The role $D$ investigates certified
relationships between \emph{different} admissible contexts; $I$
investigates non-derivability of a fixed statement and its negation
from a \emph{fixed} theory under the chosen metatheory. Neither role
may derive its conclusion from elapsed time or failed search alone.

\begin{definition}[Admissible nonvacuous guard family]
Fix a base theory $\Gamma_0$, a finite optional guard set
$G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n\}$, an unchanged object-language interpretation,
and a common declared model class. For $A\subseteq G$ define
$\Gamma_A=\Gamma_0\cup A$ and
\[
\mathcal C=\{A\subseteq G:\Sat(\Gamma_A)\},\quad
\mathcal S_T=\{A\in\mathcal C:\Gamma_A\models T\}.
\]
Only $A\in\mathcal C$ are \emph{admissible security guard contexts}.
The semantic predicate $f_T(A)$ is defined on $\mathcal C$ by
$f_T(A)=1$ iff $\Gamma_A\models T$; $f_T(A)=0$ means that a model
of $\Gamma_A\land\neg T$ exists. For $A\notin\mathcal C$ the
security predicate is \emph{inadmissible}, not true by explosion.
Certification of $A\in\mathcal C$ requires a sound, checked model
witness or a separately justified, checkable consistency certificate.
For arbitrary infinite theories such a finite checker may not exist;
no universal decision procedure is presumed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Strong guarded $D$-receipt]
A strong $D$-receipt for $T$ at guards $A_-\subsetneq A_+\subseteq G$
consists of: (i) a checker-accepted proof of $T$ from $\Gamma_{A_+}$;
(ii) a checker-accepted model of $\Gamma_{A_+}$ (to exclude
vacuous proof from inconsistency); (iii) a checker-accepted countermodel
to $T$ satisfying $\Gamma_{A_-}$; (iv) verified identity of target
meaning across the two contexts; and (v) the declared environment,
scope and guard provenance. It establishes $f_T(A_+)=1$ and
$f_T(A_-)=0$ in a nonvacuous stronger context.
It does not establish $\Gamma_0\models T$ or
$\Gamma_0\models\neg T$ unless separately proved.
\end{definition}

This is deliberately a \emph{strong} operational certificate schema.
A weaker \emph{conditional sufficiency record} consists of a checked
proof of $T$ at an admissible, satisfiable guard $A$ but has no
certified negative comparison; it is useful $D$-role evidence but is
\emph{not} yet a $\mathsf{DEPENDENCE}$ settlement. A
\emph{deletion-essentiality record} adds the checked countermodel at
$A\setminus\{g\}$ and is a strong $D$ receipt for that guard.
Consequently the strong $D$-to-semantic-$I$ bridge below need not apply
to every preliminary or weak $D$ record. No general implication
between other meanings of dependence is presumed.

\begin{theorem}[Sound guarded dependence]\label{thm:d}
If a strong $D$-receipt is accepted by a sound typed verifier and its
contexts satisfy the stated guard semantics, then entailment of $T$
differs between its certified contexts. Its promotion as a context-free
$P$ or $R$ settlement is unjustified unless a separate matching
receipt is accepted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}The proof component gives $f_T(A_+)=1$. The countermodel
component witnesses $f_T(A_-)=0$. Neither component proves the target
in any unnamed context, so a change of certificate type or scope
requires another inference and check.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Strong dependence is not independence]
A certified $D$-receipt does not, without a checked conversion into
the declared $I$-certificate language, constitute an accepted $I$
settlement, even when its models are informative about $\Gamma_0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Dual-model bridge from strong dependence]\label{thm:dualmodel}
Under the same-model-class interpretation of the guard contexts, a
strong $D$-receipt with its consistency model $M_+$ and deletion-side
countermodel $M_-$ gives two models of $\Gamma_0$, one satisfying
$T$ and the other satisfying $\neg T$. Therefore $\Gamma_0$
semantically entails neither $T$ nor $\neg T$. If the frozen $I$
verifier accepts this pair as a sufficient independence certificate,
it can also establish an $I$ settlement after a separate typed check.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both $\Gamma_{A_+}$ and $\Gamma_{A_-}$ extend $\Gamma_0$.
The model $M_+$ satisfies $T$ because the sound proof establishes
$\Gamma_{A_+}\models T$. The model $M_-$ satisfies $\neg T$ by
its verified counterexample. Both models satisfy $\Gamma_0$, so
neither proposed entailment from $\Gamma_0$ holds. The final
sentence follows only under the stated $I$-receipt rule and check.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Overlap of evidence is not collapse of roles]
This bridge is a new consequence of the nonvacuous strong guard
semantics chosen for $D$ here. It illustrates how different
settlement roles can share evidence, not that $D$ and $I$ have
identical objectives or that every dependence framework yields
independence.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Finite nonvacuous minimal-guard basis]\label{thm:antichain}
For finite $G$, the satisfiable family $\mathcal C$ is downward closed
in $2^G$, and the nonvacuous success family $\mathcal S_T$ is
upward closed \emph{relative to} $\mathcal C$. Its unique antichain
$\mathcal M_T$ of inclusion-minimal elements satisfies
\[
\mathcal S_T=\{A\in\mathcal C:\exists M\in\mathcal M_T\ (M\subseteq A)\}.
\]
No inconsistent $A$ enters this atlas, even when classical explosion
makes $\Gamma_A\models T$ true.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Deleting optional assumptions preserves satisfiability, so
$\mathcal C$ is downward closed. If $A\in\mathcal S_T$,
$A\subseteq B$ and $B\in\mathcal C$, then any model of $\Gamma_B$
models $\Gamma_A$, hence $\Gamma_B\models T$. For every successful
$A$, finite descent inside $\mathcal C$ gives a minimal successful
subset; the preceding relative monotonicity proves the reverse
inclusion. Minimal elements are unique and incomparable. Exclusion
of $A\notin\mathcal C$ is by definition.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Certified nonvacuous sufficient-condition atlas]
An entry $M$ can be promoted only with (i) a checked entailment receipt
for $T$ at $\Gamma_M$, (ii) a checked satisfiability/consistency receipt
for $\Gamma_M$, and (iii) checked negative entailment witnesses for all
proper relevant subcontexts, for example immediate deletions. A
\emph{complete} atlas additionally requires certified exhaustive
coverage of the finite guard lattice. Without negative and coverage
checks, a collection of successful entries is only a candidate atlas.
\end{corollary}

\begin{example}[Explosion rejected by the atlas]
Let $\Gamma_0=\{p\}$, $G=\{\neg p\}$ and $T=q$ in classical
propositional logic. The apparent ``success'' of $\{\neg p\}$
under unrestricted entailment is vacuous: $\Gamma_0\cup\{\neg p\}$
has no model. Therefore $\{\neg p\}\notin\mathcal C$ and it never
appears in $\mathcal M_T$.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Deletion-witness minimality]\label{thm:deletion}
Let $M\subseteq G$ have a certified proof of $T$ under $\Gamma_M$
and a checked satisfying model of $\Gamma_M$. If, for every $g\in M$,
there is a certified model of
$\Gamma_{M\setminus\{g\}}\land\neg T$, Then $M$ lies in $\mathcal M_T$, the nonvacuous
inclusion-minimal sufficient guard family. If $M\ne\varnothing$, then
for each $g\in M$ a strong $D$-receipt is furnished by the proof
for $M$ and the corresponding deletion countermodel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each immediate deletion fails to entail $T$. Any proper subset
$A\subsetneq M$ lies inside at least one $M\setminus\{g\}$; its
set of models includes the verified countermodel for that deletion,
which therefore refutes entailment at $A$. Thus no proper subset of
$M$ is sufficient. The satisfying model of $\Gamma_M$ rules out
vacuous success, and the receipt pairs satisfy the strong $D$ schema.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Criticality is explicit]
The $D$ federation can name a guard $g$ as critical to a minimal
certificate only with a checked deletion witness, not merely because
$B_5^D$ imagined the guard absent or $B_1^D$ requested it.\end{corollary}

\begin{example}[Two independent protections]
Let a toy authorization event be safe exactly when guards $g_1$
(``strong authentication'') or $g_2$ (``physical isolation'') is
present in a stipulated Boolean model. Then $f_T(A)=1$ iff
$g_1\in A$ or $g_2\in A$, and
$\Min_T=\{\{g_1\},\{g_2\}\}$. The empty guard set has a direct
countermodel; each singleton has a trivial Boolean proof. This is a
model-relative example of an antichain, not a real system audit.
\end{example}

\section{Preventing context laundering}
\begin{definition}[Scope-safe use]
A certified tuple established under $\gamma$ may be used in $\delta$
only if a checked transfer operation licenses the use
$\gamma\rightsquigarrow\delta$, including required theory,
label, environment, dependency and certificate-lifting obligations.
A $\mathsf{HAVE}$ packet with a proof from $\gamma$ is not such a
transfer by itself.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[No unlicensed cross-context promotion]\label{thm:scope}
Suppose every local inference either (a) uses premises with matching
scopes or (b) includes a verified scope-transfer receipt, and every
ledger insertion checks this property recursively through its support
DAG. Then no ledger proof of $T$ at scope $\delta$ can depend on a
foreign-scope premise without a verified chain of transfers from that
premise to $\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over the finite acyclic dependency graph for the inserted
certificate. Leaves are appropriately scoped axioms or transferred
premises by the insertion rule. For an internal inference, the rule
ensures that each parent is locally scoped or arrives by a verified
transfer. Concatenating parent chains gives the required chain for
every dependency.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Provenance-preserving federated reuse]
Shared ledgers may reduce repeated proof work without equating the
authorization labels, hypotheses, or environmental assumptions of
different security domains.\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Two-level cyclic service]

\begin{theorem}[Twenty-five-channel committed-replay bound]\label{thm:25}
Fix a pair $(r,j)$ with a checker-accepted target-typed certificate
after $m\ge1$ local transitions of a frozen baseline policy. Assume:
(i) two-level cyclic service executes one local transition in each
scheduled slot until settlement; (ii) the designated ATP implements
the successful committed baseline policy; (iii) other work leaves
its state projection, scope and verifier unchanged; and (iv) the
budget covers $25m$ dispatched transitions and all mandatory checks.

$N_{\rm dispatch}\le25m$, unless a qualifying certified outcome
terminates the run earlier. If a fully serviced dispatch, including
all mandatory intermediate administration but not the final check,
consumes elapsed time at most $d_{\max}$, idle waiting is included in
that bound, and the final checker latency is at most $\tau_V$, then
\[
 t_{\rm settlement}-t_{\rm start}\le25m\,d_{\max}+\tau_V.
\]

implies \emph{no finite wall-clock guarantee}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every block of 25 executed dispatches contains one transition for
$(r,j)$. With no earlier accepted termination, $m$ blocks supply
$m$ designated moves, which by committed replay generate the proof
candidate. This establishes the discrete dispatch bound. For the
elapsed-time claim add the worst-case time for at most $25m$ charged
dispatch intervals and the separately bounded final checker call.
\end{proof}

If one only round-robins the five inner ATPs and the outer controller

$5m$, under the same replay assumptions.\end{corollary}

\section{Exactly when sharing saves measured verification work}
\begin{definition}[Identical certified proof nodes]
A proof node $x$ includes its normalized statement, inference rule,
full context, verifier version, dependencies and label. A canonical cache key includes a byte-exact normalized statement,
proof/certificate bytes, transitive dependency digests, inference
rules, theory and environment digests, verifier version, authority
label and scope. A digest lookup is an index, not a license to trust
hash collisions: identity is checked by collision-resistant assumptions
plus byte-level equality or an equivalent authenticated representation.
Changing any field invalidates the corresponding key. Cached results
remain subject to scope checks, dependency replay, revocation and
cache-read verification charges; these costs belong in $H$ below.
Nodes with the same surface text but different scope or authority are
\emph{not} identical and cannot silently share certified status.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional DAG-reuse saving]\label{thm:reuse}
Suppose $k$ standalone investigations have certified proof-node DAGs
$D_1,\ldots,D_k$ and identical-node verification costs
$v(x)\ge0$. If checking every node afresh costs
\[
 C_{\rm sep}=\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{x\in D_i}v(x),
\]
and shared proof-DAG checking costs
\[
 C_{\rm shared}=\sum_{x\in\cup_i D_i}v(x)+H,
\]
where $H$ is all incremental routing, deduplication, scheduling,
key comparison, cache replay, invalidation, lookup and certification overhead, then
\[
 C_{\rm sep}-C_{\rm shared}
 =\sum_{x\in\cup_iD_i}(m_x-1)v(x)-H,
\]
where $m_x$ is the number of standalone DAGs containing $x$.
Consequently shared checking is cheaper \emph{if and only if} the
avoidable repeated-check cost exceeds $H$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Count the verification charge of each distinct node $m_x$ times
in $C_{\rm sep}$ and once in $C_{\rm shared}$; subtract $H$.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[No assumed federation speedup]
Twenty-five ATPs can increase overhead faster than they save checking.
Parallelism, certificate sharing and better guidance require separate
measured evidence to establish total-cost improvements.\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[No unconditional cost dominance]\label{thm:nomagic}
If the global architecture incurs any strictly positive unavoidable
startup cost $h>0$ relative to a standalone ATP, then there are target
families for which it cannot strictly beat the standalone ATP in
\emph{total charged work}, even while faithfully reproducing the
standalone proof.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a target whose complete verified certificate is already
available in the initial trusted context, so a standalone baseline
may return it with no proof-search work (or the minimum mandatory
reporting charge). Any extra unavoidable startup charge $h$ of
the new protocol cannot lower that minimum. Thus universal strict
cost dominance cannot follow merely from adding the architecture.\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[One-service macro-step and stalling]
A \emph{stable boundary} is a rule-complete state at which $B^\star$
may choose the next charged service. Write\[
 s\xRightarrow{a}s'
\]
if one legal $B^\star$ action $a$ charges exactly one service
and, after its prescribed zero-service bus, checking, ledger,
quarantine and arbitration microsteps, reaches the next stable
boundary $s'$. Intermediate accepted target status is recorded in
$s'$. A timeout, infinite administrative loop or checker divergence
is represented by a dedicated nonaccepting losing sink $\bot$,
which cannot be passed off as a successful boundary. Each enabled
action has at least one macro-successor including $\bot$ if
necessary. All nondeterministic macro outcomes are included.
A run that continuously performs such macrosteps receives no uncharged
external changes in between them. The ``one move'' bound below counts
exactly these macrosteps, \emph{not} checker microsteps or elapsed time.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Controller-respecting bisimulation]
Let $\mathcal G$ and $\bar{\mathcal G}$ be turn-based, nondeterministic
settlement transition systems, with target-accepting terminal sets
$W$ and $\bar W$. A relation $Z$ between states is a
\emph{controller-respecting bisimulation} if, whenever $sZ\bar s$:
(i) $s\in W$ iff $\bar s\in\bar W$;
(ii) the enabled $B^\star$ service-action labels agree (up to a
fixed verified renaming); and (iii) for each corresponding action
$a$, every \emph{macro-successor} of $s$ under $a$ is related
to a macro-successor of $\bar s$ under $a$, and conversely.
The relation contains any distinguished timeout/divergence sink and
matches nonaccepting sink labels. Raw checker/bus microsteps are
included in the macrostep closure, not counted as service actions.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon game-aware quotient preservation]\label{thm:quotient}
Let $Z$ be a controller-respecting bisimulation between states
$sZ\bar s$. For each finite horizon $n\ge0$, $B^\star$ has a
strategy forcing an accepted target state within $n$ service moves
against every admitted macro-successor choice from $s$ if and only if the
corresponding statement holds at $\bar s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define $W_0=W$ and recursively
\[
 W_{n+1}=W_n\cup\{s:\exists a\in A(s)\ \forall s'\in\mathrm{Succ}(s,a),
 \ s'\in W_n\},
\]
where $\mathrm{Succ}(s,a)=\{s' : s\xRightarrow{a}s'\}$
contains all completed service-macro outcomes, including failure sinks,
and is nonempty for enabled $a$; define $\bar W_n$ analogously.
For $n=0$ the claim is clause (i). Assume the claim for $n$.
Corresponding enabled actions and both directions of successor
matching in clause (iii), together with the induction hypothesis,
make the existential-action/universal-macro-successor clause invariant
under $Z$. Hence membership in $W_{n+1}$ agrees, and induction proves
the claim.\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Safe strategic state merging]
A quotient whose classes satisfy this stronger relation preserves
finite-horizon forced certified settlement. Mere equality of a
search heuristic, local proof count, or current terminal status does
not suffice.\end{corollary}

\begin{example}[An unsafe tempting quotient]
Two states are both unsettled and have identical current certified
BANK. From the first the controller can service $P$ to obtain an
accepted certificate in one move; from the second $P$ is permanently
disabled. Merging these states because their present ledger is equal
loses the one-step winning distinction.\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Scope and cost must survive quotienting]
If the target is a minimal-cost or probabilistic guarantee rather
than finite-horizon reachability, one needs additionally cost-preserving
transfers or appropriate kernel-equivalence and optimization
hypotheses. Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient} proves neither speedup nor
preservation of probability distributions.\end{remark}

\section{A scoped infrastructure theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Typed cut-and-service nonregression, model relative]
Let $G=(V,E_c,E_s)$ have separate directed compromise-reachability
edges $E_c$ and required-service edges $E_s$. A design installs
$E_c'\subseteq E_c$ and separately declares $E_s'$; no implication
between the two edge types is presumed. For each initial compromise
node $c$, $\Reach_{(V,E_c')}(c)\subseteq\Reach_{(V,E_c)}(c)$.
If a checker also verifies the required service paths in $(V,E_s')$
with any declared capacities and timing conditions, then the design
meets the conjunction of these \emph{separate modeled} containment
and service obligations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every directed compromise path using edges in $E_c'$ is also a
path in $E_c$. Independent certified service-path witnesses, and
any separately assumed capacity/timing obligations, establish the
other conjunct. No equivalence between compromise and service links
is used.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Dependence-guided segmentation]
A $D$ federation can seek minimal edge-cut guards whose deletion
witnesses establish which barriers are critical, while $B^\star$
coordinates separate containment and service certificates. Such
certificates do not validate the physical graph or all real-world
failure modes.\end{corollary}

\section{Contradiction, quarantine, and false security}
A classical inconsistent context proves every formula, so a
verifier checking only syntactic derivability does not by itself
supply useful security assurance. The $C$ federation has a special
role: it can raise a certified inconsistency flag for the precise
context in which its receipt is accepted.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped quarantine and historical report revocation]
Suppose the reporting policy $\Pi_0$ is enforced at every report
transition; all safety displays require an explicit nonvacuous
context-consistency receipt; and a checked $C$ receipt for $\gamma$
causes an atomic quarantine update covering $\gamma$ and every
certified extension scope. Then no \emph{new} safety display for an
affected scope is emitted after that update, and all previously
published \emph{protocol-governed} safety displays in affected scopes
are marked \textsf{REVOKED} in the versioned report log.
Unrelated scopes are not revoked by this receipt alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The frozen report rule suppresses new safety displays for all scopes
in the checked extension closure. Its atomic quarantine transition
marks precisely the existing affected displays revoked; the extension
predicate excludes unrelated scopes. The claim concerns the governed
log, not ungoverned external copies.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
This rule prevents reporting from a \emph{detected} inconsistent
context. It cannot guarantee detection of every inconsistency, and
finite inability to prove consistency is not proof of inconsistency.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite Ocean suites and universal claims}
\begin{proposition}[Finite-test nonuniversality]
Passing a finite set $O$ of sampled tests for a property over an
infinite domain does not logically imply universal validity without
an additional coverage or induction theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $O\subset\mathbb N$ be finite and choose $n\notin O$.
Define $p(k)$ to be true exactly when $k\ne n$. Then all tests in
$O$ pass but $\forall k\,p(k)$ is false.\end{proof}

\section{Research boundary and conclusion}
The theorems give a larger, carefully typed structure of conditional
guarantees, not an unrestricted safety result. The system remains
vulnerable to wrong specifications, incomplete environments, erroneous
checkers, compromised execution authorities, insufficient budgets,
unfair policies, unverifiable model claims, and mistaken scope
transfers. In particular, no amount of schedule creativity proves
$T$ where the fixed theory does not support it.

The conceptual advance from \emph{AMLD Security 3} is therefore a move
from isolated, verifier-gated application sketches to a specified
\emph{investigative institution}: five complementary FORGE-6
federations search for differently typed conclusions, $D$ accounts
for assumptions, and $B^\star$ chooses legal investigative direction
without deciding mathematical truth. What this institution can
\emph{discover} is an experimental question; what it may
\emph{certify} is an explicitly formal one.
