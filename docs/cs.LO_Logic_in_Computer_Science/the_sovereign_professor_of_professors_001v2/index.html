                                                    Abstract
         We develop the Sovereign Professor of Professors (SPP) as a joint limiting evaluator on three distinct
     frontiers: partial credit for structured objects with declared correctness judgments, partial credit for
     evaluators and their recursively defined reports, and assessment of possible changes to the architecture
     that produces and evaluates those objects. The construction unifies the recursive probabilistic Professor
     with costed sovereign repair geometry, while preserving the independence of proof verification. A
     countable, typed, weighted credit ledger gives a common Hilbert-space model of object and meta-
     level grading; nested authorized evidence yields an exact ignorance-reduction identity. A separate,
     monotone architectural opportunity coordinate admits a joint almost-sure and mean-square limit as both
     information and revision allowance increase. We distinguish opportunity from exercised improvement,
     accuracy from strategic utility, and a limiting estimator from a computable or general mathematical
     oracle. Counterexamples show why a tower of changing scoring targets, arbitrary evidence-dependent
     architecture, and greedy grade maximization cannot replace the hypotheses. We supply an exact finite
     example, a finite-budget decision model, an implementation contract and a settlement register. The
     main theorems are conditional mathematical results; the full sovereign interpretation and the attainment
     of general oraclehood remain open.

Central thesis. The SPP is not simply the last Professor in a vertical hierarchy. It is a common limiting
assessment of what is correct, how well correctness and progress are assessed, and what lawful changes
could improve the capacity to assess or discover it.

1    The three frontiers
The two parent manuscripts [1, 2] separate verified mathematical progress from estimated repairability
and distinguish improvement of a machine from improvement of the score used to describe it. The present
paper takes the Research Director’s proposed abstraction seriously: the domain need not consist solely of
theorem proofs. It can include any structured logical artifact for which a scoped standard of correctness
is meaningful. A Professor’s report is itself such an artifact when the grading contract specifies what
constitutes a good report. An architectural proposal is another such artifact when the claimed preservation
and improvement can be tested against a frozen contract.
The three frontiers are related but not interchangeable. The object frontier concerns candidate proofs,
countermodels, formal translations, derivations and related judgments. The evaluator frontier concerns
predictions, confidence, estimates of repair cost, and assessments of those reports. The architectural
frontier concerns changes to the search, evaluation or control machinery. A fourth operation, settlement,
lies outside the credit hierarchy: only a separately authorized checker can certify an object judgment or an
admission obligation. A large credit does not constitute settlement.

There are several meanings of “limit” in this proposal. A limit of estimates is an epistemic limit; a
supremum over legal options is an opportunity limit; a supremum of performance over accessible policies is
a capability limit. They may agree in a specially constructed model but need not agree in general. In
particular, an eventually constant sequence still has a limit; the term limit point is used in the sense of
a limit of a directed family, not a claim that every instance has a nonisolated accumulation point or a
unique geometric boundary.


2    Logical structured information and the correctness boundary
Definition 2.1 (Scoped logical object). An object is a tuple x = (τ, Γ, J, a) consisting of a type, a context,
a declared judgment and an artifact. The contract supplies a semantic predicate C(x) ∈ {0, 1} when defined
and a certificate checker V (x, c) ∈ {0, 1}. Soundness means V (x, c) = 1 ⇒ C(x) = 1. A checker for a
refutation certifies the corresponding negative judgment, not the complement of its own acceptance bit.

The domain may contain judgments of validity, derivability, equivalence, type correctness, or conformance
to a formal specification. For a prediction the contract may instead define a realized event and a proper
score; for a numerical approximation it may define a tolerance or a bounded loss. Undefined or ill-scoped

judgments are excluded or returned as undetermined. Rejection of a certificate is not proof of falsity, and
the semantic predicate need not be decidable. This distinction is crucial in incomplete formal systems.
For each eligible object i let Zi ∈ [0, 1] be a specified grade, not necessarily its correctness indicator. A
proof-repair grade can use the parent construction:

                         Zi = αi qi + (1 − αi ) exp(−Hi /hi ),    hi > 0,      0 ≤ αi ≤ 1,                    (1)

where qi ∈ [0, 1] is a normalized relevance-weighted measure of verified capital and Hi ∈ [0, +∞] is the
infimum of legal repair costs (not necessarily attained). Set e−∞ = 0. The weights, verifier, target, resource
accounting and reference population are frozen for an evaluation episode. A separate coordinate Ci can
encode semantic correctness if it is well defined and measurable. Another can encode existence of a checked
certificate by a budget deadline. Neither of these coordinates is in general equal to the repair grade.

Proposition 2.2 (No scalar settlement implication). Without additional restrictions on the grading
contract, Zi = 1 does not imply that the artifact has a certificate or that its declared judgment is correct.

Proof. Take αi = 1 and let the declared judgment be the false statement 0 = 1 in standard arithmetic,
with no accepting certificate under a sound checker. A normalized capital ledger that awards full credit for
a verified prerequisite (for example 0 = 0) gives qi = 1 and hence Zi = 1, even with Hi = +∞. Such a
ledger is permitted because no assumption links full capital to the truth of the target. Separately, when
αi = 0, a nonterminal state with a zero-cost legal path to a checked goal has Hi = 0 and grade one without
current acceptance.

Thus partial credit is a valuation on the boundary between incomplete evidence and certified resolution.
It can orient search without changing the truth condition. The distinction generalizes naturally to a
Professor’s report: an exact prediction of a score, for instance, need not be an exact proof of the original
statement.

its own limiting posterior; irreducible target error may persist. More generally, finite practical grading
accuracy must be demonstrated, not inferred from the existence of the ideal hierarchy.


5    Meta partial credit on architecture
Let s be a complete operational state, including the implementation, verified BANK, history, resource
ledger and permissions. Hold fixed the object judgment, checker, score calibration and comparison protocol.
An ordinary transition changes the state without a sovereign commit; a revision edge represents the
legal execution of an admitted change in the declared operational contract. The graph contains possible
executions, not a claim that those executions have occurred in the actual history. All edges have nonnegative
charged costs. Costs include proposal, checking, migration and execution. A proposed gift that is rejected
or undetermined is not a revision edge; its incurred costs remain chargeable.
For a fixed initial state and goal set G, define the costed horizon
                            Hk (s) = inf{cost(π) : s ⇝ G, r(π) ≤ k},         k ∈ N0 ,                          (6)
with empty infimum +∞ and r counting commits, not atoms. In finite graphs it can be computed
by shortest paths in the lifted state graph (s, k), with a gift decrementing k. This is the architectural
construction of [1]. The feasible sets grow with k, so Hk+1 ≤ Hk . For a common q ∈ [0, 1], α ∈ [0, 1] and
h > 0 independent of the allowance k, set
                                Yk = αq + (1 − α)e−Hk /h ,       Ak = Yk − Y0 .
Here Ak is the sovereign opportunity credit. It represents an advantage in the feasible envelope, not an
observed architectural improvement. It is nonnegative because retaining the ordinary path is permitted.
For stochastic worlds assume q and the Hk are measurable; a fixed countable vertex and edge universe
with measurable availability, costs, initial state and goal membership is one sufficient setting (take the
infimum over countably many finite paths). For adversarial games a specified strategy value, rather than
an existential shortest path, is needed.

Definition 5.1 (Three architectural credit records). A proposed intervention receives three separate
records: (i) admission evidence and unresolved obligations; (ii) predicted incremental utility against a
named baseline; (iii) realized or certified post-intervention change against the same baseline. The third is
absent until execution or an explicitly scoped certificate establishing the claimed comparison. A favorable
forecast is neither admission nor execution.

Proposition 5.2 (Robust comparison of intervention grades). Suppose independently justified intervals
satisfy q − ≤ q ≤ q + and L ≤ H ≤ U , with corresponding primed intervals after revision. Define
B − = αq − + (1 − α)e−U/h and B + = αq + + (1 − α)e−L/h . If B ′− > B + , the successor grade strictly exceeds
the parent grade on every permitted realization. If the sole constraints are closed grade intervals whose
Cartesian product is allowed, strict separation is necessary for such an interval-only guarantee. Additional
dependencies between the two grades can permit a stronger conclusion.

Proof. The exponential is decreasing in the horizon. The first conclusion follows by chaining the bounds.
For necessity select the smallest allowed successor grade and largest allowed parent grade.

This comparison requires bounds valid in the revised graph; a lower horizon bound can become invalid
when a shortcut is introduced. It also does not by itself certify the intervention’s cost-effectiveness. In
particular, compare total budgets including any migration overhead rather than comparing two machines
after giving one free setup.

7     What the boundary means, and what it cannot mean
7.1   Information, opportunity, and capability are separate
The risk In is monotone in information; opportunity Yk is monotone in allowance. It does not follow that
risk decreases with k since its target changes. For example, with no evidence and a fair Bernoulli variable
B, take Y0 = 0 and Y1 = B. Opportunity increases and risk rises from zero to 1/4. Conversely, a larger
allowance can make a grade constant and easier to predict. Nor is any realized Pn,k monotone in n.

7.2   Failure of the common-limit hypotheses
The common filtration assumption is substantive. If revision allowance changes what a Professor may
observe, a single Fn may not describe the experiment. A simple double array bn,k = 1{n≥k} has one iterated
limit equal to one and the other equal to zero. It cannot arise under Theorem 6.1, since it is decreasing
rather than increasing in k. It can describe a practical report that defaults to zero until disclosure. Even
exact conditional estimates can fail with allowance-dependent information: let B be a fair bit, set Yk = B
for every k, and let Fn,k be trivial for n < k and σ(B) otherwise. Each fixed-k filtration is nested, but
the posterior is 1/2 for n < k and B otherwise. The n-first iterated limit is B and the k-first limit is 1/2,
which disagree almost surely. New convergence claims require explicit compatibility of the information and
opportunity systems.

Likewise the topological or Pareto interpretation of the SPP must be declared. A natural attainable set is
the closure of tuples of risk, utility and cost under a fixed experiment. There may be many nondominated
points and no single point achieving the best value in every coordinate. For example two feasible policies
with (risk,utility,cost) equal to (0.1, 0.4, 1) and (0.2, 0.9, 1) are incomparable when low risk and high utility
are preferred. The canonical posterior limit is not automatically a Pareto-optimal implementable system.
It is a reference boundary against which such systems can be compared.


8     Actual evolution: the Professor as student and architect
The SPP is an evaluator; an operational SPP controller may also propose revisions. If the underlying
state changes with time, write Zt for the grade under the same frozen rubric of the evolving state, and
pt = E[Zt | Ft ]. Then

Theorem 8.1 (Moving-target drift). For uniformly bounded measurable Zt (in particular, 0 ≤ Zt ≤ 1)
and nested Ft ,
                             E[pt+1 | Ft ] − pt = E[Zt+1 − Zt | Ft ].
If the right side is nonnegative almost surely for every t, pt is a bounded submartingale and converges
almost surely and in L2 . A fixed target gives the usual martingale. Neither conclusion forces actual states
or architectures to stabilize.

Proof. Apply the tower property to pt+1 . Under the nonnegative drift condition use bounded submartingale
convergence and bounded convergence for the squared error.

For example a state can alternate between two distinct implementations with identical grades. If a system
can change the grading parameters instead of its capability, it can artificially increase credit: for fixed
positive finite H, e−H/h strictly increases with h. The assessment contract must therefore be separated
from the architecture being changed. A revision to the assessment contract is a new experiment with an
explicitly justified comparison map, not an in-place improvement claim.

Example 8.2 (Why perfect grading does not imply good strategy). Let α = 1/2, h = 1, and q(s) = 1.
An ordinary route reaches a familiar proof at cost four. A detour of cost one reaches p with q(p) = 0 and
no ordinary settlement path; the only goal-reaching routes from s are the two described here. An admitted
revision of cost one followed by an ordinary step of cost one reaches a novel proof. With one commit
allowed, the grade at s is (1 + e−3 )/2, but at p it is e−2 /2. A controller accepting only nondecreasing grades
rejects the first step of the cheaper, novel route. Both grades are exact. This instantiates the greedy barrier
in [1]; a finite-budget controller can instead compare terminal outcomes under the complete costed route.

The strategic Professor grades not only the last report but also the decision rule which uses it. A policy
report may itself be scored retrospectively against a predeclared regret or utility target and entered in
the same typed ledger. This is a precise sense in which meta partial credit can reach actual architecture
without confusing a prediction with its execution.


9    An exact finite experiment
Consider two independent fair bits X and B. The bit X is a hidden correctness judgment. The bit B
states whether a charged revision route is available; its availability is assumed to have been admitted in the
abstract graph when B = 1. The ordinary horizon is four in both worlds. One gift may reduce the total
charged horizon to two if B = 1, otherwise it remains four. Set α = 0, h = 1/ log 2. Therefore Y0 = 1/16
and Y1 = 1/16 + 3B/16.
Professor 0 has no evidence and reports r = 1/2 for X. Professor 1 receives a binary signal L through
a symmetric channel: P(L = x | X = x) = 3/4 for each x ∈ {0, 1}, with L independent of B conditional
on X. Professor 2 learns B. Professor 3 learns X exactly. Thus F0 is trivial, F1 = σ(L), F2 = σ(L, B)
and F3 = σ(L, B, X). We consider the realized signal L = 1 and availability B = 1; the last row uses the
further realized observation X = 1. Review costs are not modeled in this experiment.

10     Oracle limits and obstructions
10.1    Exact scoped grading is not universal oraclehood
Corollary 6.2 supplies a limiting evaluator. Exact recovery of the full vector of underlying targets X, Y∞ , A∞
occurs precisely when each Xj , Y∞ and Y0 has an F∞ -measurable version. Indeed A∞ = Y∞ − Y0 ; if
both the opportunity and its increment are recovered then so is Y0 . This criterion concerns a declared
population and target registry. It is not a claim of omniscience or a uniform algorithm for every theorem.
In the Research Director’s oracle program, oraclehood is related to attained minimization of a declared
ignorance measure. SPP minimizes a particular weighted prediction risk relative to its authorized evidence.
Whether that constitutes oraclehood depends on whether that risk and feasible comparison class coincide
with the oracle definition being used. An evaluator that has exhausted all information allowed to it may
still have irreducible risk. Conversely, possessing all grades in a narrow registry need not be sufficient to
solve arbitrary logical problems. Proposition 7.1 supplies one restricted bridge from evaluation to action,
not a general oracle theorem.

Proposition 10.1 (No effective general stabilization guarantee). There is a uniformly computable family
of bounded monotone finite-stage architectural grades whose limits cannot be uniformly approximated to
absolute error less than 1/8 by a total algorithm.

Proof. Given a machine/input index e, simulate it for k steps. Begin with a cost-two ordinary route;
when a halting trace is found, include an optional cost-one revision shortcut. Define yk (e) = 2−Hk (e) , with
Hk (e) equal to two before discovery and one after. This is a sequence of finite abstract graphs indexed by
discovery stage (not the single-graph gift budget of Theorem 6.1). The limit is 1/4 on nonhalting inputs
and 1/2 on halting inputs. An approximation with error strictly less than 1/8 separates them at 3/8,
deciding halting.

This adaptation of the computability obstruction in [2] also fits the abstract monotone-target theorem
with trivial information and discovery stage as its increasing index. It does not assert that a real sovereign
can admit a halting-encoded shortcut in an arbitrary formal system. Finite graph computation, existence
of a limit, computability of the limit and execution of an oracle are distinct questions.

10.2    Oracle opacity at the joint boundary
Let O be a binary oracle-status variable. If a learning gate maintains P(O = 1 | Fn ) = 1/2 at every finite
level, then Lévy’s upward theorem implies the same equality at F∞ . The minimum possible squared-error
risk among F∞ -measurable reports of O remains 1/4 (and the best binary classifier has error probability
1/2). The statement holds even if every object target in the declared ledger is recovered exactly, provided

11     A research program and implementation contract
Definition 11.1 (Finite SPP instance). An SPP instance is an auditable finite controller consisting of (i)
a frozen registry of judgment contracts and target IDs, (ii) an independently checked BANK and immutable
report archive, (iii) authorized evidence with provenance and cutoff times, (iv) object and meta-level
estimators with uncertainty records, (v) a costed candidate-revision planner, and (vi) a supervisor-gated
revision interface. The interface admits no unresolved or inconsistent gift, never assumes that high predicted
credit is an admission certificate, and records the actual exercise of any admitted change. The instance
reports its finite depth, resource usage and unresolved obligations.

The state machine must preserve unique sovereign office and the historical commitments of the applicable
GBG+S specification. It must distinguish proposals, authorization, verified admission and execution. No
specific meaning of an unsettled gift atom is assumed. The abstract theorems do not establish consistency
of that manual. Verification remains external to the claims being verified; evaluator rank cannot grant
verification authority. The architecture may be evaluated under a non-sovereign abstract contract even
when no GBG+S realization has been established.
A first implementation can be entirely offline: finite graphs, exact rational Bayesian tables and a deter-
ministic event log. Freeze test targets, training exposure, verifier, cost units, revision budget and scoring
registry. Compare object-only, object-plus-meta, architecture-only and joint controllers under equal total
resource budgets. Record verified settlement frequency, cost, calibration error, weighted risk, creative yield,
false admission attempts and the difference between forecast and realized architectural utility. Use separate
held-out tasks to guard against fitting the scoring system. Ablate redundant versus genuinely informative
reviewers and include a protected unchanged baseline. Report resource costs for computing grades and for
verifying architectural changes. This is an experimental specification, not a completed deployment.

Conjecture 11.2 (SPP capability bridge). The proposed research conjecture is that there exist nontrivial
oracle-relevant ATP workload families, increasing finite legal policy classes and a resource schedule for
which an effective, independently verified SPP protocol simultaneously achieves (i) vanishing decision-
relevant estimation error on the active action sets (for example the unweighted squared-error condition of
Theorem 7.2), (ii) vanishing optimization error, and (iii) convergence of the best accessible policy values to
a separately declared oracle capability value, all without changing the evaluation contract. The intended
workload family, oracle benchmark and resource schedule must be specified independently of the resulting
scores before this conjectural existence claim can be tested. As stated this is a research schema, not a fully
specified decision problem.

The requirements in the conjecture distinguish coverage, calibration, optimization and admissibility. In a
fixed finite action class the regret proposition gives the risk-to-decision implication. For increasing classes,
one additionally needs a rate controlling errors on the active set, approximation of the capability supremum,
and a legal schedule of charged operations. The existence of such schedules for the intended ATP family is
the open research problem, not a consequence of martingale convergence. A useful negative result would
exhibit an oracle-relevant class in which any effective SPP controller fails one of these requirements.

The Sovereign Professor of Professors is best understood as a structured limit rather than a single super-
grader. It preserves the question asked of logical objects, retains questions about the accuracy of those who
evaluate them, and adds questions about how the underlying machinery may be lawfully improved. The
central convergence theorem allows the three frontiers to meet without assuming that they are identical.

mathematical challenge is to determine when convergence of the right partial-credit coordinates yields
convergence of the capabilities that actually matter.
