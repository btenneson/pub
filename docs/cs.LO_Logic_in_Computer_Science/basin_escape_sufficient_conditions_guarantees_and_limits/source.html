                                                   Abstract
          A basin is a region in which a specified policy makes little certified progress, not necessarily
      an inaccessible part of the proof space. For fixed target, verifier, legal transition rules, and cost
      budget, we distinguish leaving a basin from settling a goal and characterize both by admissible
      finite witnesses; supply stronger, constructive sufficient conditions based on ranking functions,
      certified lemmas, fair proof enumeration, bounded self-settlement executions, and adaptive search;
      and separate route existence from execution and speedup. We also state game-theoretic sufficient
      conditions for forced wins, conditional guarantees for shared BANKs and simulation preorders,
      and counterexamples to common overclaims.


1 Frozen framework: three different claims

Fix a theory T , target c, sound certificate checker V, initial executable state s0 , a legal transition
system A, charged budget b, and a protocol defining accepted success receipts. An execution state
includes the search frontier, currently certified lemmas and their undischarged hypotheses, scheduler,
activated agents, provenance, and remaining budget. In particular, the target and verifier do not
change when search policy changes. Write s →A t for a legal transition, cost(s, t) ≥ 0 for its fully
charged cost, and G for states containing a checker-accepted receipt for T ⊢ c (or, if explicitly
authorized, T ⊢ ¬c in an object-level settlement task).

Definition 1.1 (Operational basin and its boundary). For a policy π, progress functional p and
diagnostic window h, a set B ⊆ S \ G containing s0 is a diagnosed search basin if a prespecified
observation over the declared number of steps or charged work detects negligible certified progress
under π. The diagnosis does not assert closure of B under every action of A. A basin exit is the first
transition from B to S \ B; settlement is a reached state in G. A certified settlement from s0 ∈ B
necessarily entails a basin exit, but an exit need not settle the goal.

Definition 1.2 (Possibility, guarantee, and cost). Relative to (s0 , B, A, G, b): (i) possible basin exit
means some finite admissible execution reaches S \ B by charge b; (ii) possible settlement means some
such execution reaches G; (iii) a guarantee requires an identified policy that realizes the event on every
permitted execution (or with a clearly stated probability); and (iv) cost improvement requires a fully
charged matched comparator. Mere earlier wall-clock completion does not establish lower total work.

Theorem 1.4 (Finite-descent sufficient condition for guaranteed escape). Suppose an authorized
deterministic policy π has a rank r : Sπ → N on all its reachable states, with r(s) = 0 implying s ∈ G,
and that the policy halts successfully on reaching G. Suppose whenever s ∈/ G (hence r(s) > 0), π takes
a legal transition s → t with r(t) < r(s) and charge at most C (including all obligatory verification,
activation, and transfer overhead). Then π reaches G in at most r(s0 ) transitions and charge r(s0 )C.
In particular, b ≥ r(s0 )C suffices.
Proof. The nonnegative integer rank decreases strictly at each nonglobal step, hence there are at
most r(s0 ) such steps. After at most r(s0 ) transitions, the policy must reach G: otherwise another
strict decrease would be required below zero. A rank-zero state is in G by assumption. Summing the
stepwise cost bound proves the charge bound.

Remark 1.5. The rank need not be computable or available in practice; discovering a sound rank and
implementing a policy that decreases it is substantive. An empirical “progress score” that sometimes
rises cannot serve as this proof without an amended argument.

2 Method A: Player-0 self-settlement (“White wins”)

The supplied note defines the first-person claim as the actual bounded event
          Winb (M, T, c, V, ξ) = 1 ⇐⇒ M (ξ) emits a complete accepted proof of T ⊢ c by b.
The designated speaker M and frozen input ξ matter. An activated subsidiary’s achievement alone
does not certify a prediction about a different speaker. The sentence “I will prove the theorem” is a
forecast of an operational event, not a new inference rule and not evidence that the event occurs.
Proposition 2.1 (Sufficient condition for successful A). Assume (A1) Player 0 is legally authorized
to activate a specified executable M as part of the frozen protocol; (A2) the activation and handoff
preserve T, c, V, actor identity, and dependency/provenance information; (A3) the activated search,
together with its authorized caller where applicable, is guaranteed to execute a finite, legally checked
proof-producing trace; and (A4) the entire trace, including activation and checking, fits budget b, and
the designated M emits the prescribed receipt. Then method A succeeds.
Proof. Conditions (A1)–(A4) construct an admissible bounded execution satisfying the event’s defining
receipt condition.

Remark 2.2 (Noncircular strengthening). One may discharge (A3) using an independently established
rank as in Theorem 1.4, or a finite verified execution schedule, rather than assuming success directly.
A proof about the future event does not replace the prescribed actual execution. For fixed deterministic
finite-time execution with a decidable checker, the resulting bounded event is in principle decidable by
faithful execution; the answer still requires execution or an independently sound operational certificate.

3 Method B: Eureka bridge lemmas

Theorem 3.1 (Certified-bridge composition). Let H encode the actual frozen admissible hypotheses.
Suppose there are finite proof objects π1 and π2 accepted respectively for

                                        H⊢ℓ         and      H, ℓ ⊢ c,

and the proof calculus permits a verified composition (cut/substitution) of these derivations without
adding or losing undischarged hypotheses. Then an accepted finite derivation of H ⊢ c exists. To settle
the frozen objective T ⊢ c, require additionally that every member of H used by the proof is authorized
by T (or established from T ), or that all otherwise open assumptions have been validly discharged in
the final certificate. Under this requirement, if a specified policy can construct/import both proofs,
verify their composition, and emit a T ⊢ c receipt within b, settlement by B is guaranteed.

Proof. Substitute π1 for any undischarged use of ℓ in π2 , or invoke the calculus’s checked cut rule.
Dependency tracking ensures every surviving assumption belongs to H. The additional discharge or
admissibility condition ensures that the final verifier checks the frozen judgement T ⊢ c, not merely a
stronger-hypothesis judgement. The operational statement follows if the declared policy executes this
finite construction and fits the budget.

What makes a bridge useful? The logical relation H ⊢ ℓ adds no new axioms. The bridge can still
change the search graph, make a difficult step locally accessible, or permit reuse. A useful nontrivial
bridge should be cheaper to discover and exploit than the original search obstacle, actually accessible
to the receiving prover, and neither merely the target renamed nor an illicit assumption. This is a
design criterion, not a theorem that every valid bridge helps.

Proposition 3.2 (Explicit cost criterion for improved Eureka escape). Let C0 be the full cost of a
matched baseline success. If B yields a verified target proof at charged total cost

           CB = Csetup + Cfind ℓ + Cverify ℓ + Chandoff + Cfinish|ℓ + Cverify c + Ccoord + Cretries ,

with all displayed categories aggregating all nonoverlapping mandatory costs, then CB < C0 is sufficient
for strict improvement in total charged cost on that instance. This inequality is a comparison criterion,
not evidence that it holds for any actual run.

Counterexample 3.3 (A valid but unhelpful lemma). Let H = {p, p → q}, c = q and ℓ = p. The
bridge is valid and permits q in one modus-ponens step, but if the baseline already uses that step,
rediscovering and sharing p adds cost rather than reducing it.

Remark 3.4 (Proof sharing can be exponentially significant). Proof-complexity literature exhibits
exponential separations between tree-like and DAG-like proof systems for some families [4]. Shared
certified intermediate derivations provide a concrete mechanism for avoiding repeated subproof con-
struction. This supports possibility on some families, not a universal exponential-to-linear conversion
or a performance claim for the proposed federation.

Theorem 4.1 (Fair enumerative discovery). Assume that the frozen static task admits an effective
enumeration of finite proposed proof traces, including every valid finite successful trace; candidate
verification terminates; and competing traces can be evaluated in isolated reproducible states (or as
self-contained proof certificates) without altering the frozen task. A dovetailed exhaustive procedure
that grants each candidate finite prefix unbounded cumulative work eventually detects any existing
successful finite trace. If a valid a priori upper bound on all required enumeration, reconstruction, and
checking charges is b, settlement is guaranteed within b.

Proof. A successful finite trace has a finite description and thus appears at a finite index of the
enumeration. Each of its finitely many required checks receives sufficient work under dovetailing and
terminates, so the receipt is eventually identified. Isolation/reproducibility makes parallel candidate
testing legal rather than illicitly combining mutually incompatible state histories. The charged upper
bound yields the bounded claim.

Remark 4.2. Effective completeness gives no uniform finite deadline. It does not decide all non-
theorems or prove independence by a failed search, and it does not apply unchanged to moving-adversary
games in which one favorable trace is insufficient.

5 Combined A+B+C: sufficient conditions

Theorem 5.1 (Federated certified-escape theorem). Suppose (F1) a frozen target c, a theory T , an
independent sound checker V, and a recorded relation between H and T that allows every final open
assumption to be checked or discharged as a proof of T ⊢ c; (F2) permitted Player-0 activation and
a well-defined designated speaker; (F3) a finite chain of lemmas ℓ1 , . . . , ℓk with checked derivations
whose open assumptions are discharged solely by H or earlier eligible lemmas; (F4) a checked terminal
derivation of c from H and this chain; (F5) a specified scheduler that either satisfies Theorem 1.4 for
successful goal states or exhaustively explores a reproducible finite search tree satisfying Theorem 4.3,
whose candidate space includes that chain and terminal proof; (F6) every BANK handoff preserves
proof dependencies and target identity; and (F7) the appropriate rank or tree bound, including all
necessary search, checking, activation, communication, and receipt costs, is at most b. Then the
designated federation settles c by b. It also satisfies the first-person prediction precisely when the frozen
protocol designates that federation as speaker (or legally assigns its output to the specified speaker)
and that speaker emits the required receipt.

Proof. Induct over the bridge chain. The first lemma is certified under H; if the first i − 1 lemmas
are available with proper dependencies, the ith verified derivation is safely composable. Compose the
terminal proof, discharging every assumption not permitted by the frozen judgement T ⊢ c as required
by (F1). The certified rank policy or finite-tree exhaustive scheduler in (F5), together with (F7),
ensures that this legal certificate is actually reached and emitted within budget. The protocol-bound
actor and receipt requirement gives the last clause.

Corollary 5.2 (Optional extensions preserve possibility). If an extended federation retains every
legal old action path unchanged at the same charge, without mandatory overhead, then adding A, B,
and C cannot remove any formerly possible escape. This does not assert that a new scheduler will
choose an old winning path or that costs improve.

6 Opponents, static proofs, and adaptive targets

6.1 Chess and other adversarial games
A verified favorable chess line covers only one possible Black response sequence; a forced win requires
∃σW ∀σB . For a fixed finite-horizon turn-based game, let W0 be declared White-winning terminal
states and recursively define

      Wk+1 = Wk ∪ {s (White turn) : ∃(s → t), t ∈ Wk } ∪ {s (Black turn) : ∀(s → t), t ∈ Wk }.

The displayed recurrence applies only to nonterminal positions with at least one legal move; terminal/no-
move conventions must be specified separately. If the initial rule-complete state lies in Wk and the

6.2 Static proofs and password tasks
In theorem proving, the adversary does not move the frozen T or c; counterexample/refutation
branches test the same problem. For a fixed finite password candidate set of size N known to contain
the fixed secret, if equality tests are sound, every candidate may legally be tested, the target remains
fixed, and the total charged cost of up to N tests fits the budget, exhaustive search guarantees success.
If the secret or acceptance rule may adapt without restrictions, that guarantee disappears; a bound
on adaptation or a winning-strategy argument is then needed.

7 Six analytic audits (not six executed solvers)

These are six analytical perspectives designated B0 –B5 for this report, not claimed outputs of six
actual FORGE-6 ATP executions and not a modification of the historical AMLD+0 roles.
 B0 Legality and actor identity: Are activation, schedules, recursion, delegation, and
    speaker/receipt identity actually authorized? Without them A may be only a sentence.
 B1 Proof validity: Does a complete accepted T ⊢ c proof path exist, with all extra assumptions
    discharged, and will the selected policy execute it rather than merely predict it?
 B2 Bridge construction: Are intermediate lemmas nontrivial, certifiable, composable, and gen-
    uinely accessible to the receiving prover?
 B3 Adaptive completeness: Does IAE allocate recurring opportunities to productive alternatives,
    or can premature abandonment cause permanent stagnation?
 B4 Obstructions and adversaries: Is a purported exit blocked by a legal-action invariant,
    exhausted budget, an independent target, or unmodelled adversarial responses?
 B5 Dependencies and full cost: Does every shared entry retain all undischarged assumptions,
    and are verification, transport, retries, and coordination included in reported improvements?
These six perspectives do not certify logical independence, imply a universal ATP, or establish
experimental execution.

8 Boundary conditions and impossibility witnesses

Proposition 8.1 (Closed-obstruction condition). If B ∩ G = ∅ and all legally reachable successors
of every s ∈ B lie in B, then no protocol restricted to those legal transitions can escape B. Likewise,
no path can satisfy a cost budget smaller than the infimum charge of legal successful paths (where a
path exists).

Proof. Closure inductively keeps every finite execution in B, disjoint from G. The cost claim follows
from the definition of an infimum.

Counterexample 8.2 (Possibility without discovery). A state s has a zero-progress self-loop and
one legal exit to a state with an immediate accepted proof. A deterministic policy that always chooses
the self-loop never escapes, although a two-step successful path exists. Merely adding an exit or telling
the policy “you will win” proves no operational success.

Counterexample 8.3 (A stronger-hypothesis proof is not target settlement). Take T = ∅ and
H = {c}, where c is not a logical validity. Then H ⊢ c has an immediate derivation, but T ⊬ c. A
Eureka bridge built from unlicensed H does not settle the frozen T -goal.

Counterexample 8.4 (Sharing without a sound BANK). An agent certifies T ∪ {a} ⊢ ℓ and shares
ℓ as if T ⊢ ℓ. A receiving prover may then appear to prove c from T using an unproved assumption.
This is not a legal escape even if a permissive logger accepts the text.

Remark 8.5 (Limit of global guarantees). For general effectively presented first-order theories,
semidecision of provability by proof enumeration is possible under standard enumerability assumptions;
universal decision of both theoremhood and non-theoremhood is not thereby obtained. Unbounded
search cannot be advertised as a uniform deadline or a proof of independence on failure. The source
note’s SIC upward closure holds only for certificate-preserving legal simulations under the applicable
cost transformation; well-foundedness is additionally needed for minimal-antichain generation [1].

9 Evidence, implications, and a concise sufficient-condition checklist

Peer-reviewed proof-complexity results show rigorous tree/DAG separations [4]; research on restarts
establishes that controlled randomization can improve difficult search distributions on some families
but does not make every hard problem easy [5]; distributed SAT research shows that independently
verifiable proof reconstruction from shared work is implementable [2], with newer parallel proof
representations demonstrating further scalability [3]. These are external analogues, not measurements
of AMLD+0, Eureka, IAE, or FORGE-6.
Seven sufficient-condition questions for any proposed escape:
S1. Is the frozen theorem, hypothesis set, designated speaker, checker, and accepted goal explicit?
S2. Is there a finite legal path (or winning game strategy) to that goal?
S3. Are intermediate products proved, independently verified, and free of unauthorized open assump-
    tions?
S4. Does the chosen mechanism actually provide an authorized transition out of the diagnosed basin?
S5. Will a specified policy execute/discover the complete route (via rank, completeness/fairness, or a
    quantified success probability)?
S6. Does the complete route fit the budget after all charges, including checking and delegation?
S7. Does the correct actor emit the exact accepted certificate/receipt, and is any speedup assessed
    only relative to a fully charged baseline?
Final judgment. The strongest defensible claim is a conditional possibility and guarantee theorem:
verified bridges, legal activation, certified composition, a specifically terminating rank policy or
complete bounded-tree schedule, and sufficient fully charged resources suffice for success. The source
note does not establish that every basin has such a bridge, that any particular proposed agent can
find one, or that a federation is universally faster. The next genuinely new theorem would identify a
nontrivial task family together with a constructive rank/fairness argument and an audited asymptotic
(or empirical) cost bound.
