                                                   Abstract

        A federation can know more about itself without becoming a better theorem prover. We make
    the missing implication precise in a finite, verifier-gated architecture-selection problem. Certified
    receipts induce partitions of hidden operational states; exact and interval-valued decision costs give
    paper certificates for the value of refinement. A finite decision tree characterizes optimal paid receipt
    acquisition. For every coalition size n ≥ 2, a parity-composition witness has zero gross decision value
    for every proper coalition, yet a strictly positive fully charged advantage for the whole coalition
    under an exact threshold. The construction separates mathematical settlement from meta-evidence
    and rules out greedy single-receipt acquisition as a general optimal policy. A second extension of the
    countably fair dyadic clock shows how an independently admitted, identity-preserving clock gift can
    promote an identified worker while retaining recurrent service for every worker. Its exact deadline
    and cost formulas give a restricted awareness-dependent sovereign separation. These are conditional
    mathematical results and finite model witnesses, not an assertion of universal gift admissibility,
    empirical ATP acceleration, or an independently executing federation.

A hard budget. The worst-case recursion gives an exact feasibility test for a fixed available budget

can instead stop recursion once the cap is reached. The unrestricted recursion alone does not compute
the value of an arbitrary stateful gate. Unlike minimizing average loss, this test prohibits a single
branch from overrunning the budget. A more general expected objective under a hard cap requires a
remaining-budget coordinate and infeasible branches excluded; no such cap is silently assumed in F .

4     A quantitative Gestalt theorem

A’s three-source witness establishes that a proposition can require a whole coalition for its derivation.
Here the whole coalition is indispensable to attaining the better decision optimum, even though every
member’s observation is individually true and checkable by the stipulated meta-checker. The result
concerns the full parity value, not the inability of a proper coalition to derive any information at all.


4.1   An explicit finite operational model

Fix n ≥ 2. Let Ω = {0, 1}n with the uniform distribution. Agent i holds a local, frozen orientation bit
xi . These bits describe the encodings of a chain of binary handoff interfaces: the ith interface applies
y 7→ y ⊕ xi . The composition is y 7→ y ⊕ p(x), where p(x) = x1 ⊕ · · · ⊕ xn . A proposed fast route chooses
a compensating orientation b ∈ {0, 1}; its interface is compatible exactly if b = p(x). Each receipt is a
tagged assertion of the current orientation at its own interface. In the finite model the meta-checker
compares the assertion to the exact frozen local state, checks source, epoch and authorization, and
accepts a composition claim only with the complete list of admitted premises and a checked XOR
calculation. For a common typed Boolean meta-theory of the orientation variables, every proper set of
bit receipts has two models with opposite parity; the complete set determines the parity. This supplies
a minimal-coalition dependence witness for the operational proposition p(x) = b relative to the frozen
initial views. Derivability alone is not a receipt; a federation-level claim still requires its own accepted
scoped witness and access. The fixed receipt costs and gift cost include any composition check and
delivery needed for the declared model (or an explicit zero incremental charge if included in another
cost category). This is a sound table/replay model, not a deployed authentication system.
Take a fixed object theory H = {s, s → t} with a sound checker of modus ponens. The target is t in all
worlds. There is a slow authorized proof-delivery route a∗ , costing h > 0 including checking. Fast-route
proposal ab costs l on a match, where 0 ≤ l < h; the gate checks compatibility before transfer and
the final object certificate is the same checked derivation of t. On mismatch the proposed transfer
is rejected, the attempt is charged w − h ≥ 0, and the unchanged slow route executes, total w. The
contract permits at most one fast-route attempt, no probing, free orientation test, direct unauthorized
certificate write, or other legal route. The costs are stipulated full charges of this finite model, not time
measurements. Role-local observations are held behind the authorization interface; a controller cannot
read arbitrary local state merely because it schedules that role. The fallback is mandatory on mismatch.
All cost information and the distribution are public; only x is hidden.

5    Awareness-dependent fair clock promotion

The first witness concerned the cost of choosing a compatible policy. We now extend the countably fair
clock from A, Theorem 5.3, and the clock-rewrite witness from SCDF 014v2. The goal is to change
access to scarce service slots without erasing any worker’s infinite-horizon service guarantee.
Definition 5.1 (Interleaved dyadic service contract). Let S be one sovereign office and let ai , i ∈ N =
{0, 1, . . .}, be an effectively indexed set of worker identities. Each nonnegative even tick belongs to S,
and at odd tick 2s + 1 the baseline serves av2 (s+1) . A finite permutation π of the worker indices (identity
off finite support) may be installed only at the initial tick 0 by a B14-style clock gift, with a positive
parent-bound external authorization and preservation witness. The resulting schedule is

                                   χπ (2s) = S,         χπ (2s + 1) = aπ(v2 (s+1)) .

The original schedule is used at the commit boundary. No worker is created or destroyed, no credit is
minted, and the unique sovereign and checker are unchanged. A known total effective step selector,
including permitted charged no-ops, is required; the schedule alone does not imply discovery. An infinite
run is a mathematical service model, not a finite-budget deployment. For a finite trial only its finite prefix
is executed; an external contract may use effective on-demand worker state, rather than preinstantiating
infinitely many processes.

Corollary 5.3 (The hidden-worker separation). Consider the same uniform n-bit states and authenti-
cated receipt interface of Section 4, now with the designated worker j = 1 + p(x). The only permitted
initial rewrite is one of the two transpositions (0 1) or (0 2); declining is also allowed. Both transpositions
satisfy the same structural admission contract for every hidden state, and admission, receipt access,
budget and preparation supply no further information about j. In particular the gate does not test
whether the proposed transposition promotes the target-producing worker. The designated worker’s proof
progress and service telemetry are not available until after the irrevocable initial choice; no later rewrite
is legal. The precommit receipts describe the frozen workload/worker assignment, which is invariant
under the permutation, and are valid for that parent. They need not be reinterpreted as claims about the
postcommit clock. The ordinary proof interface is frozen, and the target requires exactly m ≥ 2 services
from the designated worker. At deadline d = 2(2m − 1), a controller with all receipts and an admitted
matching gift guarantees settlement on every state. A controller with only a proper subset along every
observation path cannot guarantee settlement, even if granted the same two rewrite choices. With a
uniform hidden state any such possibly randomized controller succeeds with probability at most 1/2 by
that deadline. This upper bound is attained by choosing either fixed transposition without queries, when
its admitted cost fits the budget. The comparator cannot use an authorization failure, a trial rewrite, a
target-specific hint or a second clock decision as a free parity query. The no-rewrite baseline fails on
every state.

This deadline is counted from the clock epoch, not from the beginning of receipt acquisition. The
comparison includes identical service deadlines and a sufficient common charged budget for both arms,
including all receipt and gift expenses. To compare total elapsed physical latency one needs a separate
duration model, just as B requires. For m = 2, the baseline completion counts are 12 or 24, and a
matching promotion completes in six service ticks. Neither the awareness result nor the fair-clock result
alone implies the combined separation.


6    Evidence gates and limits of the extension

Proposition 6.1 (A typed preservation and imitation boundary). Assume the same sound object
checker V , immutable object hypotheses, initially sound admitted object records, and finite well-founded
scoped proof dependencies for every state and both arms. All ordinary transitions that can modify
the object store obey the object-commit gate; no alternate writer is permitted. Let meta-receipts and
revision evidence enter a separate typed store, with no import rule turning them into object premises.
Admit an action changing active receipt permission or clock only after a sound external gate checks its
exact parent, authorization, cumulative consistency and operational preservation; unknown or rejected
evidence never activates the change. Then every accepted object theorem in a finite mixed history is
an object consequence of the original hypotheses, regardless of the receipt-selection policy. Under these
assumptions the examples do not prove intrinsic non-singleton superiority: any comparator admitting a
causal, legal policy translation that preserves the observation interface, successful settlement on every
required state or branch, and no-greater complete cost inherits the same or better optimal cost. A
separate hindsight replay chosen using a hidden state is not sufficient for a policy comparison. This
clause concerns the same expected or worst-case objective and failure convention, not arbitrary latency
or security claims.

In the finite parity trial a versioned state can contain (x, v, A, J, L, z, c, B, S) (hidden bits, version,
permissions, observed indices, receipts, phase, cost, theorem BANK, sovereign); use the following
guards as the transition specification. For each fixed x the resulting finite reachable-state diagram
gives an interpretation of its version-indexed controller descriptions, not of an unspecified universal
gift theory. The pre-state denies full coalition access; the successor admits it. The permission gift, if
selected, is committed before queries; the witness is a single-gift trial with no subsequent epoch change.
The finite-state model checks authorization and rejects forged, stale, or mismatched receipts, without
treating rejection as proof of inconsistency. Keep the same object proof rule, and distinguish local
facts, accepted meta-receipts and the final object proof. The paid terminal action completes an actual
proof-delivery move. In detail: initially the version is 0, the observed set and stores are empty, and the
stage is preselection. The optional grant is a one-time version-0 to version-1 transition at the initial
authorized boundary with a matching parent, positive external witness and charged cost K. At version
0, a query must leave at least one coordinate unread; at version 1 any unused coordinate may be queried.
The receipt guard checks the current version, source, scope, access and true bit; on acceptance it
appends only to the meta-store and charges ki . After query selection closes, exactly one terminal action
is permitted; an unauthorized query or malformed receipt is not an information-producing move in this
ideal trial. If an implementation admits rejected probes as actions, their outputs and charges must be
included in a new contract and its information lower bound re-established. The transfer compatibility
guard routes a mismatch to the charged fallback, and only the checked object derivation enters BANK.
All terminal states are closed. This grammar is finite because there is at most one grant, at most n
distinct queries and one terminal action (with bounded fallback). For the clock trial use the finite
permutation as an effective schedule description, prove its preservation obligations by Theorem 5.2, and
realize any bounded run as a finite history. For the parity grammar, use a controller metalanguage
with constants for the finite states, version-indexed permission predicates, an edge relation given
exactly by the legal transitions and fixed sovereign identity; the finite transition structure interprets
the corresponding closed diagram, including its negative edge facts. All version-0 descriptions remain
historical rather than contradictory current assertions. Object proof validity is checked separately by
modus ponens. This gives consistency for this explicitly stipulated finite controller diagram, not for
an external axiomatization containing further constraints. The clock grammar is instead an effective
schedule family with finite executed prefixes. These are SCDF-native restricted models, not a proof
that an arbitrary GBG+S atom or a joint gift history is admissible. No actual gift has been granted to
a deployed system.
Failure modes. If a receipt is stale after a revision, the old historical claim does not certify the current
configuration. If the gate reveals the compatibility bit for free, the lower bound fails. If the baseline
can pool all receipts, the restriction disappears. If the wrong fast route costs less than 2h − l, a blind
attempt may improve expected cost. If an object proof is accepted based on the orientation claim alone,
the model violates C’s typed boundary. If schedule rewrites add mandatory overhead or change which
workers can prove the target, the simple clock formula no longer applies. If checking is not sound,
acceptance is not factivity. These are explicit scope conditions, not after-the-fact exceptions.
