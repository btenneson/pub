\section*{1. What was repaired and why}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Verifier closure (material).} Original v0.2 stipulated sound terminating checking but did not require an otherwise valid graft to be accepted. New Definition 2.1 defines correct finite MP proof DAGs and an \emph{iff} checker, with explicit subproof heredity, opening of a cut occurrence, and substitution/graft closure. Thus Theorem 3.1 has the hypothesis its proof needs.
\item \textbf{Proof-specific compatibility (material).} Original Theorem 4.2 identified $H\vdash L$ with eventual fixed-Scout meeting without quantifying over acceptable Probe receipts. Repaired Theorem 4.2 first gives the exact criterion $\exists\pi\in E_P(L)\,\mathsf{Comp}(\pi,\rho_L)$; the simpler $H\vdash L$ iff is stated only with formula completeness \emph{and uniform compatibility}. Certificate completeness supports a separate existence-of-compatible-proof formulation.
\item \textbf{DAG opening and several obligations.} Proposition 6.3 expressly relies on hereditary extraction and opening; Proposition 8.2 requires jointly compatible finite discharge receipts and suitable Probe fairness. This prevents importing an arbitrary accepted certificate into an incompatible proof scope.
\item \textbf{Chronology and provenance.} The first-settlement time is $s_T=+\infty$ if the unassisted branch never settles. The manuscript names its checks as internal paper-level review and uses the requested author name form \texttt{<Brian Tenneson>} in both typeset credit and PDF metadata.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{3. Adversarial counterchecks}
\textbf{Bounded verifier obstruction.} For $H=\{p,p\to\ell,\ell\to t\}$, a sound verifier capped at one MP step accepts separately the prefix $H\vdash\ell$ and suffix $H,\ell\vdash t$ but rejects the two-MP splice. The new checker hypothesis explicitly rules this out. It is a countermodel to the \emph{old} weak verifier assumptions, not to new Theorem 3.1.

\textbf{Stranded Scout.} The valuation $p=t=\mathrm{true}$, $q=\mathrm{false}$ satisfies $\{p,p\to t,q\to t\}$ and falsifies $q$; the fixed Scout's route through $q$ cannot be discharged despite $H\vdash t$.

\textbf{Three midpoint controls.} A target-first derived $L=t\land(q\to q)$ is rejected by the target-free proof ancestry test; a genuine $p\to\ell\to t$ cut can be found after independent settlement; and a closed $q\to q$ bridge from $H=\{t\}$ can be structurally legal yet logically inessential. All three examples support \emph{limited} claims and do not imply an advantage.

\textbf{Finite illustrative execution.} A small standalone program checked 10 cases: accepted one-step forward and conditional proofs, accepted their two-step graft with a full local checker, reproduced rejection of the graft by the sound one-MP-cap checker, and rejected swapped MP ports, incorrect conclusions, unresolved open assumptions, and backward/cyclic dependency indices. Those tests only illustrate the finite rules; they do not machine-verify arbitrary quantified theorems.
\section*{4. Remaining scope boundaries}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item A proof of $T$ yields at best the degenerate cut $L=T$ without more assumptions. Existence of a \emph{genuine} target-free intermediate proof occurrence needs the additional witness required in Proposition 6.3.
\item Fairness gives eventual encounter under unbounded execution, not an effective universal time bound or a proof that first rendezvous beats direct settlement.
\item $\mathsf{Mid}$ is decidable for a supplied finite proof triple. Deciding whether \emph{some} eligible triple exists for a formula is not established.
\item Translation from an MP-only presentation back to a different first-order calculus depends on the external decoder's correctness, not established here.
\item Real AMLD instances, machine-checker integration, charged runtime benchmarking, and empirical speedup remain unperformed.
\end{enumerate}
