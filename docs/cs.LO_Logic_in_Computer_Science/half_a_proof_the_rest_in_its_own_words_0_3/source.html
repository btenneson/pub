\begin{abstract}
An AMLD+0-style conjecture-settlement prompt supplied the first approximately one-half of
an existing mathematical proof from \emph{Exact Addressing}, Preliminary Release 3.3.2,
Section 15, and asked the designated settler to assess the future-tense claim
``I will finish the proof.'' The prompt disclosed 197 of 395 whitespace-delimited
source-LaTeX tokens and withheld 198. An in-chat reconstruction produced a complete
conditional derivation of the capstone's expected-cost bound and amortized threshold.
This revised note explicitly identifies where the reconstructed proof differs from the author's existing proof in length,
logical method, explanatory detail, and mathematical ingenuity. The reconstructed write-up
is longer but uses essentially the same argument. Its contribution is clarity and faithful
reconstruction, not a new lemma or fundamentally distinct proof strategy. Because the
full source was accessed earlier in the same conversation, and because no authenticated
six-agent AMLD+0 execution or independent checker receipt was obtained, the exercise
cannot establish blind discovery or certified AMLD+0 settlement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and experimental boundary}
The research question is not just whether the theorem can be shown true. It is whether a
settler supplied with a truncated, valid mathematical history can construct a valid
continuation, and how that continuation compares with the finished reference proof.
The title invokes a partly built crossing: a settler receives half of a worked
argument and supplies the missing reasoning in its own words. The metaphor does
not assert a new mathematical route. As shown below, the reconstructed argument
reaches the same theorem by essentially the same mathematical strategy.
The source is the conditional \emph{Certified Geometric Discovery and Amortized Search
Advantage} theorem in Section 15 of \emph{Exact Addressing 3.3.2}. The earlier
\emph{Depths of a Simulation 45.2} induction example is a different experiment and is
not the source of the target proof considered here.

The prepared source proof contains \textbf{395} whitespace-delimited LaTeX tokens.
The supplied prefix ends after ``Fix task $i$, let $g=g_i$ and $p=p_i$.''
It contains \textbf{197} tokens, or approximately \textbf{49.87\%}; the remaining
\textbf{198} tokens were withheld from the submitted prompt. These percentages are
textual measurements, \emph{not} counts of formally certified inference steps and not
measurements of geometric basin depth.

The experiment is accurately described as an \emph{in-chat candidate reconstruction}.
The original source's full proof was fetched during prompt preparation in this same
conversation, so genuine separation from its hidden continuation was not established.
The subsequent reconstructed argument is mathematically assessable, but its success
cannot be interpreted as a controlled blind test.

\section{The mathematical target}
Under H1--H6, the capstone establishes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T1}: theoremhood is invariant under effective exact rule-preserving recoding;
\item \textbf{T2}: the exact shortest-proof layers are invariant under that recoding;
\item \textbf{T3}: a protected exhaustive prover eventually emits a verifier-accepted certificate for each provable target;
\item \textbf{T4}: the guided procedure has a fully charged expected-cost advantage whenever the displayed lower bound is positive.
\end{itemize}
The quantitative T4 target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
\begin{split}
\mathbb E[C_{\mathsf B}-C_{\mathsf H}]
\ge {}&\frac q2(p_0N-g_0)-a_0NL_N\\
&-q[(a_1\beta_0\Delta_0+a_2g_0)L_N+a_3],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $L_N=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$. It also establishes a strict expected-cost
advantage for all sufficiently large $N$ and all $q\ge KL_N$, provided
$K>2a_0/p_0$ and the stipulated constants are independent of $N$.
This is a \emph{conditional} theorem: the hypotheses do not by themselves establish
that any particular solver or problem family satisfies the locality or charged-cost
assumptions in practice.

\subsection{Reader's note: the differences, explicitly}
\noindent\textbf{These are not identical proofs line by line.} The agreement reported
above is agreement in the \emph{major mathematical inferences and strategy}, not a
byte-level match, a statement that every sentence has an exact counterpart, or an
independently certified equivalence of formal derivation objects. For the reader,
the specific differences are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{D\arabic*.}, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Length and compression.} The reference uses 395 whitespace-delimited
LaTeX tokens and the normalized candidate 515: 120 more tokens overall (about 30\%).
Within the withheld region, the reference has 198 tokens and the candidate's
new continuation has 333: 135 more (about 68\%). These token counts are
format-dependent textual measures, not formal step counts or a speed benchmark.

\item \textbf{How T1--T3 are presented.} The original proof states proof transport,
length invariance, and protected enumeration economically. The normalized candidate
rephrases them, emphasizes the reverse transport and the finite enumeration index,
and explicitly notes the case of an earlier successful emission. This is clarification,
not an additional theorem or a different approach.

\item \textbf{Probability calculation.} Both proofs use exactly the same two-case
conditioning on whether the unique accepted candidate is in the selected neighborhood.
The candidate devotes more prose to the interpretations of the two expected ranks:
$(g+1)/2$ inside the neighborhood and $g+(N-g+1)/2$ outside it. Both obtain
$\mathbb E[R_{\mathsf H,i}]=(N+g+1-pN)/2$ and gross savings $(pN-g)/2$.
The candidate did not find a different probabilistic argument.

\item \textbf{Cost-accounting exposition.} The original immediately turns the
single-task rank saving into a $q$-task lower bound, subtracts the charged H5 ledger,
and invokes H6 for shared-cost treatment. The candidate spells out the same sequence
with extra commentary about linearity of expectation, why independence of tasks is
unnecessary, and when common costs may cancel. It adds explicitness, not a new cost bound.

\item \textbf{Asymptotic details.} The reference compresses the argument into a
positive coefficient $Kp_0/2-a_0$ of $N$ with remaining terms only $O(\log N)$.
The candidate introduces $b_N$, expands the bracket, and explains why positivity
holds for sufficiently large $N$. The same hypothesis $K>2a_0/p_0$ and the same
threshold conclusion are used; no improvement in the threshold or weakened
assumptions is obtained.

\item \textbf{New lemmas, ingenuity, and provenance.} Neither version needs a new
intermediate lemma in the continuation. The candidate offers no new invariant,
proof method, stronger conclusion, or strictly shorter proof. The original has the
compressed key ideas; the candidate supplies a pedagogical expansion. Moreover,
the complete original proof had already been retrieved in this conversation before
the attempt. Similarity therefore cannot establish blind or independent discovery.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Equivalence in conclusion is weaker than identity of steps.}
Both arguments support the same conditional theorem under the same hypotheses,
subject to mathematical review. This does \emph{not} mean their proof texts or
formal inference traces are identical; neither an external machine-checked
step-by-step correspondence nor a certified proof-object equivalence is claimed.

\subsection{Ingenuity and mathematical novelty}
\textbf{Proof strategy: essentially the same.}
Both proofs exploit an elementary and effective device: condition on whether a unique
successful candidate lies inside the prioritized subset. That gives a precise expected
rank benefit $(p_iN-g_i)/2$, which is then compared with a separately charged guidance
ledger. The second idea is asymptotic amortization: for $q$ of order $\log N$, the
positive $N\log N$ term overtakes the prescribed overhead once
$Kp_0/2>a_0$. These are the substantive insights in \emph{both} proofs.

\textbf{Expository ingenuity: somewhat stronger in the candidate.}
The reconstruction does a better job of displaying the two conditional ranks,
showing where each inequality enters, and separating gross benefit from net,
fully charged savings. Those choices improve auditability and readability.

\textbf{New mathematics: not demonstrated.}
The candidate introduces no independent intermediate lemma, new invariant, different
proof architecture, stronger theorem, or smaller hypothesis set. It should not be
advertised as an original mathematical proof method; at most, it is a longer and
clearer exposition of an existing proof.

\textbf{Difficulty: meaningful but bounded.}
The withheld part required nontrivial bookkeeping across a probability calculation,
an expectation bound, an overhead ledger, and a quantified asymptotic threshold.
However, the theorem statement already supplied the target inequality, and the
visible prefix explicitly identified the intended T4 calculation and variables
$g,p$. The challenge was reconstruction of a strongly signposted continuation,
not discovery of an unknown theorem.

\section{Mathematical checks and evidentiary status}
Three algebraic identities were independently simplified with the SymPy symbolic
algebra library while preparing this note: the two-case guided-rank expectation,
the gross expected-rank saving, and the rearranged amortization bound. All three
simplified to zero residual. This checks the \emph{algebraic transformations} only;
it is neither a proof of H1--H6 nor acceptance by an external formal verifier.

The new argument appears correct conditional on the exact meanings of H1--H6.
It does not show an implemented guided solver, does not prove existence of a
substantive task family meeting H4--H5, and does not establish empirical speedup.
Equally important, the claimed first-person settlement event needs an authenticated
prover and a genuine independent checking receipt. Neither is supplied by an
in-chat argument.

\section{Conclusion}
The 50\%-prefix exercise produced a mathematically coherent continuation of the
\emph{Exact Addressing 3.3.2} conditional capstone. Quantitatively, the normalized
candidate uses 515 source-style LaTeX tokens versus 395 in the existing reference
proof, while its new continuation uses 333 tokens versus 198 in the withheld suffix.
Qualitatively, it is more detailed but not a substantially more inventive argument:
both proofs follow the same conditioning, subtraction, and asymptotic-amortization
path. The result is worth retaining as a proof-reconstruction demonstration and
comparative audit, not as evidence of independent discovery, a verified six-agent
federation, or a settled first-person prediction.
