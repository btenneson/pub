Volume II: The Geometry of Certified Frontiers

Cuts, Antichains, Proof-Carrying Maps, and the Price of Discovery

CAMI (AI-assisted mathematical exposition)
Research interlocutor: Brian Tenneson
October 2026 · PRELIMINARY RELEASE

“A frontier without information is not an accelerator.”

Abstract

We develop a finite theory of path-hitting frontiers, certified information refinement, sound pruning, and the economics of scouting. Results are conditional on explicit graph, verifier, and probability assumptions. Priority and external peer review are not claimed.

1. Formal board

Let G=(S,E,r,τ,T,c) be a finite rooted directed acyclic graph, with clock τ strictly increasing along every edge, target set T, and nonnegative edge costs c. Write u≼v if u=v or u reaches v. A history is a root-to-sink directed path.

2. Path-cut theorem

Theorem. F⊆S meets every history if and only if deleting F eliminates all paths from r to any original sink outside F.

Proof. A path surviving deletion is exactly a history avoiding F; either condition is the negation of such a path's existence.

One-crossing theorem. If F is both path-complete and an antichain under reachability, each history meets F exactly once. Completeness gives at least one meeting, while two points on one history are comparable.

3. Settlement recursion

Let d(s) be the minimum cost of a path from s to a target, or +∞ if none exists. Then

d(s)=0 if s∈T; otherwise d(s)=min { c(s,u)+d(u) : (s,u)∈E }.

Proof. Every nonempty successful path begins with an outgoing edge; reverse topological induction proves the recurrence.

Frontier decomposition. If all targets are sinks and F is a path-complete antichain,

d(r)=min { d(r,f)+d(f) : f∈F }.

Every accepting history crosses F exactly once; split at the crossing. Conversely concatenate optimal prefix and suffix, which cannot overlap in a DAG without producing a cycle.

4. Width–height theorem

Let w be the largest antichain cardinality and h the maximum number of vertices on a directed path. Then |S|≤wh.

Proof. Give each vertex rank equal to the maximum number of vertices on a path ending there. Comparable distinct vertices have different ranks. Each of h rank layers is an antichain, hence has at most w vertices.

5. Certified map preorder

Fix authorized assumptions H and a sound verifier. A certified map M is a finite collection of verified statements about the fixed board. Define M⊑N if every statement of M is entailed by H∪N.

Theorem. ⊑ is reflexive and transitive; quotienting by mutual entailment yields a partial order. Proof. Both properties follow from reflexivity and transitivity of logical entailment; the quotient enforces antisymmetry.

6. Safe pruning theorem

If a sound verifier certifies that no target is reachable from each state in U, suppressing exploration beyond U preserves whether a root-to-target path exists.

Proof. A successful path through u∈U would provide a forbidden suffix from u to a target, contradicting the certificate.

7. Scout value

Under independent success probability p per scout, value V of at least one success, and cost c per scout, the expected value of m scouts is

W(m)=V[1−(1−p)^m]−cm.

The mth scout adds Vp(1−p)^(m−1)−c, so is worthwhile exactly when Vp(1−p)^(m−1)>c. If the first success incurs verification cost v, replace V by V−v. Correlation and shared costs invalidate these simplified assumptions.

8. Symmetry barrier

Theorem. If one accepting leaf is selected uniformly from N leaves and each test reveals only success or failure, every adaptive strategy testing distinct leaves requires an expected (N+1)/2 tests.

Proof. Conditional on prior failures, the accepting leaf remains uniformly distributed among untested leaves. Its test position is uniform on 1,…,N.

9. Open conjectures

Certified locality surplus. There may be natural search-board families in which the total charged cost of map-assisted certified settlement is asymptotically lower than a frozen uninformed baseline.

Cross-board frontier transfer. A certified reachability-preserving relation between boards may allow transfer of a frontier cut certificate more cheaply than reconstructing it.

10. Research standard

Fix board generator, target distribution, verifier, budgets, and accounting before trials. Compare blind traversal, antichain enumeration, certified mapping, and mapping plus scouts. Record verification, rejected reports, and total costs. A frontier is a geometric object; an informative certified map is an epistemic one. Neither alone proves acceleration.
