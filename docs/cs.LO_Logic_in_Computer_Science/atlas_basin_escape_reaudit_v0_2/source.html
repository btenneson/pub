# Second-pass mathematical audit

## 1. Correctness of the revised settlement semantics

Definition 8.1 now uses **existential potential settlement**:

$$
\operatorname{PotSet}_{c,b}(X)
\iff
\exists\rho\in\operatorname{Runs}_b(X,q_c)
\quad \operatorname{Acc}_{q_c}(\rho)\in\{+,-\}.
$$

The successful trace must be legal, verifier-accepted, and within the fully charged common bound. In contrast, Definition 3.1 evaluates the **one actual authenticated run** of the frozen machine/scheduler/input to determine whether the designated first-person speaker emits a proof of **$c$**, not just of either sign. These two events are properly kept distinct.

**Counterexample retest (former blocking issue):** Let $X$ have a successful proof branch. Let $Y$ contain that branch, but let $Y$'s frozen actual scheduler choose an idle branch. Potential settlement may hold for both systems, whereas actual success may hold only for $X$. The repaired paper correctly allows that divergence. The old counterexample no longer falsifies the **new** Theorem 8.3.

## 2. Preorder and quotient validity (Theorem 8.3)

Definition 8.2 requires a uniform transport of every successful trace across **every task in a common task family**, preserving legal certificate polarity and not exceeding the **same** bound. Identity transport gives reflexivity. Composing transports gives transitivity **provided** their legality and budget-preserving properties compose, as the definition explicitly requires. Mutual simulation is therefore an equivalence relation and induces a partial order on the quotient. If $X\sim Y$, the witness-transfer implication holds both ways, so membership in $U^\exists_{c,b}$ is representative-independent. The upward-set claim then follows by one witness transfer. **PASS, conditional on the promised operational transports.**

**Scope:** The theorem is an elementary order-theoretic consequence of a deliberately strong simulation contract, not a demonstration that the contract has been implemented, or a proof that a randomly selected scheduler will emit the certificate. It is appropriate to retain the word “conditional.”

## 3. Budget semantics and signed task family

The former same-budget gap is closed by explicitly requiring target-bound preservation in Definition 8.2. Remark 8.5 correctly weakens a non-budget-preserving simulation to a cross-budget conclusion at $g_{X,Y}(b)$. This repair prevents any implicit zero-cost transfer of proofs, activation, checking, or communication. **PASS.**

The conjecture preorder in Definition 8.9 uses the **same** simulation poset and bound for both $c$ and $d$, so comparing $U_d\subseteq U_c$ is now type-correct. It is reflexive/transitive by the corresponding set-inclusion laws. **PASS.**

## 4. BANK dependency theorem (Lemma 6.3)

Definition 6.1 preserves proof, theory, assumptions, source, checker version, charge, and provenance. Definition 6.2 adds the missing **mandatory receiver-side check**: every undischarged assumption is either admitted in the current context or supported by an independently accepted derivation there, and proof composition must be sound. Lemma 6.3 now explicitly invokes both storage fidelity and this consumer-side enforcement. The example $p\vdash p$ versus $\vdash p$ correctly demonstrates why accurate archival metadata alone is insufficient. **PASS under the stated kernel and soundness assumptions.**

Residual implementation obligation: an actual shared BANK must enforce this import policy at **every** read/use interface. An informal design description alone cannot rule out a buggy implementation.

## 5. Minimum-antichain theorem (Theorem 8.7)

The proof is valid. For any $x$ in an upper set $U$ in a well-founded poset, choose a minimal member $m$ of $U\cap\downarrow x$. Such $m$ is also minimal in $U$; otherwise a smaller $U$-member would also lie below $x$. Thus $x\in\uparrow\operatorname{Min}(U)$. The reverse containment uses upper closure. Distinct minima are incomparable. **PASS.**

Remark 8.8 correctly notes that the minimal antichain need not be finite. An equality-only countably infinite poset is well-founded and has infinitely many minimal elements. A finite-basis result would need a stronger restriction, such as a well-quasi-order. The rational open-cut counterexample correctly limits arbitrary closed-threshold claims.

## 6. Negation symmetry (Proposition 8.10)

The result is expressly conditional on a shared classical-compatible checker/task family and **same-budget** accepted proof conversion when necessary. A proof of $c$ can be transformed into one of $\neg\neg c$; a proof of $\neg\neg c$ can be transformed back to $c$ only where the requisite principle (e.g., classical double-negation elimination) is valid. The manuscript explicitly charges conversions, so it does not infer same-budget equivalence for free. **PASS as a conditional proposition.**

Object-level symmetric settlement still does not equate the separate actual first-person predictions “I will prove $c$” and “I will prove $\neg c$.” This boundary is preserved.

# Review of performance claims and protocol freezing

The repaired experimental loss is $L_{b,\lambda}=C$ for verified success, versus $b+\lambda$ for bounded non-success, for a scalar aggregate charged bound $b$ and predeclared failure penalty $\lambda>0$. Vector resources require a predeclared scalar charge convention plus separate hard dimension limits. This makes “cheap failure” an invalid shortcut to a lower expected *settlement loss*. It does **not** prevent researchers from obtaining different experimental conclusions at different penalties; $\lambda$ must be declared **before** seeing the outcomes.

The four comparator arms remain: solo prover; matched independent workers without sharing; shared BANK without adaptive activation; and Player-0-activated federation. The protocol freezes executable versions, target, checkers, speaker, seeds, legal actions, scheduler, resource accounting, handoffs and analysis. No forced speedup is claimed from reachability monotonicity. The conjecture adds an explicit noninferiority constraint for success probability at the common bound. **PASS as a prospective controlled-test design**, not an executed trial.

1. **No mechanized proof certificate:** The logical proof checks here are manual. Theorems 8.3 and 8.7 can be formalized in an independent proof assistant if later requested, but that has not occurred.
2. **No working certified simulator / BANK implementation:** Definitions specify correct behavior; they do not prove an executable meets the contracts. In particular, verifying operational trace translation and budget preservation can be a substantial task.
3. **No empirical speedup result:** The existence conjecture has not been tested. Conditional cost formulas do not establish observed performance.
4. **Source fidelity still unverified:** The original references were retained and labeled as such. The companion full texts were not independently re-audited, and the cited GBG+ publication URL could not be retrieved using public web access in this session.
5. **No silent protocol authorization:** Nothing in this document authorizes a run, substitutes a solver, revises a frozen bank, or modifies a published repository.
