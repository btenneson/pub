\begin{abstract}
The Level-1 multiarticle \emph{The Algebra of Certified Dream Surplus} supplies a unique least dependency-complete waking package when the required packet witnesses are fixed. It does not determine which evidence to retain when a post-wake query admits several individually validated, differently priced witnesses. We define a finite certified wake-sufficiency system: a typed acyclic evidence arena, frozen witness alternatives for bounded queries, and additive nonnegative retention charges. We prove a witness-selection normal form and an exact finite minimum-cost expression; construct an exponential antichain of inclusion-minimal sufficient packages; derive the mandatory-evidence core and show that it may be insufficient; characterize exact safe deletion, one-query extension, and shared-ancestry savings; distinguish submodularity of fixed-witness costs from failure of submodularity after optimizing witness choices; and reduce finite Set Cover to budgeted certified waking in a depth-one, positive-integer-cost arena. We relate these results to trait thresholds, nondominated cost--answer frontiers, and conditional revocation repair. This is a snapshot optimization theory, not a theorem about discovering proofs, universal theorem-proving speedups, or unrestricted authorization changes. An ATLAS repair pass has clarified the role of alternative selection, strict budgets and guards, zero-cost ties, complexity edge cases, and the limitations of audit evidence. The original W0 and W1 are unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question beyond a single fixed certificate}\label{gl-first-W2-s1}
In the ancestral singleton design W0~\cite{W0}, one automated theorem prover hosts fourteen ordinary dreaming roles $P_1,\dots,P_{14}$ and a distinguished coordinating Player 0. Its internal game admits fresh typed evidence only after an actor-attributed and independently checked victory. Evidence remains dream-local until an authorized waking transaction. A history receipt is not an object proof; successful evaluation is not proof authority.

The immediate ancestor W1~\cite{W1} separates game trajectories, acyclic packet dependencies, and operational post-wake trait tiers. For a frozen, conflict-free single-witness dependency DAG, the least waking package for specified packet nodes $Q$ is their ancestral ideal $\Anc(Q)$ (W1, Theorem 5.5 as reported by this manuscript). But two different proofs may warrant the same bounded mathematical question; several checked historical views may warrant a bounded historical question; and a single authenticated source may support multiple queries through separately checked, typed bridges. Choosing among witnesses becomes part of the mathematical problem.

\begin{quote}\itshape
Given a finite pool of already validated, current-epoch typed evidence and predeclared post-wake questions, which dependency-complete package preserves the required answers at the lowest incremental retention charge? What evidence is compulsory regardless of witness choice, and what remains true after explicit revocation?
\end{quote}

We call the resulting object a \emph{witness-selective sufficient ideal}. Its existence does not imply that an ATP discovers any of its candidate witnesses: every potential witness has already been supplied and checked in the stipulated finite arena.

\section{A typed, witness-selective waking system}\label{gl-first-W2-s2}
\subsection{The currently valid finite arena}
\begin{definition}[Certified wake-sufficiency system]
Fix one waking epoch and a finite set $A$ of immutable packet identities. Each packet has passed its declared \textsc{PROOF}, \textsc{AUDIT}, or \textsc{EVAL} checker; formation-checked \textsc{HYPOTHESIS} records may be retained but never alone warrant a proved query. Every $v\in A$ has an exact set of \emph{internal} prerequisites $\dep(v)\subseteq A$. Any prerequisite outside $A$ must be explicitly guaranteed valid in the already available live base $L$ and incurs no additional charge in this model. The directed edges $p\to v$ for $p\in\dep(v)$ form an acyclic graph. Write $p\preceq v$ for reflexive transitive reachability, $\downarrow v=\{p:p\preceq v\}$, and $\Anc(Q)=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\downarrow q$.

Let $U=\bigsqcup_{\tau\in T}U_\tau$ be a disjoint, finite collection of typed bounded query obligations, with $T$ the fourteen operational trait indices of W1. Let $S_L\subseteq U$ be the queries already warranted by the live base. For each $\gamma\in U\setminus S_L$, freeze an explicitly listed set $W_\gamma\subseteq A$ of alternative warrant packets. Membership $w\in W_\gamma$ means that a \emph{separately validated, type-correct, scope-authorized, fixed-version} finite projection of $w$, together with the declared prerequisites, warrants the exact query $\gamma$. Two alternative witnesses must have equivalent warrant meaning for that specific query, not necessarily identical provenance. A checked hypothesis formation does not warrant a proof query.

Assign each $v\in A$ a \emph{nonnegative} additive incremental charge $c(v)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$, and $\cost(J)=\sum_{v\in J}c(v)$. Fix all validators, checking outcomes, bridge signatures, scopes and permissions for the epoch. \emph{Every order ideal in $A$ is authorized}; there are no mutual exclusions, subset-dependent guards, global all-or-nothing constraints, or validity losses from adding other accepted packets.
\end{definition}

These assumptions are substantive. Real wake transactions may additionally have expiring credentials, negative/absence tests, simultaneous physical limits, or checker behavior changing with other stored packets. Such systems must first be translated to a different validated arena. Additivity concerns a declared retention tariff; it is \emph{not} an implicit end-to-end charge for discovering or checking witnesses.

\begin{definition}[Sufficiency]
An ideal is a downward-closed $J\subseteq A$; the finite set of all such ideals is $\Id(A)$. Its warranted answers are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
S(J)=S_L\cup\{\gamma\in U\setminus S_L:J\cap W_\gamma\ne\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
For $\Gamma\subseteq U\setminus S_L$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible}
\mathcal F_\Gamma=\{J\in\Id(A):\Gamma\subseteq S(J)\},\qquad
m(\Gamma)=\begin{cases}\min_{J\in\mathcal F_\Gamma}\cost(J),&\mathcal F_\Gamma\ne\varnothing,\\+\infty,&\mathcal F_\Gamma=\varnothing.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Such a $J$ is called $\Gamma$-sufficient. The supported questions are fixed finite judgments, not future proof-search outcomes.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Trust boundary]
Validation of the packet and validation of each authorized query projection are distinct stipulated checks. Seeing the same source through two checked projections does not create two independent primary discoveries. Any purported bridge that bypasses the appropriate query checker falls outside the system.
\end{remark}

\section{The witness-selection normal form}\label{gl-first-W2-s3}
W1 fixes target packet identities before taking their ancestral closure. Here the target is a typed question that may have several admissible packets.

\begin{theorem}[Normal form and exact minimum]\label{thm:normal}
For a finite certified wake-sufficiency system and $\Gamma\subseteq U\setminus S_L$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathcal F_\Gamma\ne\varnothing$ if and only if $W_\gamma\ne\varnothing$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
\item Every \emph{inclusion-minimal} $J\in\mathcal F_\Gamma$ can be written $J=\Anc(\{f(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma\})$ for a choice function $f(\gamma)\in W_\gamma$.
\item With the convention that the empty choice has empty ancestry and cost zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimum}
m(\Gamma)=\min_{f\in\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_\gamma}\cost\!\left(\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\downarrow f(\gamma)\right).
\end{equation}
Interpret the minimum over an empty family of choice functions as $+\infty$. Whenever feasible, at least one minimum-cost ideal is witness-generated, even when charges can be zero.
\end{enumerate}
The converse of (ii) is \emph{not} automatic: a choice-generated ideal may have a proper sufficient subideal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every sufficient ideal $J$ contains some $f(\gamma)\in J\cap W_\gamma$ for each $\gamma$. Downward closure therefore gives $J_f:=\bigcup_\gamma\downarrow f(\gamma)\subseteq J$, and $J_f$ remains sufficient. Conversely, any selection of one warrant per query yields a sufficient ideal. This proves (i); if $J$ is inclusion-minimal, $J_f\subseteq J$ forces equality, proving (ii). Nonnegative additivity gives $\cost(J_f)\le\cost(J)$, so minimizing over selections and over all ideals yields the same answer. This proves (iii); for $\Gamma=\varnothing$, $\varnothing$ is the empty witness selection and is cost-minimal. For the caution following (iii), take a chain $a\prec b$ with $W_\gamma=\{a,b\}$: choosing $b$ yields $\{a,b\}$, although $\{a\}$ is sufficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The fixed-witness case]
If $W_\gamma=\{w_\gamma\}$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$, then $\Anc(\{w_\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\})$ is the unique \emph{inclusion-minimal} sufficient ideal and is a minimum-cost ideal. Zero-cost superideals may tie in cost; hence it is not necessarily the unique optimizer.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}Every sufficient ideal contains every $w_\gamma$ and its ancestors. The common ancestry is itself sufficient.\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Choosing a path rather than both]
Two independent, individually checked proof packets $a,b$ both warrant a PROOF query $\gamma$, with charges $c(a)=2$ and $c(b)=5$. The sufficient ideals are $\{a\}$, $\{b\}$ and $\{a,b\}$, with $m(\{\gamma\})=2$. Witness choice, rather than just taking ancestry of a prescribed witness, is now optimized.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Upper-family geometry and exponential branching]\label{thm:antichain}
For feasible $\Gamma$, $\mathcal F_\Gamma$ is an upward-closed, union-closed family within $\Id(A)$. Its minimal members form an antichain, and every member contains some minimal member. It need not be intersection-closed. There are validated depth-zero arenas with $n$ required queries and exactly $2^n$ distinct inclusion-minimal sufficient ideals, while their mandatory core is empty.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $J$ contains a warrant for each query, any larger ideal does too; hence the family is upward-closed and union-closed. Finite descent proves that each member contains a minimal member, and distinct minimal members are incomparable. For failure of intersection closure, take independent $a,b$ as alternative witnesses for a single query: the sufficient ideals $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ intersect in the insufficient empty ideal.

For the exponential family, use $2n$ independent packets $a_i,b_i$ and $n$ obligations with $W_{\gamma_i}=\{a_i,b_i\}$, $1\le i\le n$. Inclusion-minimal sufficiency requires exactly one packet from each pair; hence precisely $2^n$ ideals are minimal. Each packet can be avoided by taking its mate, so the intersection of all sufficient ideals is empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
W1's complete stable-guard order-ideal family is closed under both union and intersection. Imposing disjunctive witness obligations filters it to an upper family whose intersection closure may fail. A unique least sufficient package therefore cannot be assumed.
\end{remark}

\section{What must survive every valid choice?}\label{gl-first-W2-s4}
\begin{definition}[Mandatory evidence core]
For feasible $\Gamma$, define $\Core(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{J\in\mathcal F_\Gamma}J$. This is the set of packets forced by \emph{all} sufficient packages without a separate budget cap.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact forced-core formula]\label{thm:core}
In the frozen monotone-admission system, for every feasible $\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core}
\boxed{\displaystyle\Core(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ \bigcap_{w\in W_\gamma}\downarrow w.}
\end{equation}
The core is an order ideal, but need not be sufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $p$ is in the right side, some $\gamma$ has $p\preceq w$ for every $w\in W_\gamma$. Every sufficient ideal selects a witness of this query and contains $p$; thus $p$ is in the core. Conversely, if $p$ is not in the right side, for every $\gamma$ there exists $w_\gamma\in W_\gamma$ with $p\not\preceq w_\gamma$. The selection ideal $\bigcup_\gamma\downarrow w_\gamma$ is sufficient by Theorem~\ref{thm:normal} and excludes $p$, so $p$ is not compulsory. Ideals are closed under unions and intersections.\end{proof}

\begin{example}[An insufficient empty core]
For independent proof warrants $a,b$ with $W_\gamma=\{a,b\}$, the core is $\downarrow a\cap\downarrow b=\varnothing$. Yet every sufficient package must retain $a$ or $b$; the empty core warrants neither.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A compulsory shared premise]
A checked common source $s$ precedes two independently checked warrants $a,b$, where $\dep(a)=\dep(b)=\{s\}$ and $W_\gamma=\{a,b\}$. The core is $\{s\}$; the minimal sufficient ideals are $\{s,a\}$ and $\{s,b\}$. The bare source is compulsory, not sufficient.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Budgeted and guarded cores differ]\label{prop:budgetcore}
Adding a cost cap, or restricting authorized ideals using subset-dependent guards, can change which packets are forced among the remaining feasible packages. The original formula \eqref{eq:core} must not be applied unchanged to that restricted family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For independent alternative witnesses $a,b$ with $c(a)=1$, $c(b)=2$, the unconstrained core is empty. With budget $B=1$, the only sufficient ideal is $\{a\}$, hence $a$ is budget-forced. For an authorization-guard example without changing costs, declare that only ideals containing $a$ are permitted. Then among guarded sufficient ideals for $W_\gamma=\{a,b\}$, packet $a$ is forced even though it is not in the unguarded core. In either extension the intersection is defined only if the restricted feasible family is nonempty.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial computation of the unbudgeted core]
For an explicitly encoded DAG and explicitly listed witness sets, feasibility and the compulsory core can be computed in deterministic time polynomial in the arena input length, even though minimum-cost sufficient waking is NP-hard in general.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Check that each required $W_\gamma$ is nonempty. Compute the ancestor set of each listed witness by graph traversal, intersect those sets for each query, then union across queries as in \eqref{eq:core}. At most polynomially many reachability traversals are performed on the explicit graph. The comparison concerns a different task from minimizing $\cost(J)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact evidence deletion and marginal acquisition}\label{gl-first-W2-s5}
\begin{definition}[Upward deletion]
For $p\in J\in\Id(A)$, let $\Up_J(p)=\{v\in J:p\preceq v\}$ and $D_p(J)=J\setminus\Up_J(p)$. For $R\subseteq J$, put $D_R(J)=J\setminus\bigcup_{p\in R}\Up_J(p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact safe-forgetting criterion]\label{thm:delete}
For $J\in\mathcal F_\Gamma$ and $p\in J$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $D_p(J)$ is the unique largest ideal contained in $J$ that excludes $p$.
\item A $\Gamma$-sufficient subideal of $J$ excluding $p$ exists exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delete}
\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\ \exists w\in J\cap W_\gamma\quad p\not\preceq w.
\end{equation}
\item If \eqref{eq:delete} holds, deleting $p$ and all descendants currently in $J$ preserves precisely these frozen obligations. It does not preserve unasked historical evidence or every possible future ATP ability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v\in D_p(J)$ and $u\preceq v$, then $u\in J$; were $p\preceq u$, transitivity would force $p\preceq v$, a contradiction. Thus $D_p(J)$ is an ideal. Every subideal omitting $p$ must omit its descendants, so it is contained in $D_p(J)$. A query remains warranted after deletion precisely if its old witness set contains some $w$ that is not a descendant of $p$, which is \eqref{eq:delete}.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Relative versus global necessity]
A packet can be indispensable within the selected package and absent from $\Core(\Gamma)$. If $J=\{a\}$, $W_\gamma=\{a,b\}$ and $a,b$ are independent, $a$ cannot be deleted from $J$ while retaining $\gamma$, although the alternative valid package $\{b\}$ excludes $a$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Exact one-query incremental extension]\label{thm:increment}
Let $J$ be a valid ideal, and let $\gamma\notin S(J)$ have at least one frozen validated candidate warrant. Suppose existing packets retain validity and additional packets carry the same nonnegative incremental charges. The smallest extra charge to extend $J$ to warrant $\gamma$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
\delta_J(\gamma)=\min_{w\in W_\gamma}\cost\bigl((\downarrow w)\setminus J\bigr).
\end{equation}
An attaining extension is $J\cup\downarrow w^*$ for a minimizing $w^*$. This step need not be jointly optimal for several new questions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any valid extension $J'\supseteq J$ supporting $\gamma$ contains some $w\in W_\gamma$ and its complete ancestry. Its added charge is at least $\cost((\downarrow w)\setminus J)$; taking the best $w$ proves a lower bound. Adjoining the minimizing ancestry is an ideal attaining that bound.\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Greedy single-query decisions may fail]
Three independent checked packets $u,v,z$ have charges $1,2,3$. Authorized projections let $u$ warrant $\alpha$, $v$ warrant both $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and $z$ warrant $\beta$. From an empty ledger, the least costly stand-alone witness for $\alpha$ is $u$, after which adding $v$ for $\beta$ costs another $2$, for total $3$. Jointly retaining $v$ alone costs $2$ and supports both obligations.\end{example}

\section{The economics of shared certified prerequisites}\label{gl-first-W2-s6}
For a fixed finite packet target set $Q\subseteq A$ define $f(Q)=\cost(\Anc(Q))$.
\begin{theorem}[Shared-ancestry identity and submodularity]\label{thm:submod}
For finite $Q,R\subseteq A$ and $v\in A$,
\begin{align}
f(Q\cup R)&=f(Q)+f(R)-\cost\bigl(\Anc(Q)\cap\Anc(R)\bigr),\label{eq:union}\\
f(Q\cup\{v\})-f(Q)&=\cost\bigl((\downarrow v)\setminus\Anc(Q)\bigr).\label{eq:marginal}
\end{align}
Moreover $f$ is monotone and submodular: if $Q\subseteq R$, then
\[
f(Q\cup\{v\})-f(Q)\ \ge\ f(R\cup\{v\})-f(R).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The ancestry of a union is the union of the ancestries. Additive weighted inclusion--exclusion yields \eqref{eq:union}; isolating previously unretained ancestors yields \eqref{eq:marginal}. Since $\Anc(Q)\subseteq\Anc(R)$, we have $(\downarrow v)\setminus\Anc(R)\subseteq(\downarrow v)\setminus\Anc(Q)$. Nonnegative costs prove the marginal inequality and monotonicity.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimized obligation cost is not generally submodular]\label{prop:nonsub}
Even with three independent unit-cost packets and no prerequisites, the optimized query cost $m$ can violate submodularity. On feasible families, $m$ is monotone and subadditive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let independent packets $x,y,z$ warrant respectively $\{\alpha,\beta\}$, $\{\beta,\chi\}$ and $\{\alpha,\chi\}$ through independently checked projections. Then
\[
m(\{\alpha,\beta\})=m(\{\beta,\chi\})=m(\{\beta\})=1,
\qquad m(\{\alpha,\beta,\chi\})=2.
\]
Writing $S=\{\alpha,\beta\}$ and $T=\{\beta,\chi\}$, submodularity would require $1+1\ge2+1$, which is false. Monotonicity follows because requiring more queries cannot enlarge the feasible family. For feasible $S,T$, let $J_S,J_T$ be minimum-cost ideals. Their union satisfies $S\cup T$ and costs at most $\cost(J_S)+\cost(J_T)$, proving $m(S\cup T)\le m(S)+m(T)$.\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Distinct optimization domains]
The submodularity of $f$ concerns chosen \emph{packet identities}. The failure for $m$ concerns optimizing over \emph{query-witness alternatives}. Neither property implies that a myopic packet scheduler optimally selects witnesses for several obligations, and neither proves empirical proof-search speedups.\end{remark}

\section{A finite complexity barrier}\label{gl-first-W2-s7}
\begin{definition}[Budgeted certified waking, \bn]
Input: an explicitly listed acyclic typed evidence arena, exact dependency edges and warrant lists $W_\gamma$, positive integer packet costs in binary, a finite required set $\Gamma$, and a nonnegative integer budget $B$ in binary. All checker judgments and projections are prevalidated input promises of the abstract model. Question: does there exist an ideal $J$ supporting $\Gamma$ with $\cost(J)\le B$?
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Set Cover reduction and conditional NP-completeness]\label{thm:NP}
Finite Set Cover has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to \bn{} using only a depth-one dependency DAG, positive integer packet charges, and fixed nonconflicting checked AUDIT projections. Thus, using the standard NP-completeness of finite Set Cover, \bn{} is NP-complete in its explicit encoding. No part of the reduction requires a proof checker to solve Set Cover.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take a Set Cover instance with finite universe $X=\{1,\dots,n\}$, subsets $C_1,\dots,C_m\subseteq X$, and integer $k\ge0$. Let $r=\sum_{j=1}^m|C_j|$ be the number of incidences and set $M=r+1$. Introduce a source packet $s_j$ of charge $M$ for each $C_j$. For every incidence $i\in C_j$, introduce a bridge packet $b_{ij}$ of charge $1$ whose sole dependency is $s_j$. Its fixed checked AUDIT query projection warrants $\gamma_i$; set $W_{\gamma_i}=\{b_{ij}:i\in C_j\}$. Edges are only $s_j\to b_{ij}$, so the graph has depth one. Let $\Gamma=\{\gamma_i:i\in X\}$ and $B=kM+r$.

If at most $k$ sets cover $X$, retain their source packets and one bridge packet for every universe element, together with required ancestors. The charge is at most $kM+n\le kM+r=B$, since a cover of nonempty $X$ requires every element to appear in at least one incidence. For $X=\varnothing$, the empty ideal is already sufficient and has zero cost, so the same implication holds. Conversely, suppose a sufficient ideal $J$ costs at most $B$ and contains $t$ source packets. Then
\[
tM\le\cost(J)\le kM+r<(k+1)M,
\]
which implies $t\le k$. Each required query is warranted by some retained $b_{ij}$; ideal closure forces its $s_j$. Those $t$ sets cover $X$. If some $i\in X$ has no incidence, then $W_{\gamma_i}=\varnothing$ and the constructed instance is correctly infeasible. The construction has $m+r$ packets and $r$ edges and is polynomial in the incidence-list input length; the integer $M$ and budget have polynomial-length binary encodings.

Finally, \bn{} lies in NP: a proposed $J$ can be checked in polynomial time for exact predecessor closure, all required witness intersections, and the binary integer cost bound. Standard NP-completeness of Set Cover yields the claimed classification.\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The reduction can be instantiated using checked finite membership/audit receipts, not object-theory theorem proofs. The hardness is \emph{combinatorial retention selection} within the finite interface, not proof verification, proof generation, or every waking architecture. Set Cover's background classification has not been claimed as a new result.
\end{remark}

\section{Trait thresholds and the finite answer frontier}\label{gl-first-W2-s8}
\begin{definition}[Trait thresholds]
For each $\tau\in T$, fix nested finite obligations
\[
E_{\tau,0}\subseteq E_{\tau,1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{\tau,d_\tau}\subseteq U_\tau,
\qquad E_{\tau,0}\subseteq S_L.
\]
Define $g_\tau(J)=\max\{k:E_{\tau,k}\subseteq S(J)\}$. For threshold vector $h=(h_\tau)_{\tau\in T}$ with $0\le h_\tau\le d_\tau$, let
\[
\Gamma_h=\left(\bigcup_{\tau\in T}E_{\tau,h_\tau}\right)\setminus S_L.
\]
The disjoint union of query tags preserves trait and type distinctions.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact simultaneous trait criterion]
An ideal $J$ has $g_\tau(J)\ge h_\tau$ for every $\tau$ if and only if $J\in\mathcal F_{\Gamma_h}$. Consequently, its minimal declared retention charge is $m(\Gamma_h)$, given by \eqref{eq:minimum}. If any required non-live query lacks a validated witness, the vector is unattainable within this arena.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By nesting, $g_\tau(J)\ge h_\tau$ exactly when $E_{\tau,h_\tau}\subseteq S(J)$. Conjoin these inclusions and remove the already live $S_L$. The result is precisely $\Gamma_h\subseteq S(J)$. The minimum and infeasibility conclusions follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:normal}.\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Certified answer frontier]
Fix a finite target query pool $\Omega\subseteq U\setminus S_L$. Associate to every ideal $J$ the pair $(\cost(J),S(J)\cap\Omega)$. An attainable pair $(c',Q')$ dominates $(c,Q)$ when $c'\le c$, $Q'\supseteq Q$, and at least one relation is strict. The \emph{certified answer frontier} consists of attainable, nondominated pairs, identifying duplicate pairs only once.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite frontier completeness]\label{thm:frontier}
The frontier is nonempty and has at most $2^{|A|}$ distinct pairs. For any $B\ge0$ and any monotone utility $u:2^\Omega\to\mathbb R$, some utility-maximizing budget-feasible ideal has a nondominated pair. The same holds for utilities monotone in the declared trait-grade vector, provided $\Omega$ includes all non-live tier obligations relevant to those grades. No polynomial-time frontier enumeration is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The empty ideal is attainable, so the finite set of ideals yields at least one nondominated pair and no more than $2^{|A|}$ pairs. Because $c\ge0$ and $B\ge0$, the empty ideal is budget-feasible. Choose a budget-feasible utility maximizer first with \emph{minimum cost} among such maximizers, and then with \emph{largest answer-set cardinality} among the remaining ties. If another attainable pair dominated it, the dominating ideal would also satisfy the budget and, by monotonicity, have no smaller utility; it would also be an optimizer. Strictly lower cost contradicts the first tie-break, while equal cost and strictly more answers contradicts the second. For trait utilities, when $\Omega$ contains every tier-relevant non-live query, answer-set inclusion cannot reduce any declared grade; the same tie-break proof applies. Without that coverage assumption the implication is unjustified.\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A four-point frontier]
Two independent checked warrant nodes $a,b$ cost $2,3$ and support respectively $\alpha,\beta$. For $\Omega=\{\alpha,\beta\}$ the attainable pairs are $(0,\varnothing)$, $(2,\{\alpha\})$, $(3,\{\beta\})$, and $(5,\{\alpha,\beta\})$. None dominates another because each strict answer gain requires an increased charge.
\end{example}

\section{Revocation and the necessity of rebuilding the arena}\label{gl-first-W2-s9}
\begin{definition}[Frozen invalidation]
Let $R\subseteq A$ consist of packets newly rejected by explicit current-version revalidation. Assume every packet with no ancestor in $R$ preserves its prior validator result, scope, permissions and meaning, the live base $S_L$ remains warranted, and no new global constraint appears. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survive}
A^-=\{v\in A:(\downarrow v)\cap R=\varnothing\}
=A\setminus\bigcup_{r\in R}\{v\in A:r\preceq v\},\qquad W^-_\gamma=W_\gamma\cap A^-.
\end{equation}
The signed dependency list is never modified merely by deleting invalid ancestors.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact conditional revocation repair]\label{thm:revoke}
Under the frozen invalidation assumptions, $A^-$ is an ideal of $A$, and $\Gamma$ remains attainable in the surviving arena precisely when $W_\gamma\cap A^-\ne\varnothing$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$. For an old committed ideal $J$, its largest surviving subideal is
\[
J\cap A^-=D_{R\cap J}(J).
\]
If that subideal loses a query, a surviving alternative witness outside $J$ can restore it through a \emph{newly checked, authorized atomic transaction}; no automatic resurrection or global knowledge preservation is implied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $v\in A^-$ and $p\preceq v$, $\downarrow p\subseteq\downarrow v$, so $p\in A^-$. Thus $A^-$ is an ideal and the restricted witness arena retains the positive frozen hypotheses. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:normal}(i). The intersection $J\cap A^-$ removes exactly those nodes in $J$ with an ancestor in $R$. If $v\in J$ has a revoked ancestor $r$, closure of $J$ ensures $r\in J$; therefore the removed set is exactly $\bigcup_{r\in R\cap J}\Up_J(r)$. Every surviving subideal must be inside $J\cap A^-$. A sufficient replacement package need not be contained in the original $J$; its authorization must be rechecked at the new epoch.\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Surviving substitute]
Suppose a historical source receipt $s$ depends on a now-revoked key, warrant $a$ depends on $s$, and independent warrant $b$ is scope-equivalent for the same query. The old package $J=\{s,a\}$ loses its warrant when $s$ becomes invalid. If $b$ is still validated and authorized, a new transaction can choose $\{b\}$. This does not restore the old key's authority or preserve unasked queries.
\end{example}

\section{Where the framework stops}\label{gl-first-W2-s10}
\paragraph{Validity versus utility.} The support map $S(J)$ contains prevalidated finite answers, not predictions of future proof-search time, held-out intuition, or self-soundness. A perfect retention optimizer does not guarantee improved theorem proving or empirical intelligence.

\paragraph{Query-relative sufficiency.} Witness equivalence is equivalence \emph{for the named query and checker version}. A coarse query might fail to distinguish historically distinct sources. If preserving their separate provenance matters, it must be an explicit obligation; calling $m(\Gamma)$ ``minimum memory'' without naming $\Gamma$ and its validation contract is incomplete.

\paragraph{Dynamic authorization.} A live Player 0 may face moving resources, expiry, negative guards, checker upgrades and strategic conditions that determine which witnesses are produced. A static snapshot theorem cannot make all possible proofs available, turn an absence receipt into monotone evidence, or prove that recomputing the snapshot optimum at every turn is globally optimal.

\begin{problem}[Adaptive certified sufficiency]
Characterize explicitly versioned finite update rules that permit incremental repair of the sufficient-package frontier as authorizations, checked warrants and query requirements change. Specify which stored validation receipts can safely be reused and which require recomputation.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Tractable structured fragments]
Find substantive structural restrictions on witness incidence and packet dependencies admitting polynomial-time exact retention optimization, with full cost accounting. W1's unique-witness case is an elementary tractable fragment; depth one alone does not suffice by Theorem~\ref{thm:NP}.
\end{problem}

\begin{conjecture}[Empirically useful selective waking]
For some explicitly fixed, nontrivial singleton-ATP task distribution, a legal, fully charged, prevalidated policy selecting witnesses using a certified answer frontier achieves strictly greater expected held-out post-wake proof-search utility than at least one well-defined equal-access baseline under a matched total resource budget. This is experimental and algorithm-specific; it is not entailed by the finite theorems and does not claim universal optimality.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Conclusion}\label{gl-first-W2-s11}
The transition from W1 to W2 is from ``what is the ancestry of this selected certificate?'' to ``which certified witnesses ought we select to answer these named questions at a declared cost?'' W1 contributes typed admission, exact dependencies, guarded waking, and operational trait obligations. W2 elevates alternative, already checked warrants to first-class mathematical objects. Within that frozen model the results give an exact selection normal form, exponential branching, a compulsory core that can be insufficient, sound finite deletion and marginal extension, shared-evidence economies, a non-submodular optimized query cost, depth-one budget hardness, explicit trait thresholds, and a finite answer frontier with conditional revocation repair.

These results are \emph{conditional finite combinatorics}; neither a new general proof-search algorithm nor a measured proof-speedup follows. The remaining research tasks concern changed evidence, actual full-system charges, and blind empirical comparisons with equal-access baselines.
