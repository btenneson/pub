The Geometry of Mathematical Obstruction

Proof–Obstruction Duality, Certified Deductive Navigation, and MAPQUEST in the 24th Century

Research direction: Brian Tenneson
Mathematical drafting and analysis: ChatGPT (Picard research protocol)

The paper asks whether reasoning about what every successful proof must overcome can complement searching directly for a proof. In finite monotone deductive hypergraphs, it develops target cuts, blocker duality, replayable cost lower bounds, transport of obstruction certificates under knowledge growth, abstraction-sensitive bounds, and a finite repair-cover criterion. It connects these constructions to MAPQUEST while keeping the scope and verifier fixed.

Research status: 11-page Atlas-audited research edition 001v2. Finite-model tests and manuscript-level arguments; no deployed theorem prover, independent proof-kernel checking, or measured search speedup is claimed.

Who and what is Picard?

Picard is a named mathematical research and writing role operated through ChatGPT, not a separate human author or an independently deployed AI system. The work is directed by Brian Tenneson, Research Director. In this collaboration, Tenneson defines the scientific questions, contributes the underlying research manuscripts and mathematical frameworks, and sets the scope. ChatGPT working under the Picard research protocol develops and tests definitions, explores mathematical constructions, proposes lemmas and proofs, seeks counterexamples and obstruction certificates, connects proposals to prior literature, and prepares draft manuscripts for audit and revision.

The name Picard also refers to an earlier formal Picard Protocol in Tenneson's semi-ideal-computer (SIC) research, concerning a mathematical machine's ability to prove a specified property of its recoded copy. That formal protocol is a research object; calling the current ChatGPT collaboration Picard does not establish that it satisfies the idealized protocol, has consciousness, possesses autonomous authority, runs continuously, or has installed self-modifying code.

Picard's editorial distinction is between mathematical possibility and demonstrated capability: a manuscript-level argument is not the same thing as an independently checked proof; a finite test is not a general theorem; a proposed verifier or successor contract is not an operational deployment. These two articles explicitly preserve those distinctions, credit established results, and expose remaining research obligations.

                                                    Abstract
         A proof describes a sufficient collection of inference instances; an obstruction describes a condition
     that every successful collection must overcome. We develop this dual perspective for finite monotone
     deductive hypergraphs, where shared lemmas are charged once. Cut crossing, blocker duality, and a
     cut-based integer program recover established shortest-hyperpath and transversal mathematics. We
     use these foundations to develop a certificate-oriented approach to deductive navigation: rational
     cut prices give replayable lower bounds, surviving cuts transport information as verified knowledge
     grows, and an explicit forward relaxation permits lower bounds to transfer from a finite abstraction
     to a larger proof system. We prove a finite repair-cover criterion for independently composable edge
     additions and distinguish it from arbitrary operational revisions. An exhaustive small-model check
     accompanies the paper. The concluding sections specialize the mathematics to the MAPQUEST
     trilogy and then to a sovereign game, the “24th century.” The work is a mathematical research
     proposal with manuscript-level proofs, not an implemented theorem prover or a claim of speedup.

Scope and provenance. Sections 1–7 are independent of MAPQUEST and sovereign games. Sections
8–9 apply the results without modifying the three supplied source manuscripts. Known cut and blocker
results are identified as such; the certificate transport and revision interfaces are developed here as
research constructions, without a priority claim. The final target and its verifier are not changed by the
proposed applications.
Keywords: deductive hypergraph; obstruction; proof DAG; weighted cut; certificate; automated
theorem proving; sovereign revision.
Reading map. Sections 1–3 establish the deductive geometry and duality; Sections 4–7 develop cost
certificates, transport, complexity and discovery. Sections 8–9 specialize to MAPQUEST and sovereign
revision; Section 10 collects open obligations.

1    A finite deductive landscape
Let X be a finite set of formula identifiers (including any necessary context tags), B ⊆ X an initial set
of established formulas, T ∈ X a target, and E a finite set of individually identified inference instances.
An instance e consists of its premise set Pe ⊆ X, its single conclusion qe ∈ X, and a rational cost
ce ≥ 0. Distinct instances may have identical premise and conclusion sets. Any ordered premise list,
substitution, side condition or proof context needed for an actual certificate belongs to the instance’s
separate witness record. A zero-premise instance is allowed. In the abstract model all instances have
fixed legality once their premises are present. Context-sensitive, consumable, or nonmonotone systems
require a richer state model before the following results can be used.
    For A ⊆ E, define clA (B) by starting from B and repeatedly adjoining qe whenever e ∈ A and Pe is
contained in the current set. The process terminates because X is finite. It computes the least A-closed
superset of B. A derivation is a finite list of instances in which each premise is in B or is an earlier
conclusion. Thus T ∈ clA (B) if and only if A contains a derivation of T . The empty derivation is valid
when T ∈ B.
Definition 1.1 (Additive proof cost). The cost of a derivation is the sum of the costs of its executed
inference occurrences. Define
                                            {                          }
                                              ∑
                           JE (B, T ) = min     ce : A ⊆ E, T ∈ clA (B) ,
                                                e∈A

with the minimum of the empty feasible family interpreted as +∞.
    Every derivation determines a feasible support of cost no greater than its occurrence cost. Conversely,
closure produces a derivation from each feasible support using an instance at most once. Hence JE
is the exact minimum additive derivation cost. This identification relies on reusable knowledge and
nonnegative, fixed costs. It does not measure how long it takes to discover the derivation or check the
complete certificate. Every operational implementation must account for those costs separately.

Three views of discovery. Forward closure identifies what can be derived. Backward search proposes
possible dependencies of the target. An obstruction asks what every possible successful support must
accomplish. The third view provides constraints, not automatically a preferred proof. Its value lies in
the interaction with the first two.

3    Proof–obstruction duality
Definition 3.1. Let P be the inclusion-minimal subsets of E that derive T from B. Let O be the
inclusion-minimal subsets O ⊆ E for which T ∈

Theorem 3.2 (Blocker duality and cut identification).

                   O = Tr(P),        P = Tr(O),       O = min{δE (U ) : U ∈ U(B, T )}.
                                                              ⊆

Proof. Removal of O prevents success exactly when O intersects every minimal successful support.
Taking minimal sets proves the first identity. A set A is successful exactly when it hits every minimal
removal obstruction: if not successful, E \ A contains such an obstruction; if it misses one, it is contained
in the remaining unsuccessful catalogue. Minimality proves the second identity. Removing all crossing
instances of any cut prevents success by Theorem 2.2. Conversely, for any removal obstruction O,
closure under E \ O gives a cut whose crossings are contained in O. Taking minimal elements yields the
third identity.

    This includes the degenerate cases: an initially established target has P = {∅} and O = ∅; a target
unreachable even with all of E has P = ∅ and O = {∅}. No hitting set exists for a family containing
the empty set, and the unique minimal hitting set of an empty family is empty.
    The first two identities are classical hypergraph transversal duality, not a new combinatorial theorem
[2]. The deductive interpretation is useful because proof checking and closure provide complementary
oracles. It does not imply that all minimal proofs or obstructions can be listed efficiently. For example,
m independent choices between two parallel instances along a chain yield 2m minimal proofs with only
2m instances. Conversely, m disjoint two-edge paths from a seed to a target have 2m minimal removal
obstructions.

Proof. Feasible binary vectors correspond exactly to successful supports by Theorem 2.2. The objective

    The cut formulation and iterative addition of missing constraints have prior art in shortest-hyperpath
optimization [3]. For an exact finite optimizer, begin with any subset of cut constraints, solve the
restricted binary program to optimality, and compute closure of the selected support. If the target is
reached, the restricted optimum is also the full optimum. Otherwise add the cut complementary to
closure. This cut is violated by the current binary solution and therefore was not already imposed.
Repeat. At most |U(B, T )| new cuts can be added. Infeasibility of the restricted problem also certifies
infeasibility of the full problem, provided the solver’s conclusion has a trusted proof. No polynomial-time
bound follows; fractional separation is a different problem from binary closure separation.

Example 4.5 (Fractional certificates need not be exact). Take three requirements a, b, c, and selectors
uab , uac , ubc obtainable from a seed at unit cost. Each selector supplies its two named requirements by
zero-cost unary instances; a zero-cost binary conjunction chain derives T from all three. Any successful

1/2 and all zero-cost variables 1 satisfies every cut: if no zero-cost instance crosses a cut, backwards
closure along the zero-cost conjunction forces at least one requirement into the cut and then both its
selectors into the cut. Both corresponding selector instances cross. Thus the LP value is at most 3/2,
whereas the integer optimum is 2. A lower bound is not automatically a complete optimality certificate.


5    The transport of obstruction certificates
A static certificate can be expensive to discover. An intelligent navigator should ask which portions of
a previously established lower bound survive a change of search state or presentation. We develop three
transport results. Their claims are about the declared cost model, not about correctness of unverified
mathematical proposals.

Theorem 5.3 (Sound transfer from an optimistic abstraction). Consider a concrete monotone deductive
system with possibly infinite formula and instance sets Xc , Ec , and a finite abstract system Xa , Ea .
Suppose a map π : Xc → Xa sends concrete initial formulas into Ba and the concrete target to Ta . For
every concrete inference e, require an abstract derivation of π(qe ) from π(Pe ) of additive cost at most
the concrete inference charge ce . An empty abstract derivation is allowed. Then

                                       JEa (Ba , Ta ) ≤ JEc (Bc , Tc ),

where the concrete value is an infimum over finite derivations. Every checked abstract cut-price receipt
transfers as a lower bound. An abstract empty-cut certificate rules out every concrete proof covered by
the mapping contract.

Proof. Follow any finite concrete derivation in order. Initially the mapped seeds are present. At
each concrete step, the mapped premises are available by induction. Execute the stipulated abstract
derivation; its cost is at most the concrete charge. Previously established abstract formulas can be
reused. The resulting abstract derivation reaches Ta at no greater cost. Taking infima gives the
inequality. If no abstract derivation exists, a concrete derivation would contradict the simulation. The
price claim follows from Theorem 4.3.

    This transfer theorem is deliberately one-sided. Abstract inferences may overapproximate concrete
legality, abstract proof paths may be spurious, and the converse does not follow. Conversely, an
underapproximation formed by collecting only visited instances can produce a false global obstruction:
the omitted direct route s → T of cost 1 defeats a restricted catalogue containing only a route of cost
100. Merely increasing a Hilbert resolution does not establish the simulation hypothesis. In an infinite
formal system that hypothesis needs a symbolic proof over rule schemata and witnesses, not a sample
of observed candidates.


6    Computational complexity and the role of sharing
Theorem 6.1 (Hardness in an elementary fragment). For finite acyclic single-head deductive hypergraphs
of premise arity at most two and nonnegative integer costs, deciding whether JE (B, T ) ≤ k is NP-
complete.

Proof. A support is a polynomial-size witness: sum its weights and compute closure in polynomial time.
For NP-hardness reduce SET COVER. Given a nonempty universe {a1 , . . . , an }, subsets S1 , . . . , Sm
and cover bound k, start from seed s. For each j add s → uj of cost one. For each ai ∈ Sj add uj → ai
of cost zero. Use a fixed binary conjunction chain with zero costs to obtain T precisely when all ai are
established (for n = 1 use a1 as target). The graph is acyclic in the order seed, selectors, requirements,
conjunction stages. Every cover induces a successful support of its cardinality, and every successful
support contains paid selectors covering the universe. Thus the two decisions agree. The construction
is polynomial in the incidence description.

   Shortest-hyperpath hardness is already known even for acyclic singleton-head systems [4]; the
reduction above records a convenient explicit proof for the chosen fragment. Zero-premise axioms and
duplicate instances do not affect membership in NP.

Proposition 6.2 (Unary tractability and an exact finite-state algorithm). If every instance has arity
at most one, the minimum cost is an ordinary nonnegative shortest-path problem from a super-source
linked at zero cost to all seeds. Zero-premise instances are edges from the super-source at their stated
cost. More generally, an exact algorithm for arbitrary finite X runs shortest-path search on at most
2|X| knowledge states, using transitions S → S ∪ {qe } whenever Pe ⊆ S and qe ∈ / S, with cost ce .

Proof. In the unary case a target derivation contains an ancestor chain ending at a seed or a zero-premise
rule; selecting that chain never increases cost. Each graph path is a derivation. For the second assertion,
an edge is exactly an enabled knowledge-expanding inference; redundant occurrences may be deleted
from any proof without increasing cost. State paths and such derivations correspond. Every transition
strictly increases cardinality, so the state graph is acyclic and admits a dynamic program in increasing
cardinality.

    The second algorithm is exact but exponential, not a practical general ATP. In both cases cost is a
property of executed inferences under the finite model. Even a cheap optimal proof may be difficult to
discover in an effective infinite syntax.


7    From obstruction mathematics to a discovery method
The mathematics suggests a procedure in which forward closure, backward obligations and obstruction
receipts are complementary. A bounded discovery engine may propose a region U , verify its crossing
catalogue or its abstract simulation contract, assign cut prices and use the most expensive obligations
as targets for search. A successful new lemma changes B; Theorem 5.1 permits receipt reuse. A
proposed new representation must justify its proof translations and cost relation; Theorem 5.3 permits
lower-bound transfer only in the direction stated. A proposed macro needs an expanded proof certificate
and changes the charged catalogue only when its operational availability is established.

Budget-safe pruning. Given a remaining budget b, if a valid lower bound on all possible continuations
exceeds b, the declared additive-cost task cannot finish within that budget. This conclusion is not a
proof of ¬T . A lower bound on only a selected catalogue cannot prune paths outside it. If search,
checking, migration and other charges are all nonnegative and the additive inference model is a lower
bound on the actual charged executions, the same certificate safely excludes a fully charged budget.
Otherwise it remains advisory.

Joint targets. For a finite target family D, replace cuts containing T by cuts intersecting D. A
support derives every member of D exactly when it hits every such cut: apply the single-target result
to each target. A joint proof cost can be smaller than the sum of individual costs because common
lemmas are reusable. This provides a rigorous way to pose multi-target navigation rather than treating
lemma popularity as mathematical value. It does not establish that joint search is computationally
cheaper after discovery overhead.

A small computational check. The companion obstruction checks.py uses the Python standard
library and seed 2400. It generates 160 finite random systems with 2–6 vertices, one seed, 1–8 indexed
instances, arity 0–2, and costs 0–4. Cycles, duplicates and zero-premise instances are permitted. It
exhaustively enumerates every inference subset and target cut in each sample, computes closure, and
compares cut feasibility, minimal proof supports and minimal removal obstructions. It also checks
the repair-cover identity in Section 9 for three randomly generated packages per model. The actual
execution returned:
     160 models; 11306 inference-subset checks;
     1280 gift-subset checks; all passed.

8     Specialization to the MAPQUEST trilogy
The MAPQUEST trilogy [5, 6, 7] supplies an addressable search architecture rather than a new
logical calculus. Paper I uses rule-tagged premise-coordinate components, finite Hilbert orderings and
enumerable instantiation witnesses. Paper II adds a protected fair queue, target cones and waiting
frames indexed by missing premises. Paper III requires full-packet verification after extraction of a
finite provenance DAG. We use the November 8 v2 controlling amendments: numerical proximity is not
proof distance; arity is coordinate count rather than the topological dimension of a rational point space;
fair service must actually complete; local acceptance alone is not global certificate acceptance.

An obstruction-directed guided lane. Keep the ordinary Hilbert-fair service and exact rule/witness
identifiers intact. At bounded guided turns, a new procedure may:

    1. Build a finite, version-tagged candidate catalogue or an explicitly justified optimistic abstraction.
       Distinguish these two models in every receipt.

    2. Compute a backward cone from the target and forward closure from available verified formulas.
       Generate cuts from unsuccessful candidate supports; use waiting frames to record missing premises.

    3. Produce cut-price receipts, or use restricted-catalogue scores as heuristics only. Rank a proposed
       action by its cost plus a valid remaining-cost estimate, alongside declared heuristic advice.

    4. Upon an accepted new formula, update waiting frames and transport surviving cut prices. On
       target acquisition assemble and replay the complete scoped proof packet under the original verifier.

A concrete action ranking is c(e) + L(B ∪ {qe }) for an enabled candidate with a verified lower-bound
function L; its value is a lower bound on the best completion through that immediate action in the
additive model. Ranking does not prove that the chosen action has the shortest continuation. When
a complete proof of cost U has been found, prune only the continuation branch conditioned on that
immediate action if its lower bound (plus any already incurred path cost) exceeds U ; equality may be
pruned if no alternative optimum is needed. This does not justify removing the inference from the
global catalogue: it may be useful later after other premises or shared knowledge have been established.
Recompute or transport bounds when the state or catalogue changes.

Proposition 8.1 (Protected positive completeness). Suppose the v2 MAPQUEST hypotheses hold:
effective complete candidate addressing including axiom instances; terminating acceptance-complete
local verification; durable scoped provenance; a monotone BANK; and actual nonstarving finite service
of eligible proof-required instances. Also suppose full-packet acceptance is ensured by the declared
compositionality or checked replay contract, and packet extraction, replay and receipt emission for a
reached target receive terminating, nonstarving service. A valid finite proof must be expressible in the
addressed interface with its full context. If obstruction analysis is bounded or preemptible and cannot
remove required fair obligations, then the extended unbounded search retains positive completeness and
reports proof success only after full acceptance.

Proof. For a finite proof, process its nodes in topological order. Axioms and hypotheses are seeds
or eventually generated by fair service. After all premises of a node are available, its legal instance
is eventually serviced and accepted; its provenance is retained. Finite induction establishes eventual
acquisition of the target. The full acceptance hypothesis gives the required final packet. The obstruction
lane cannot invalidate this induction under the stated protection. In a finite stopped run without an
accepted packet, the result remains unknown (U).

    This is a preservation result, not a proof of acceleration. The principal experiment compares the
existing two-queue method with the additional obstruction-guided lane, at matched targets, seeds,
verifier, initial BANK and total charged budget. Record candidate visits, cut computation, verification,
peak memory, certificate size, actual proof success and total work. A third control should spend the
same guidance budget on alternative ordinary search to isolate the cost of certificate generation. Tests
should hold out target solutions and prevent preloaded proof leakage. The hypothesis that obstruction
receipts improve discovery is open.


9    Sovereign navigation in the 24th century
Here “24th century” denotes an underlying game involving a sovereign. The previous sections require
no sovereign, no Player Zero and no architectural intervention. The following is a specialization, not
an axiom of deductive geometry. Use a versioned operational controller, frozen mathematical target
and verifier, and the cumulative admission and exercise distinction of the sovereign basin-escape and
CDF-C frameworks [8, 9].

Definition 9.1 (Monotone repair catalogue). Fix a finite envelope E + on X and a baseline E0 ⊆ E + .
Let G be a finite collection of packages, each adding a fixed set Dg ⊆ E + of instances when legally
exercised. Assume, for this restricted model, that each selected package can be jointly admitted and
executed in an authorized finite order, that additions persist, and that no interaction changes other
instances, initial knowledge, or the receipt semantics. Every exposed inference is already sound for the
frozen object calculus; closure witnesses can be executed and replayed under the unchanged verifier.
For the unconstrained set-cover optimization assume every subset of packages is jointly executable
(otherwise optimize over the separately specified feasible family). These are hypotheses, not consequences
of individual consistency.

Theorem 9.2 (Exact repair-cover criterion).⋃Let M = {U ∈ U(B, T ) : δE0 (U ) = ∅} be the old
un-crossed cuts. For H ⊆ G, write EH = E0 ∪ g∈H Dg . Then

                       T ∈ clEH (B) ⇐⇒ ∀U ∈ M ∃g ∈ H : Dg ∩ δE + (U ) ̸= ∅.

Proof. Every cut not in M has a persistent baseline crossing. A cut in M has a crossing in EH exactly
when some selected package supplies one. Apply Theorem 2.2.

    When package charges are additive and nonnegative, and every subset is jointly executable, minimiz-
ing the acquisition cost of a successful package collection is a set-cover problem over the old un-crossed
cuts, with package g covering the cuts it crosses. This optimizes acquisition only; total settlement
requires including exercise, proof, verification and all construction costs. An empty cut in the whole
envelope proves the insufficiency of that envelope. The theorem is nonvacuous even when the baseline
cannot derive the target.

Why the general sovereign case is different. Gifts may remove permissions, revise representation,
require an ordered support-indexed grammar or change which later gift is admissible. Cumulative
consistency, actual authorized exercise, lifecycle, unique sovereign office, provenance and all resource
costs must be checked for the complete history. Set cover does not model those dependencies. The
actual object-game transition system, enclosing game of admission and exercise, and target receipt
must be distinguished. A full state-space construction, with version and history included, can use a
settlement-game certificate instead of the monotone formula closure model. Where adversarial successors
exist, a single repaired path is not a winning strategy; a forced result needs all-outcome coverage. No
unrestricted syntactic relative-consistency theorem or general consistency oracle is assumed.

A proposed discovery cycle. A mathematical navigator first obtains a scoped obstruction, then
imagines a capability that crosses it, then tests whether the new capability is useful in a complete
candidate continuation. A candidate revision is not admitted merely because it makes a lower bound
smaller. It needs a typed contract and an external cumulative-consistency and preservation receipt,
legal authorization and actual execution. The final proof needs the same complete-packet acceptance as
before. In this sense a navigator can investigate changes to its own roads without gaining the authority
to declare a theorem true.


10     Conclusion and research obligations
The general contribution is a deductive interpretation and certificate-oriented development of proof–
obstruction duality. It connects sufficient supports, unavoidable crossings, lower-bound accounting,
knowledge growth and transfer through optimistic abstractions. In a restricted finite repair model it
also identifies which combinations of operational capabilities suffice to remove every baseline obstruction.
None of these results requires sovereignty as a starting assumption.
    The principal open problems are to find compact abstraction contracts for useful infinite rule families;
determine tractable cut-price and certificate-reuse subclasses; measure the net value of obstruction-
guided search against fair and heuristic comparators; and generalize the repair criterion to ordered,
interacting, adversarial sovereign histories. The MAPQUEST extension is the first intended application,
not a prerequisite of the mathematics.

original edition reports an executed finite test; the exact original script was not supplied for this audit.
The v2 audit also independently checks the finite identities with a separate reproducible script (Section
