\begin{abstract}
This paper rewires singleton enumeration around the AMLD+0 architecture.  The
starting observation remains that a single designated mathematical query can
hide an enormous search space.  The AMLD+0 viewpoint adds a second distinction:
a singleton \emph{query} need not induce a singleton \emph{settlement region}.
A fixed conjecture $T$ may be investigated by several verifier-gated settlement
roles---proof, refutation, dependence, independence, and contradiction---while
a distinguished Player 0 decides which enabled role receives the next service
turn.  Player 0 does not choose the selected role's internal move and cannot
alter verifier acceptance.  Ordinary AMLD search is recovered when Player 0
replays or delegates to the declared baseline scheduler, so the present
rewiring preserves the original search theory as a passive subcase.

Within this architecture, the Ocean, Universal Proof Horizon, password search,
and structured-versus-opaque singleton distinction acquire a controller-level
interpretation.  Local navigators choose expansions inside channels; Player 0
allocates computational attention among channels; the shared verifier-governed
BANK transmits accepted discoveries; and the verifier remains the fixed trust
boundary.  The password model then becomes a control case proving that
scheduling can exploit information but cannot create information absent from
the observation interface.  The paper retains the original horizon,
information-gap, CNF-pruning, execution-separation, and fair-isomorphism
results, and adds AMLD+0 consequences concerning passive recovery, optional
control, typed settlement horizons, and the separation of local navigation
from global scheduling.
\end{abstract}

\section{The AMLD+0 version of the central question}

The original singleton-enumeration question begins with a one-element target
set
\[
\Tset=\{t\},
\qquad |
\Tset|=1,
\]
and observes that this says almost nothing about the number of legal states,
legal moves, candidate histories, or search decisions surrounding $t$.
That observation remains intact.  AMLD+0 adds a second layer.

Suppose the mathematical object of study is one conjecture $T$.  The query is
singleton in the literal sense that the run is organized around one designated
formula.  But AMLD+0 does not force all successful computation to terminate in
one undifferentiated state.  It contains typed settlement roles
\[
P,R,D,I,C,
\]
sharing a verifier-governed BANK and a fixed verifier.  A successful run may
therefore terminate through one of several certificate-backed settlement
channels, depending on the declared formal experiment.

This gives the first principle of the rewired paper.

\begin{principle}[Singleton Query / Typed Settlement Principle]
A singleton mathematical query does not imply a singleton operational
settlement region.  In AMLD+0, one fixed target $T$ may have several typed,
verifier-gated modes of certified settlement, even though the object-level
query itself remains unique.
\end{principle}

Thus at least three cardinalities must be kept separate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the number of designated queries, which here is one;
    \item the number of typed settlement regions admitted by the architecture;
    \item the number of distinct certificates or histories capable of reaching
    any such region.
\end{enumerate}

The complexity of a run may be large because of the third item, because of the
second, or because the controller must decide how to allocate work among
several search processes before any certificate is available.

\section{AMLD+0 as the controller architecture}

We use the foundational AMLD+0 separation between settlement-agent dynamics,
shared verified memory, certification, and scheduling \cite{tenneson-amldplus0}.
Fix a formal system $F$, admitted hypotheses $\Gamma$, a target $T$, a shared
BANK $B$, a verifier $V$, a declared baseline scheduler $\sigma_0$, and five
settlement roles $P,R,D,I,C$.

\begin{definition}[AMLD+0 singleton-settlement state]
A global AMLD+0 state is represented schematically by
\[
X=
(B,S_P,S_R,S_D,S_I,S_C,H_0),
\]
where $B$ is the trusted BANK, each $S_J$ is the internal search state of role
$J\in\{P,R,D,I,C\}$, and $H_0$ is the controller-visible history and auxiliary
state available to Player 0.  The verifier $V$ and baseline scheduler
$\sigma_0$ are fixed components of the declared experiment rather than
mutable coordinates of the ordinary run.
\end{definition}

For a search-oriented channel one may have, for example,
\[
S_J=(D_J,F_J,X_J,P_J,\Sigma_J,\ldots),
\]
with discovered states, frontier, expanded states, predecessor/certificate
information, and heuristic or reconnaissance data.  These are \emph{local}
coordinates.  They belong to the settlement agent, not to Player 0.

\begin{definition}[Player-Zero service event]
At a scheduling event, Player 0 selects
\[
J\in\{P,R,D,I,C,\varepsilon\}.
\]
If $J$ is an enabled settlement role, that role receives the next service turn
and chooses its own legal internal action.  If Player 0 selects the passive
symbol $\varepsilon$, the next service decision is delegated to the declared
baseline scheduler $\sigma_0$.  Player 0 does not select the chosen role's
internal move and cannot change verifier acceptance.
\end{definition}

The distinction can be summarized as
\[
\boxed{
P_0\text{ chooses the channel;}\qquad
J\text{ chooses the move;}\qquad
V\text{ judges the certificate.}
}
\]

This is the principal rewiring of the original higher-level search game.  In
the earlier presentation, the central search decision could be written
\[
\operatorname{Expand}(v),\qquad v\in F.
\]
In AMLD+0 that is ordinarily a \emph{local agent action}.  Player 0 acts one
level above it by deciding which settlement process is serviced next.

\section{The Ocean remains a singleton GBG inside AMLD+0}

Consider a finite directed graph
\[
G=(V,E)
\]
with distinguished source $s$ and target $t$.  An edge $u\to v$ represents one
legal inference step.  As before, this is a one-player generalized board game
in the sense of the GBG framework \cite{tenneson-gbg}.

\begin{definition}[Ocean GBG]
The Ocean generalized board game has game states $V$, initial state $s$, legal
moves given by $E$, and singleton winning set
\[
\Wset=\{t\}.
\]
A play is a directed path beginning at $s$, and a winning play reaches $t$.
\end{definition}

Under unit edge cost the intrinsic winning horizon is
\[
L^*=d_G(s,t).
\]
Nothing about AMLD+0 changes this local graph fact.  The important change is
where the Ocean is placed in the architecture.  An Ocean may be the local
search geometry of one settlement role, while other roles explore different
certificate spaces or metatheoretic obligations in parallel.

Thus the statement
\[
L^*=d_G(s,t)
\]
may describe the intrinsic horizon of one channel without describing the total
AMLD+0 cost needed to discover, select, verify, and exploit that route.

\begin{remark}[Local horizon versus controller cost]
A shortest route inside $S_P$ is not automatically a shortest global AMLD+0
run.  Global cost can include service given to other channels, verifier work,
BANK operations, reconnaissance, controller computation, and any declared
fallback schedule.  Conversely, another channel may produce a certificate or
BANK deposit that makes the globally cheapest settlement route different from
the route initially visible to $P$.
\end{remark}

\section{Universal Proof Horizon inside a typed settlement architecture}

The Universal Proof-Horizon viewpoint begins with an effective proof system, a
mechanically checkable proof relation, and a computable proof-cost measure
\cite{tenneson-uph}.  One may enumerate candidate proofs in increasing cost and
return the first valid proof of the target.  If a proof exists, this identifies
a minimum-cost proof relative to the declared presentation and cost measure;
if no proof exists, naive enumeration need not terminate.

For a fixed theorem $T$, let
\[
\Wset_T=\{\pi:\pi\text{ verifies as a proof of }T\}.
\]
The basic singleton lesson is still
\[
\text{one desired theorem}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{small candidate-proof space}.
\]

AMLD+0 refines this lesson.  The Prover's proof horizon is only one possible
route to a certified report.  Write
\[
\Sett_J\subseteq\mathcal X
\]
for the global AMLD+0 states in which the verifier has accepted a settlement
certificate emitted through channel $J$.  Without fixing a more detailed
settlement taxonomy, define the positive settlement region by
\[
\Sett_{+0}:=
\bigcup_{J\in\{P,R,D,I,C\}}\Sett_J.
\]

When a directed accepted-state metric is declared, one may define channel
horizons
\[
H_J(X)=d(X,\Sett_J)
\]
and the intrinsic typed-settlement horizon
\[
H_{+0}(X)=d(X,\Sett_{+0})
          =\min_J H_J(X),
\]
whenever these distances are finite and measured in the same declared unit.
This quantity is geometric: it describes the cheapest accepted route in the
full state graph.  It does not imply that Player 0 knows which $J$ attains the
minimum.

\section{Passive rewiring: the old paper is a subcase}

The AMLD+0 architecture was designed so that ordinary AMLD is recovered when
Player 0 makes no adaptive scheduling intervention
\cite{tenneson-amldplus0}.  Specializing that fact to singleton search yields
the following.

\begin{theorem}[Singleton Passive-Rewiring Theorem]
Let $\mathcal A$ be an ordinary AMLD run on a fixed singleton query $T$, with
settlement roles $P,R,D,I,C$, shared BANK $B$, verifier $V$, and baseline
scheduler $\sigma_0$.  There is an AMLD+0 extension $\mathcal A^{+0}$ such
that the projection onto the ordinary AMLD coordinates reproduces the run of
$\mathcal A$ whenever Player 0 either
\begin{enumerate}
    \item selects the same enabled role that $\sigma_0$ would select, or
    \item selects $\varepsilon$ and delegates the service decision to
    $\sigma_0$.
\end{enumerate}
For stochastic or nondeterministic agents, exact trajectory recovery is
understood relative to the same fixed random or choice trace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Adjoin Player 0 without changing any local agent transition rule, BANK update
rule, verifier rule, or initial ordinary AMLD state.  Induct on service events.
If the projected ordinary states agree immediately before event $n$, then
scheduler replay or silent delegation selects the same role as $\sigma_0$.
That role receives the same local state and, relative to the same random or
choice trace when necessary, makes the same internal move.  The same verifier
judges the same proposed deposits or settlement certificates.  Hence the
projected state agrees after event $n$.  The induction holds for every finite
prefix.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conservative reinterpretation of the original paper]
Every result in the original singleton-enumeration analysis that depends only
on the ordinary search trajectory remains available as the passive AMLD+0
baseline.  Active Player-Zero control is therefore an additional experimental
variable rather than a replacement of the earlier theory.
\end{corollary}

This is important methodologically.  The AMLD+0 rewrite does not obtain new
results by silently changing the verifier, proof system, or local search
rules.  It adds a controller coordinate whose effect can be compared directly
with a declared baseline.

\section{The higher-level game is now a scheduling game}

A local search role may still maintain
\[
S_J=(D_J,F_J,X_J,P_J,\Sigma_J,\ldots)
\]
and may still choose an action such as
\[
\operatorname{Expand}_J(v),\qquad v\in F_J.
\]
The controller-level game, however, has actions of the form
\[
\operatorname{Serve}(J)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\varepsilon.
\]

This separates two optimization problems that the original paper treated in a
single navigation layer:
\begin{align*}
\text{local navigation: } &
\text{which legal action should channel }J\text{ take?}\\
\text{global scheduling: } &
\text{which enabled channel should receive the next service turn?}
\end{align*}

The separation matters because a locally excellent navigator can be globally
starved, while a globally intelligent scheduler cannot repair a channel whose
local policy ignores all useful structure.

\begin{principle}[Two-Level Navigation Principle]
AMLD+0 search quality is jointly controlled by local settlement-agent policies
and the Player-Zero service policy.  Neither layer in general determines the
other.
\end{principle}

\section{The shared BANK changes the geometry}

The BANK makes the several search channels more than an unrelated portfolio.
Verifier-authorized intermediate results can become shared resources.  If
accepted deposits are support-safe and conservative relative to the declared
formal system, they can change the \emph{explicit} search landscape without
silently enlarging theoremhood.

Suppose channel $J$ deposits a verified lemma $\lambda$ into $B$.  Another
channel $K$ may then obtain a shorter local route because $\lambda$ is now an
available trusted premise.  In controller language, service allocated to $J$
can therefore have option value even when $J$ never produces the final
settlement certificate.

This leads to an AMLD+0 version of the Ocean metaphor: Player 0 is not merely
choosing among five independent boats.  It is allocating expeditions whose
verified discoveries can redraw the shared navigational chart.

\begin{remark}[No verifier shortcut]
A BANK deposit changes what is explicitly available to search only after the
verifier authorizes it under the declared rules.  Player 0 cannot promote an
unverified formula to trusted status merely because doing so would shorten an
estimated horizon.
\end{remark}

\section{Password space as an AMLD+0 control case}

Password search is useful because it isolates a case where a singleton target
is embedded in a large candidate space that is almost empty in navigational signal.
Let $\A$ be an alphabet of size $k$, let passwords have length $m$, and let
\[
\Omega=\A^m.
\]
Let the hidden accepted password be $W\in\Omega$.

A rule-complete full state may be represented by
\[
x=(W,G,U,R),
\]
where $G$ is the set of tested candidates, $U=\Omega\setminus G$, and $R$ is
the response transcript.  The searcher observes only
\[
O(W,G,U,R)=(G,U,R).
\]
A legal move is $\operatorname{Guess}(q)$ for $q\in U$.

\begin{definition}[Observation-admissible policy]
A policy is $O$-admissible when its action depends only on its own randomness
and the history of available observations and actions, not on hidden
coordinates of the full state.
\end{definition}

The point of importing this model into AMLD+0 is not that password guessing
literally requires the five metalogical channels.  Rather, it provides a
clean theorem about the limits of scheduling: duplicating or rescheduling
searchers cannot manufacture a directional signal that the interface never
reveals.

\begin{theorem}[Player-Zero Noncreation of Information]
Let $W$ be uniformly distributed on a candidate set $\Omega$ of size $N$.
Suppose every enabled worker receives only equality feedback---``correct'' or
``incorrect''---for complete guesses, and the federation enforces global
nonrepetition: once any candidate has been tested, no worker tests it again.
Player 0 observes only the combined legal transcript and worker/controller
history and has no side channel, hidden advice, or access to $W$.  Then any
AMLD+0-style schedule together with any collection of such workers induces,
conditional on all controller and worker randomness, an ordering of the $N$
candidates.
Consequently the rank of $W$ in the total sequence of distinct tested
candidates is uniform on $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix all controller and worker randomness.  Until success, every legal response
is identical: ``incorrect.''  Thus the entire deterministic federation,
including Player 0's scheduling decisions, chooses a first untested candidate,
then a second, and so on.  Because global nonrepetition is enforced, these
choices form an ordering of $\Omega$.  Uniform $W$ has uniform rank in any fixed
ordering.  Averaging over the previously fixed randomness does not change the
rank distribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scheduling can exploit information but cannot create it]
In the equality-only opaque password model, adding Player 0, adding more
workers, or adaptively reallocating service cannot improve the information
content of the observation stream.  Any improvement must come from legitimate
prior information, a richer observation map, additional legal queries, or a
change in the problem itself.
\end{corollary}

This is the sharp conceptual boundary for active AMLD+0.  Scheduling is a
control resource, not an oracle.

\section{Structured and opaque singleton search}

The password control case motivates the same distinction as the original
paper, now stated relative to both local policies and Player 0.

\begin{definition}[Structured singleton search]
A singleton search problem is \emph{structured} when the observations
available to the search architecture contain features correlated with
accessibility, distance, expected settlement cost, or other legitimate
progress information toward certified settlement.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Opaque singleton search]
A singleton search problem is \emph{opaque} when candidates that remain
untested are indistinguishable with respect to all information legally
available to the search architecture, except through direct testing.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[AMLD+0 Singleton Navigation Principle]
A singleton query does not prevent intelligent navigation or intelligent
scheduling.  What matters is whether the observation history contains
information correlated with progress and whether the local agents and Player 0
can exploit that information without crossing the verifier trust boundary.
\end{principle}

This creates a useful four-way distinction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the query may be singleton;
    \item the intrinsic certificate space may be huge;
    \item the observation geometry may be structured or opaque;
    \item the controller may allocate work well or poorly relative to the
    structure that is actually observable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{DATA MIND under AMLD+0}

The original paper described DATA MIND abstractly as choosing
\[
 v\in F
\]
so as to maximize expected progress toward the unique target.  Under AMLD+0,
that description belongs naturally to a \emph{local} settlement agent or to a
navigation module used by such an agent.

For example, a local value estimate may be written
\[
Q_J(v)\approx
\text{estimated value of expanding }v\text{ inside channel }J.
\]
Player 0, by contrast, may maintain a service value
\[
Q_0(J\mid H_0)\approx
\text{estimated value of giving the next service turn to }J.
\]
These are different objects.  The first ranks actions within a channel.  The
second ranks channels given the current shared history.

A clean experimental decomposition is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{local candidate ordering}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{verifier/BANK updates}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{Player-Zero service allocation}
}
\]
with feedback in the opposite direction through the newly observed shared
state.

Neither layer changes the formal verifier.  Heuristics change the order in
which legal work is attempted; the verifier alone determines whether a
proposed certificate or BANK addition becomes trusted.

\section{Player-Zero control value and the passive baseline}

Because $\varepsilon$ delegates to the baseline scheduler, active control can
be compared against a precisely defined passive run.  Let
\[
C_{\sigma_0}(X)
\]
be expected declared settlement cost from state $X$ under the baseline
scheduler, and let $C_{\pi_0}(X)$ be the corresponding cost under a Player-Zero
policy $\pi_0$, with the same local agents, verifier, BANK rules, and cost
accounting.

Define the expected controller gain
\[
G_0(X;\pi_0)
:=C_{\sigma_0}(X)-C_{\pi_0}(X).
\]
Positive $G_0$ means the intervention lowers expected declared work; negative
$G_0$ means it raises expected declared work relative to baseline.

\begin{proposition}[Optional-Control Dominance]
Assume that Player 0's policy class contains the passive policy that selects
$\varepsilon$ at every scheduling event, and that using this passive action
introduces no extra charged cost beyond the baseline mechanism it invokes.
Then
\[
\inf_{\pi_0} C_{\pi_0}(X)\le C_{\sigma_0}(X).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The always-$\varepsilon$ policy belongs to the admissible Player-Zero policy
class and reproduces the baseline cost by hypothesis.  The infimum over a set
containing that policy cannot exceed its value.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This proposition is an option-value statement, not a speedup theorem.  An
arbitrary active Player-Zero policy may be worse than baseline.  The theorem
says only that a controller optimization problem with costless fallback does
not have a worse \emph{best achievable} value than the fallback it contains.
\end{remark}

\section{Information-constrained Bellman gap for opaque passwords}

Let $\Omega$ contain $N\ge 1$ candidate passwords, let $W$ be uniformly
distributed on $\Omega$, and use the equality-only observation model above.
Let $\Pi_O$ denote the class of policies that are $O$-admissible, nonrepeating
on failed queries, and continue until success.  For $\pi\in\Pi_O$, let
$T_\pi$ be its number of guesses.  For every full
state the intrinsic full-information Bellman cost is one guess.

\begin{theorem}[Information-Constrained Bellman Gap]
For the equality-only password game, every admissible possibly randomized
nonrepeating policy $\pi$ satisfies, for $0\le k\le N$,
\[
\Pr(T_\pi\le k)=\frac{k}{N}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E[T_\pi]=\frac{N+1}{2}.
\]
If
\[
V^{\mathrm{id},\mu}_{\mathrm{Bayes}}=1
\]
is the full-observation expected settlement value and
\[
V^{O,\mu}_{\mathrm{Bayes}}
:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_O}\mathbb E[T_\pi],
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{V^{O,\mu}_{\mathrm{Bayes}}}
     {V^{\mathrm{id},\mu}_{\mathrm{Bayes}}}
=\frac{N+1}{2}.}
\]
For $N=2^n$ the information-constrained Bellman gap is exponential in $n$.
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
Condition on the internal randomness of the policy.  Until success every
observed answer is ``incorrect,'' so a deterministic nonrepeating realization
specifies an ordering $q_1,\ldots,q_N$ of the candidates.  Since $W$ is uniform,
its rank in that ordering is uniform on $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.  Therefore
\[
\Pr(T_\pi\le k)=\frac{k}{N},
\qquad
\mathbb E[T_\pi]=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N j=\frac{N+1}{2}.
\]
Averaging over policy randomness leaves these identities unchanged.  A
full-information controller guesses $W$ immediately, so the stated ratio
follows.
\end{proof}

From the AMLD+0 viewpoint, the theorem applies to the federation as a whole
whenever the combined controller/worker observation is still equality-only.
The controller can alter service order but not the information-theoretic rank
symmetry.

\section{Opaque singleton search under scheduling}

\begin{theorem}[Opaque Singleton No-Polynomial-Settlement]
Let $W$ be a uniformly random $n$-bit password and suppose the only feedback
from a guess is equality with $W$.  If an $O$-admissible target-blind policy makes at
most $q(n)$ distinct guesses, then
\[
\Pr(\text{settlement within }q(n)\text{ guesses})
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{q(n)}{2^n}\right\}.
\]
Equality holds when the policy tests exactly
$\min\{q(n),2^n\}$ distinct candidates unless success occurs earlier.
Consequently, for every polynomial $q$,
\[
\Pr(\text{settlement within }q(n)\text{ guesses})\to 0.
\]
Thus the uniform opaque-password family has no high-probability polynomial
settlement policy in the observation-constrained equality-query model.
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
A policy using at most $q(n)$ guesses tests at most
$\min\{q(n),2^n\}$ distinct candidates.  Conditional on any fixed realization
of its randomness, those candidates occupy at most that many positions in the
induced ordering.  Uniform $W$ lies among them with probability at most
\[
\min\left\{1,\frac{q(n)}{2^n}\right\}.
\]
If the policy continues through exactly that many distinct candidates unless
success occurs earlier, the bound is attained.  For polynomial $q$,
$q(n)/2^n\to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[AMLD+0 opaque-control corollary]
If every AMLD+0 worker and Player 0 are restricted to the same equality-only
transcript and all charged candidate tests are counted globally, then merely
splitting the work among channels or workers does not evade the theorem.  A
polynomial global test budget still covers only polynomially many members of a
$2^n$-element candidate set.
\end{corollary}

This gives a strong negative control for controller experiments.  If active
Player 0 performs substantially better on a singleton benchmark, the gain must
be traced to legitimate structure, prior information, richer observations,
shared certified discoveries, or a change in the allowed action set---not to
scheduling in the abstract.

\section{Constraint revelation in CNF and 3-CNF}

Password space becomes more informative when compared with SAT.  A known CNF
formula exposes constraints before a complete assignment is tested.  Partial
assignments may falsify clauses and prune the prefix tree.

Fix a variable ordering $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and let $\varphi$ be a satisfiable CNF
formula.  Let $P_k(\varphi)$ be the assignments to $x_1,\ldots,x_k$ that have
not yet falsified any clause, let
\[
W_k:=|P_k(\varphi)|,
\]
and define
\[
J_k:=\log_2\!\left(\frac{2^k}{W_k}\right)
    =k-\log_2 W_k.
\]

\begin{theorem}[CNF Prefix-Pruning Monotonicity]
If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, then
\[
W_0=1,\qquad 1\le W_k\le 2^k,\qquad W_{k+1}\le 2W_k,
\]
and
\[
W_n=\#\operatorname{SAT}(\varphi).
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
0=J_0\le J_1\le\cdots\le J_n
=n-\log_2\#\operatorname{SAT}(\varphi).}
\]
If the satisfying assignment is unique, $J_n=n$.  The statement applies in
particular to satisfiable and uniquely satisfiable 3-CNF formulas.
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
The empty assignment is the unique length-zero prefix, so $W_0=1$.
Restricting any satisfying assignment to the first $k$ variables gives a
surviving prefix, so $W_k\ge1$, while trivially $W_k\le2^k$.  Each surviving
$k$-prefix has at most two extensions, hence $W_{k+1}\le2W_k$.  At depth $n$,
a complete assignment survives exactly when it satisfies $\varphi$, so
$W_n=\#\operatorname{SAT}(\varphi)$.  Finally,
\[
J_{k+1}-J_k
=1-\log_2\!\left(\frac{W_{k+1}}{W_k}\right)\ge0.
\]
The endpoint formula and uniquely satisfiable case follow.
\end{proof}

For AMLD+0, $J_k$ is an example of a legitimate observable feature that may
inform either local action choice, global service allocation, or both.  The
theorem does not imply that SAT becomes easy; it proves only that SAT may
reveal progressively asymmetric information that the ideal password oracle
withholds.

\section{Four equivalent local horizons}

The Ocean construction, GBGs, the Universal Proof-Horizon viewpoint, and
Bellman settlement geometry all contain a notion of distance to success.  For
a unit-cost one-player proof game they coincide.

\begin{theorem}[Singleton GBG--Proof-Horizon--Bellman Equivalence]
Let $G=(V,E)$ be an effectively generated, locally finite, directed one-player
GBG with initial state $s$ and singleton winning set $\{t\}$.  Suppose each
legal move has unit cost and the reachable GBG transition system is
step-for-step identical to the associated sequential proof system.  If $t$ is
reachable from $s$, let $L^*$ be the minimum winning match length, $h(t)$ the
minimum number of inference steps in a proof of $t$, $V(s)$ the Bellman
settlement value, and $d_G(s,t)$ the directed graph distance.  Then
\[
\boxed{L^*=h(t)=V(s)=d_G(s,t).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
The step-for-step identity makes legal matches from $s$ to $t$ exactly the
admissible sequential proof paths.  Thus minimum match length equals minimum
proof length, and both equal directed graph distance.  Unit action costs make
the cost of a settling trajectory equal to its edge count, so the Bellman
value equals the same distance.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[AMLD+0 interpretation]
The equality is a \emph{local intrinsic invariant}.  It can identify the
horizon of a particular proof channel or encoded proof game.  It does not
collapse the controller-level quantities
\[
H_{+0}(X),\qquad C_{\pi_0}(X),\qquad C_{\sigma_0}(X)
\]
into the same number.  Typed alternatives, BANK effects, service allocation,
and discovery overhead remain additional coordinates.
\end{remark}

\section{Shortest certificates do not control AMLD+0 execution cost}

\begin{definition}[Irrelevant expansion]
In a singleton-target directed search graph with target $t$, an expansion of
$v$ is \emph{irrelevant} if
\[
d_G(v,t)=\infty,
\]
so no continuation from $v$ can contribute to a certificate reaching $t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Horizon--Execution Separation]
For every $L\ge1$ and $M\ge1$, there exists a finite singleton-target
one-player GBG with an exact search policy $\pi$ such that
\[
L^*=V(s)=L,
\]
the returned certificate has exactly $L$ legal moves, but $\pi$ performs at
least $M$ irrelevant state expansions before completing it.  Thus certificate
stretch
\[
\frac{\text{returned certificate length}}{L^*}=1
\]
does not bound search overhead.
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
Construct a unique successful path
\[
s=v_0\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_L=t
\]
and at least $M$ reachable decoy states that do not lead to $t$.  Give every
edge unit cost.  The shortest successful path has length $L$.  Let $\pi$
expand the $M$ decoys first and then the successful path.  Its predecessor map
still reconstructs an optimal $L$-edge certificate, while at least $M$
irrelevant expansions were performed.  Since $M$ is arbitrary, certificate
stretch alone cannot bound overhead.
\end{proof}

AMLD+0 adds a second separation on top of this theorem.  Even if every local
channel eventually returns an optimal certificate relative to its own graph,
Player 0 may allocate arbitrarily large service to unproductive channels before
servicing the winning route.  Conversely, a good Player-Zero policy can reduce
wasted cross-channel work without changing the length of the final
certificate.  Therefore at least the following should be reported separately:
\begin{itemize}
    \item final certificate cost or length;
    \item local expansion counts by channel;
    \item verifier and BANK costs;
    \item Player-Zero service counts by channel;
    \item total charged settlement cost;
    \item baseline-versus-active controller difference.
\end{itemize}

\section{Efficient representations and fair settlement degree}

\begin{theorem}[Efficient GBG Isomorphism Preserves Fair Settlement Degree]
Consider two families of finitely encoded, exact singleton-target GBGs,
$\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal H$, equipped with settlement systems $M_G$ and
$M_H$.  Suppose there are mutually inverse translations $f$ and $g$, computable
with polynomial declared primitive work, between encoded instances and
corresponding state/action representations such that initial states and
settlement status are preserved, legal transitions are preserved bijectively,
action costs are preserved, encoded sizes are polynomially related, and
terminal certificates/verifier acceptance are transportable with polynomial
declared primitive work in both directions.  Then the associated settlement
pairs lie in the same fair settlement degree, and for corresponding states
$x$ and $f(x)$,
\[
\boxed{V_{M_G}(x)=V_{M_H}(f(x)).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\atlasnote

\begin{proof}
Each settling trajectory in $\mathcal G$ transports through $f$ to a
trajectory in $\mathcal H$ with the same accumulated action cost, and $g$
provides the converse.  Taking infima gives equality of Bellman values.  The
assumed polynomial construction, size, answer, and certificate-transport
bounds provide fair reductions in both directions, hence equality of fair
settlement degree \cite{tenneson-fair}.
\end{proof}

To preserve an AMLD+0 experiment rather than merely a local GBG, an even
stronger recoding is appropriate: the translation should also preserve typed
channel identity, controller observations, legal Player-Zero service actions,
BANK/verifier semantics, and the charged costs of controller decisions.  A map
that preserves only reachability can hide precisely the scheduling geometry
that AMLD+0 was introduced to study.

\section{A hierarchy of AMLD+0 singleton-search regimes}

The original paper placed singleton search on a spectrum from opaque guessing
to exact shortest-path knowledge.  AMLD+0 keeps that local spectrum and adds a
controller dimension.

At the local level:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{opaque guessing}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{weakly structured navigation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{strongly structured navigation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{exact local horizon knowledge}
\end{array}
}
\]

At the controller level:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{fixed baseline schedule}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{history-sensitive allocation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{BANK-aware allocation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{exact controller-level horizon knowledge}
\end{array}
}
\]

These spectra are independent in principle.  One may have a highly intelligent
local prover under a poor scheduler, or a sophisticated Player 0 allocating
turns among locally weak agents.  The most capable architecture combines
informative local state, effective local policies, useful shared verified
memory, and a controller that exploits only legitimate observations.

\section{The rewired hierarchy of quantities}

The original singleton-search synthesis separated target cardinality,
intrinsic horizon, observable information geometry, navigation policy, and
actual cost.  AMLD+0 requires one additional layer and makes the distinction
between local and global policy explicit:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{singleton query / target cardinality}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{typed settlement regions and intrinsic local horizons}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{observable information geometry}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{local settlement-agent policies}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{shared verifier-governed BANK state}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{Player-Zero service policy}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\[-2pt]
\text{actual verified settlement cost}
\end{array}}
\]

No arrow is reversible in general.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
    \item one query does not imply one settlement mode;
    \item a short intrinsic horizon does not imply that an admissible policy
    can identify it cheaply;
    \item informative local geometry does not imply good scheduling;
    \item good scheduling does not imply informative local geometry;
    \item an optimal final certificate does not imply low total search cost;
    \item no controller policy is permitted to alter verifier acceptance.
\end{itemize}

This layered non-equivalence is the conceptual space in which AMLD+0 is meant
to operate.

\section{Experimental consequences}

The rewiring suggests a direct experimental protocol for any singleton
benchmark.

First run the declared passive baseline $\sigma_0$.  Record the settlement
report, certificate, channel service counts, per-channel expansions, verifier
work, BANK activity, and total charged cost.  Then run an active Player-Zero
policy with the same formal system, initial BANK, local agents, verifier, cost
model, and contamination firewall.  Compare the runs using quantities such as
\[
G_0=C_{\sigma_0}-C_{\pi_0}.
\]

For a structured Ocean, one can ask whether Player 0 learns to allocate service
toward channels whose verified discoveries shorten subsequent horizons.  For
an opaque password control, the Player-Zero Noncreation of Information theorem
predicts no information-theoretic gain from scheduling alone.  For SAT or
other constraint-rich domains, features such as prefix pruning can be tested
as legitimate controller observations.

This produces a sharper empirical question than ``did the intelligent system
solve the singleton target faster?'':

\begin{quote}
Which part of the gain came from local navigation, which came from shared
verified discoveries, which came from Player-Zero allocation, and which was
already present in the passive baseline?
\end{quote}

That decomposition is precisely what the AMLD+0 architecture makes measurable.

\section{Conclusion}

Singleton enumeration remains a useful way to expose a basic misconception in
search: a one-element target set need not be computationally small.  The
AMLD+0 viewpoint strengthens the lesson by separating the uniqueness of the
mathematical query from the multiplicity of certified settlement routes and
from the controller problem of deciding where computation should go next.

The Ocean remains a valid one-player singleton GBG at the local level.  The
Universal Proof Horizon remains an intrinsic measure of shortest certified
routes.  Password space remains the opaque control case.  CNF pruning remains
an example of visible structure.  What changes is the architectural level at
which navigation is interpreted.

A local settlement agent may decide which vertex, lemma, assignment, or
certificate candidate to inspect.  Player 0 decides which enabled settlement
process receives the next turn.  The shared BANK transmits only
verifier-authorized resources.  The verifier remains the trust boundary.
Passive Player 0 recovers the old AMLD trajectory; active Player 0 exposes a
new, separately measurable source of operational performance.

The central conclusion of the rewired paper is therefore:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{singleton target complexity is not only a geometry of candidates;}\\
\text{under AMLD+0 it is also a geometry of certified attention allocation.}
\end{array}}
\]
But that additional control power has a precise limit.  Player 0 may exploit
structure already present in legal observations and verified shared state; it
cannot schedule information into existence, and it cannot schedule around the
verifier.
