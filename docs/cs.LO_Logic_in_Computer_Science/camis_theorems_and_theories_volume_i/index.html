CAMI’s Theorems and Theories — Volume I: The Antichain Frontier

PRELIMINARY RELEASE — October 2026.

Mathematical research exposition developed with CAMI (OpenAI's ChatGPT), with Brian Tenneson as research consultant. This online summary records the volume's results and assumptions; it is not a substitute for the full proof manuscript.

Scope

The volume studies chronological and ancestry frontiers of finite search trees, certified proof search, and sound scout delegation. All claims below depend on their stated structural or verifier assumptions.

Five results

  1. Clock-Frontier Antichain. In a directed search structure whose edges strictly increase an integer-valued time coordinate, no two distinct states at the same time are reachable from one another by a positive-length directed path.
  2. Nonchronological Maximal Frontier. In a finite rooted tree, every inclusion-maximal antichain in the ancestor order meets every root-to-leaf path.
  3. Certified Frontier Settlement Decomposition. Given a finite rooted search tree, a maximal ancestry antichain A, and an accepting target, an accepting root-to-leaf path exists if and only if some vertex of A has an accepting descendant (including itself where appropriate).
  4. Certified Scout Soundness Preservation. If the underlying verifier is sound and each accepted scout transfer is independently checked against that verifier, adding scouts does not introduce false certified BANK statements.
  5. Scout Partition Settlement Preservation. If an ancestry antichain partitions all root-to-leaf paths and every induced subtree is searched completely under the same acceptance criterion, settlement by the partition is equivalent to settlement by complete search of the original tree.

Research conjectures

Antichain-Guided Scout Advantage: suitable frontier selection may reduce charged discovery cost on structured instances. Omnicartographic Frontier Compression: certified multiresolution maps may compress the search frontier without losing target coverage. Recursive Graduate Scout Advantage: verifier-gated recursive scout spawning may offer net advantages on some fixed problem families after all costs are charged. These are conjectural research directions, not established universal speedup claims.

Proof sketches and limitations

For the clock frontier, a nonempty directed path increases time strictly, contradicting equal endpoint times. For maximal antichains in a finite rooted tree, if a root-to-leaf path missed the antichain, its leaf would be incomparable with every antichain element, contradicting maximality. The settlement decomposition follows by intersecting an accepting path with the antichain. Scout soundness is inherited from the sound verifier, not from scout creativity. Partition preservation follows by exhaustive coverage of all relevant paths, with completeness essential.

These statements do not prove general search acceleration, physical time travel, or any open Millennium Prize Problem.
