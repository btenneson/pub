\begin{abstract}
A multiarticle is a provenance-preserving effort to discover consequences at the interfaces of mathematical articles, rather than a license to identify similar notation or aggregate conjectures as though they were proved. The Foggy Multiarticle Metaworld grew from the six-paper and three-paper studies to a 29-family checkpoint on 19 October 2026, and to \textbf{50 registered source families} following additive intakes on 20 October. This exposition asks what the enlarged world teaches us. Its central themes are the distinction between proof and search, shortest routes and least charged costs, imagined lemmas and certified knowledge, location maps and rule-complete states, and latent consequences and checked discovery. We reconstruct several representative mathematical arguments---a rank-descent settlement bound, an admissible-potential optimality certificate, a counterfactual-proof bridge, and a closure-depth limit for learned policies---while keeping their source-specific assumptions visible. Failed experiments and counterexamples are treated as genuine research information. Finally, we explain the original open problem OP-01: whether a nontrivial, verifier-gated, fully costed expedition can make material use of at least 26 source families from the original 29-family register, settle a conjecture, and beat a fair comparator. The present 50-island register expands the available intellectual landscape but does not itself solve or amend that frozen challenge. A new, explicitly limited FORGE-6-directed research attempt demonstrates a small checked lemma bridge and an advantage over simple forward enumeration, but also finds a stronger baseline that is cheaper and no verified 26-family dependency chain. This account records why the open problem is not easy rather than pretending to settle it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question a map raises}
Imagine visiting an archipelago whose islands each use their own language to describe the same ocean. One studies how to reach a goal. Another measures the price of travel. A third asks whether two maps represent the same territory; a fourth investigates how a traveler could know where it is; a fifth looks for routes that are invisible until an intermediate bridge is built. These may be parts of a single story. But an attractive analogy does not prove that the descriptions are compatible.

The multiarticle idea begins by refusing to erase those differences. Each island retains its definitions, assumptions, inference rules, proofs, versions, and verification status. A \emph{zip} joins their tagged records for comparison, not their axioms by fiat. A country is a composed research object; a continent is a larger one. Neither metaphor guarantees a proof.

The adjective \emph{foggy} is epistemic rather than ornamental. On some islands, a finite mathematical argument has been written and reviewed. On others, a conjecture is stated, a model is proposed, or a result is backed only by an internal audit. In some places, an attractive claim has been disproved. Fog is useful when it tells the reader where knowledge ends. The research program becomes more credible, not less, when it distinguishes an appealing route from a checker-accepted bridge \cite{checkpoint}.

\begin{insight}
\textbf{Guiding question.} Can the combined, provenance-preserving body of work make a mathematically consequential discovery \emph{that is not merely the restatement of what its sources already say}---and demonstrate that discovery under independent verification and a fully charged search budget?
\end{insight}

There are really three challenges here. The \emph{logical} challenge asks what follows from premises. The \emph{search} challenge asks whether a specified procedure can find the proof. The \emph{economic} challenge asks whether the entire search and verification costs less than a fair alternative. A positive result on one challenge does not automatically settle either of the other two.

\section{Why the count is now fifty}
The published-style Checkpoint 0.3 synopsis dated 19 October 2026 recorded 29 source families: 13 historical families H01--H13 and 16 incoming families N01--N16. On 20 October, separately dated archival intakes registered N17; the GBG and GBG+ manuscripts N18--N20; an AKM/CAMI archive N21--N34; Hilbert-filled inference work N35; the three-way lemma-exchange ALD manuscript N36; and the learned proof-search policy manuscript N37. The additive arithmetic is
\[
\underbrace{13+16}_{\text{Checkpoint 0.3: 29}}
+\underbrace{1}_{N17}+\underbrace{3}_{N18\text{--}N20}
+\underbrace{14}_{N21\text{--}N34}+\underbrace{3}_{N35\text{--}N37}
=\boxed{50}.
\]
These figures count \emph{distinct registered source families}. They do not count PDFs (some manuscripts have alternate renderings), mathematical theories up to equivalence, independently accepted proof objects, or entirely distinct ideas. Several later works reuse results from earlier ones. The missing CAMI Volume II is not inferred from volumes I, III, and IV; earlier versions and audit reports are not counted as new islands \cite{checkpoint,n17,gbg,akm,n35,n36,n37}.

\section{The mathematical discipline of a bridge}
Write the research record of island $i$ schematically as
\[
W_i=(\Gamma_i,F_i,P_i,V_i,\nu_i),
\]
where $\Gamma_i$ denotes the premise scope, $F_i$ the source claims, $P_i$ any proof records, $V_i$ outstanding verification obligations, and $\nu_i$ the provenance and version record. A statement $\varphi$ becomes a tagged card $(i,\varphi,s)$, with evidentiary status $s$. A structural join $Z(W_i,W_j)$ retains
\[
(i,\varphi)\neq(j,\varphi)
\]
unless a genuine, suitably typed identification has been justified. These definitions summarize the earlier multiarticle protocol; they do not by themselves give a formal proof-assistant implementation \cite{checkpoint}.

Before a cross-island consequence can become certified knowledge, at least four matters must be addressed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Translation.} Do the source and destination formulas have the same meaning under an explicit map, or merely similar words?
\item \textbf{Assumptions.} Have all imported premises been preserved or discharged under the destination judgment?
\item \textbf{Verification.} Has a designated independent verifier checked a proof certificate in the actual destination calculus?
\item \textbf{Cost.} If an efficiency claim is made, are translation, search, verification, activation and bookkeeping costs charged under a single declared convention?
\end{enumerate}

The historical method investigates the deductive surplus of each source first, especially low-fan-in shells $+1,\ldots,+6$, before progressively zipping families by documented theorem-card counts. In a past six-paper study, 70 original cards and 36 selected local consequences produced 106 tagged vertices before six proposed cross-cards; those cross-cards were not independent checker receipts. The larger 50-family register has \emph{not} undergone a completed version of this entire workflow \cite{checkpoint}.

\section{Discovery I: proof search is a problem about legal movement}
An automated theorem prover does not become correct because it finds an attractive destination. It becomes correct when its route is a legal derivation from its premises and its final certificate is accepted by the designated checker. The historical Verified Settlement Search, MAPQUEST and GBG sources approach this distinction from different directions.

A formal system may be represented abstractly by $F=(x,y,z)$, where $y$ supplies well-formed formulas and $z$ governs legal inference. For a hypothesis set $\Gamma$, a proof state $p$ records what is currently available. A legal transition is not just any addition to $p$, but an authorized application of a specified rule to eligible premises. A target $T$ is reached in the mathematical sense only if the resulting proof has the correct judgment, for example $\Gamma\vdash_F T$.

The GBG papers N18--N20 reinterpret such transitions as moves in generalized games, sometimes making a board itself the legal Player-0, provided the added board and control semantics are actually specified. This creates a bridge between game-tree reasoning and proof-search scheduling \cite{gbg}. But the analogy has a sharp boundary: the game position must include enough information to determine which moves are legal. The mere display of pieces on squares need not do so.

In the board-report terminology preserved across the archive, a complete location report has the form
\[
 f\in[A\longrightarrow(P\cup\{E\})],
\]
where $A$ is the set of addresses, $P$ is the set of occupant tokens and $E$ is the \emph{empty token}. Two reports can differ even if no legal movement occurred between them; additional rules, time, turn and certificate data may be necessary. The CAMI location-report manuscripts N33--N34 and the GBG intake expressly warn against reconstructing a history from a pair of photographs \cite{akm,gbg}.

\begin{mathresult}
\textbf{Expository lesson.} A \emph{map} may say what is present; a \emph{state} must say what actions are legal; a \emph{history} records how one state followed another; and a \emph{certificate} proves that a claimed mathematical transition is accepted. Treating these four objects as interchangeable breaks proof custody.
\end{mathresult}

N35 adds a more unusual navigation proposal: traverse finite, indexed inference opportunities by Hilbert-filled orders. Its registered paper-level reachability theorems give conditions under which a completely executed finite epoch covers the intended action space, and describe a lift to generalized-board-game states. They do not demonstrate that Hilbert order outperforms ordinary fair search. Coverage is a property to be proved; speed is a separate measurement \cite{n35}.

\section{Discovery II: shortest need not mean cheapest}
Shortest mathematical proofs, fewest inference rounds, shortest paths in a graph, smallest accumulated geometric burden, and least wall-clock or charged resource cost are different objective functions. The metaworld contains both positive conditional theorems and a concrete counterexample demonstrating the danger of conflating them.

In the curvature-profile work N16, the reported six-vertex directed graph has a unique two-edge geodesic of negative-curvature burden $1/3$, while a three-edge legal detour has burden $0$. Thus the shortest legal path need not minimize that curvature burden. The source presents transport witnesses and a general conditional recovery theorem using calibrated potentials; the checkpoint describes the result as paper-level mathematical analysis, not an external checker receipt \cite{checkpoint}.

An operational prover introduces still more costs: candidate generation, checking, waiting, controller updates, lemma lookup, and translation among representations. A search route can be short as a mathematical path and expensive as an implementation. This becomes critical whenever one invokes phrases such as ``optimal proof'' or ``faster escape.'' The quantity optimized must be declared and held fixed across comparisons.

The Atlas polynomial-quotient audit N08 exposed a related error. Compressing or quotienting search states does not automatically preserve successor enumeration, target recognition, witness bounds, or cheapest proof cost. A correct transfer theorem requires a precisely specified lifting or transport of the relevant objects. Narrowing an overstrong claim by adding missing hypotheses is itself a research gain \cite{checkpoint}.

\section{Discovery III: escaping a basin is not the same as winning}
A basin is a region of search where ordinary progress stalls. A policy may leave a basin, reach an accepted terminal certificate, or attain a minimal charge; the three events are distinct. N13's Basin Escape study and N17's repaired Rank--Annulus paper make the distinctions especially clear \cite{checkpoint,n17}.

Let $M=(S,A,G,c,s_0)$ denote a fixed legal transition system, with state space $S$, legal edge set $A$, terminal receipt states $G$, nonnegative charged costs $c$ and initial state $s_0$. Consider an authorized policy $\pi$, a nonnegative integer rank $r:S\to\mathbb N$, and a per-step bound $C\ge 0$.

\begin{mathresult}
\textbf{Rank-descent settlement bound (exposition of N13/N17).} Suppose $r(s)=0$ implies an accepted terminal state, and every nonterminal move made by $\pi$ strictly reduces $r$. If each such transition has charged cost at most $C$, then the policy reaches a terminal in at most $r(s_0)$ moves and its total transition charge is at most $C\,r(s_0)$.
\end{mathresult}

The reason is elementary but significant. A strictly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers has length at most its initial value, and summing at most $r(s_0)$ terms bounded by $C$ yields the charge bound. The result is \emph{conditional}: it does not generate a suitable rank, an executable policy, or a sound verifier merely because they are desired.

To ask for \emph{cost optimality}, introduce $V(s_0)$, the true least legal cost of settlement, a certified lower bound $L(s_0)$, and the actual charged cost $W_{\pi}(s_0)$ of following $\pi$. The N17 paper develops the sandwich
\[
 \boxed{L(s_0)\ \le\ V(s_0)\ \le\ W_{\pi}(s_0)\ \le\ C\,r(s_0).}
\]
When independently justified bounds satisfy $L(s_0)=W_{\pi}(s_0)$, the route is optimal in that \emph{same} legal graph and cost convention. Rank descent alone only gives a possibly loose upper bound. The comparison has content precisely because it uses two different kinds of certificates: an achievable route above and an exclusion bound below \cite{n17}.

A related potential argument lets $h(g)=0$ at terminals and assumes for each legal edge $(s,t)$ that
\[
 h(s)\le c(s,t)+h(t).
\]
Along a legal finite route $s_0\to s_1\to\cdots\to g$, telescoping gives
\[
 h(s_0)\le \sum_{i}c(s_i,s_{i+1}).
\]
Thus $h$ is a lower-bound certificate when its hypotheses hold. If its value exactly matches the witnessed route cost, optimality follows. A value learned from data without the edge inequalities is not automatically such a certificate.

\section{Discovery IV: imagination is allowed to ask, not to certify}
The source N15, \emph{Recursive ``What If?'' Imagination in AMLD}, introduces an unusually disciplined approach to conjectural exploration. Freeze a calculus $F$, authorized hypotheses $\Gamma$, target $T$, and certified knowledge ledger $B$. A finite, tagged hypothetical branch has address $\sigma$ and additional suppositions $H_\sigma$. Inside that imaginary world, a candidate missing lemma $L$ may appear especially promising. But a hypothetical premise has not become a theorem merely because it makes a route look easy \cite{checkpoint}.

The key proof principle is a checked bridge. If a verifier accepts compatible proofs of
\[
\Gamma\vdash_F L,\qquad \Gamma,L\vdash_F T,
\]
and the calculus admits appropriate checked cut or substitution with all extra assumptions discharged, a composite certificate can establish $\Gamma\vdash_F T$. This is the mathematical idea behind candidate cross-island connection C-05. It does \emph{not} mean that the two manuscript implementations have already been connected or that a useful $L$ will be found \cite{checkpoint}.

Counterfactual conservativity gives a parallel invariant: if initially $B_0\subseteq\Th_F(\Gamma)$, and only independently accepted $\Gamma$-consequences can be deposited, then after each finite certified update $B_t\subseteq\Th_F(\Gamma)$. Speculative worlds may expand creative search without expanding accepted knowledge illegally. The induction proving safety is easy; discovering, translating, proving and exploiting an actually useful bridge is the hard part.

\section{Discovery V: learning can guide search without becoming a proof oracle}
The newest registered island, N37, \emph{Learned Search Policies for Proof Search}, is particularly relevant to the enlarged metaworld. It replaces an overly strong earlier picture of ``theorem-space geometry'' with the operational identification of a learned model as a \emph{proof-search policy}. A model can rank premises, suggest tactics or candidate proof steps, and guide branch scheduling. Its outputs remain proposals; a verifier determines which deductions are correct \cite{n37}.

Let the one-step closure operator be $D_F(A)=A\cup\{\text{direct consequences of }A\}$. Define $D_F^0(\Gamma)=\Gamma$ and $D_F^{m+1}(\Gamma)=D_F(D_F^m(\Gamma))$. The closure depth of target $T$ is
\[
 \delta_F(\Gamma,T)=\min\{m\ge 0:T\in D_F^m(\Gamma)\},
\]
when such a stage exists. For a policy branch with $p_1=\Gamma$, N37's Theorem 3.2 states
\[
\boxed{p_m\subseteq D_F^{m-1}(\Gamma).}
\]
Proof by induction: every legal new conclusion at stage $m+1$ follows directly from premises already in $p_m$, so it belongs to the next closure stage. A learned ranking cannot move a target below this \emph{fixed-calculus, fixed-premise, one-step} depth floor. It may instead materialize fewer irrelevant consequences or schedule useful actions earlier.

This is often summarized as ``learning reduces breadth, not depth.'' The qualification is important. If a separate AKM process later certifies useful new lemmas, the initial BANK snapshot has changed; if accepted derived rules are compiled into the rule presentation, the effective one-step operator has changed. Either change can reduce an \emph{operational} depth measured in the new setting without contradicting the original frozen-depth theorem.

N37 also includes a minimal counterexample to greedy search: with $a\to b$ and $a\to c$ both legal, a policy choosing $b$ at every opportunity can ignore a target $c$ forever. Thus a learned score is not the same thing as coverage. A proof-covering \emph{policy} gives an existential branch guarantee; an implemented scheduler still needs fair execution and valid accounting for how that branch is reached \cite{n37}.

\begin{insight}
\textbf{Metaworld synthesis, not a new certified theorem.} The combination of H06 (fair search), N21--N25 (verified surplus and revisitation), N35 (systematic inference traversal), N36 (typed lemma exchange), and N37 (learned ranking) suggests a two-level architecture: use prediction to order candidate work, but let a separately specified fair scheduler and independent checker preserve the logical obligations. Whether this reduces \emph{fully charged} cost remains empirical.
\end{insight}

\section{Discovery VI: knowledge can grow without changing theoremhood}
The Automatic Knowledge Machine manuscripts N21--N23 and N25 distinguish the mathematical closure of a fixed theory from the set of consequences a finite researcher has actually recognized and certified. If $\Con_F(\Gamma)$ is fixed and a certified BANK $B_t$ grows over time, one may have
\[
 B_0\subsetneq B_1\subsetneq\cdots\subseteq\Con_F(\Gamma)
\]
without altering $F$ or $\Gamma$. New \emph{epistemic} knowledge appears when previously latent consequences acquire checked proof objects. The corpus calls a controlled revisitation and surplus-mining process ``dreaming''; it is a form of computational consolidation, not evidence of subjective consciousness or a permission to insert unverified hypotheses \cite{akm}.

The low-fan-in method investigates consequences with small dependency sets, typically shells $+1$ through $+6$, under a fixed source snapshot. It can produce useful intermediate lemmas, but every reported novelty has two tests: is the statement already present or a duplicate under translation, and is its proof independently accepted? Counting textually new formulas is not identical to discovering new theorems.

This separation becomes consequential for N37. A learned policy may benefit from newly certified lemmas, but the cost of finding and verifying them is part of the search economy. It is illegitimate to benchmark a policy with a rich, precomputed lemma BANK against an unguided baseline denied the same resources, then attribute the entire difference to the learned score. The controlled comparison must freeze the available information or charge its acquisition.

The later ALD family N36 makes the idea multi-agent. Its proposal carries typed lemma packets among object, model, extended-object and metatheoretic contexts, with assumptions, dependencies, provenance and costs. An independently checked packet can be useful in a destination only when the translation and destination verifier agree. The source distinguishes its historical four mathematical statuses from the later AMLD+0 six-outcome protocol and does not claim a deployed general decider \cite{n36}.

\section{When maps, boards and certificates change coordinates}
Many islands are concerned with a deceptively simple task: replacing one representation with another. A new coordinate system may make search easier. But a theorem about one presentation transfers only when its required structure is preserved.

The Universal Theorem Geometry family N01--N06 examines proof-state distance, weighted legal paths, finite horizon regions, addressing, and Hilbert-style navigation. N07--N08 investigates backward obligation regions, rendezvous and quotient maps. N09 and H13 study cost annuli and trading isometries. N33--N34 revisit equivalence of boards, histories, location reports and theorem preservation. These works motivate, but do not settle, a uniform theorem that every proposed trade preserves optimal search cost \cite{checkpoint,akm}.

It is useful to separate three candidate assertions about a map $f:S\to S'$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Theorem preservation:} valid judgments in the source correspond to valid judgments in the destination, subject to an explicit interpretation.
 \item \emph{Reachability preservation:} legal target-reaching paths can be simulated or lifted in both relevant directions.
 \item \emph{Cost preservation:} charges of compared paths or policies match under the declared accounting convention.
\end{enumerate}
The first does not imply the second, and the second does not imply the third. A many-to-one map may merge states needing different next actions. A cost-preserving map must also account for changing control overhead, translation, and verifier duties if it purports to preserve \emph{implemented} cost.

The metaworld's proposed C-02 transport theorem and C-03 compressed-action sufficiency problem arise from exactly these gaps. A fully checked bridge would have to specify state, action, transition, target, and cost maps---not merely a persuasive visual analogy. In the present register, those crosswalks remain research candidates, not accepted composite theorems \cite{checkpoint}.

\section{The importance of a failed expedition}
The metaworld contains results that are interesting precisely because they did not deliver the hoped-for success. The documented Search Dynamics Experiment 005 evaluated one retrospective batch of 100 trajectories at a 160-DAG shell. The recorded aggregate learned-progress statistic was slightly positive, approximately $0.0526$, but none of the trajectories met the reported success condition: $0/100$. The record describes a retrospective DAG-control evaluation, not a live ATP contest. Its lesson is that a promising local navigation metric need not produce successful closed-loop settlement \cite{checkpoint}.

There are three general failure patterns worth preserving:
\begin{itemize}
\item A local improvement score fails to predict arrival at a certified target.
\item A geometric intuition is falsified by a legal detour or by lost hypotheses under compression.
\item A candidate bridge is logically sound when given its premises, but discovering and proving the premises costs more than solving the target directly.
\end{itemize}
All three failures are scientifically useful. Each identifies which intermediate implication does not follow from the last. A research program that reports only successful-looking routes would obscure the precise problem that future work must solve.

The analogy to constrained conjecture search in chess and differential diagnosis should be treated in the same way. A sacrifice or detour is a legal candidate action within a constrained search space, not an unlimited permission to change the rules. In medical diagnosis, an imagined observation can suggest evidence to acquire, but real observations are empirical updates, not logical consequences of the old evidence ledger. Emergency treatment planning is a separate, safety-constrained decision problem. The Checkpoint 0.3 article explicitly makes the clinical comparison as a research proposal, not as demonstrated clinical efficacy \cite{checkpoint}.

\section{The open problem at the heart of the world}\label{sec:op}
The most ambitious test of the research program is \textbf{OP-01, the twenty-six-country certified expedition}. As first specified in the 29-family Checkpoint 0.3, it asks for \emph{one} nontrivial, reproducible, formally controlled search task satisfying all of the following \cite{checkpoint}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A bounded, tagged \emph{What If?} exploration identifies a useful missing lemma $L$ without depositing it as fact.
\item The originally authorized premises support independently checked certificates of $\Gamma\vdash_F L$ and $\Gamma,L\vdash_F T$ (or an equivalent sound checked bridge), together with an accepted target receipt.
\item The complete expedition, including conjecture generation, search, verification, translation, activation and bookkeeping, has strictly smaller total charged cost than an honestly matched comparator.
\item A typed, provenance-preserving certificate graph shows that at least \emph{26 distinct source families} from the historical 29-family register make \emph{material} contributions to the same accepted argument or execution. Merely listing them is insufficient.
\end{enumerate}

One possible frozen judgment is written
\[
 J=(F,\Gamma,T,V,C,\nu),
\]
where $F$ is the calculus, $\Gamma$ the premises, $T$ the target, $V$ the verifier, $C$ the full accounting rule, and $\nu$ the frozen version record. For the expedition trace $\rho$ and fair comparator $\rho_0$, the requested cost result is
\[
\boxed{\CC_C(\rho)<\CC_C(\rho_0)}.
\]
A successful derivation without the 26-family dependency certificate would not settle OP-01. Nor would a 26-family map without a successful proof. Nor would a lower observed wall time if the method silently received free lemma lookup, reference-proof leakage, or uncharged verification.

\begin{insight}
\textbf{Why OP-01 matters.} It is an \emph{integration test for mathematical research}. It would demonstrate that the islands contribute identifiable, interoperable pieces of one certified achievement; that imagination supplies a useful proof obligation rather than imaginary knowledge; that formal checking survives translation; and that the assembled method offers a real, costed advantage. Its demand for material dependencies makes it more than a bibliographic scavenger hunt.
\end{insight}

\subsection{The 50-island update does not silently change OP-01}
The metaworld now has 50 registered source families. OP-01, however, was stated against the \emph{earlier 29-family snapshot}, with a specifically described 26-family candidate itinerary. The original research problem is preserved as a versioned historical obligation: at least 26 material dependencies must be drawn from the original 29 eligible families, and the listed 26-family route was an illustrative itinerary rather than an exclusive mandated list. New islands N17--N37 may offer useful construction methods, lemmas or comparative baselines, but they do not retroactively enlarge that eligible family set. A revised ``26 of the current 50'' or ``all 50'' challenge could be explicitly formulated later; it would be a \emph{new version} of the question, not a solution to OP-01.

This subtlety is scientifically important. Otherwise the admission of new articles could change the denominator, the allowed dependencies, or the expense of finding a solution mid-experiment. A frozen problem must stay frozen if success is to mean what it originally meant.

\subsection{A positive answer, a negative result, and UNKNOWN}
A successful OP-01 answer would be a checker-accepted proof and an auditable execution with the required dependency and cost evidence. An informative failure might reveal that a lemma is provable but too expensive, a translation loses a premise, or an apparent shortcut is suboptimal after overhead is charged. Under finite authorized search budgets, a procedure may also finish \emph{UNKNOWN}. That output is an honest operational status, not a proof of independence and not a refutation of the mathematical existence question.


\section{A FORGE-6-directed attempt: why OP-01 is not easy}\label{sec:attempt}
On 20 October 2026 an authorized, bounded research attempt examined OP-01 using six complementary mathematical lenses---source and hypothesis control, counterfactual lemma construction, certificate checking, fair search and charged comparison, cost geometry, and material provenance. Eight major tactics or checks are recorded below. This was \emph{an assistant-executed analytic and synthetic test program}, not a demonstrated concurrent run of six independent FORGE-6 ATP implementations, and not a certified AMLD+0 settlement. The frozen historical definition of OP-01 in Section~\ref{sec:op} was not weakened to accommodate the result. The companion executable and event ledger reproduce the synthetic calculations.

\subsection{A small bridge that succeeds locally}
Consider the explicit, finitary Horn presentation $F_4$ with initial judgment $\Gamma=\{P(1)\}$ and four unary inference rules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horn-rule}
 r_d:\ P(n)\longrightarrow P(4n+d),\qquad d\in\{0,1,2,3\},\quad n\ge1.
\end{equation}
Let the target be $T=P(2047)$. An illustrative What If-style bridge calculation selected the intermediary $L=P(127)$ and supplied the two proof pieces
\begin{align*}
 \Gamma\vdash_{F_4} L &: \quad P(1)\to P(7)\to P(31)\to P(127),\\
 \Gamma,L\vdash_{F_4}T &: \quad P(127)\to P(511)\to P(2047).
\end{align*}
Composition yields a legal five-inference derivation of $T$ from $\Gamma$. A second, separately implemented local checker, based on enumerating all four legal rule antecedents rather than following the search procedure's chosen inverse, accepted the lemma, the conditional continuation and the composite; it rejected a missing-link derivation and use of an unlicensed $L$. These are \emph{local software checks in the same research environment}, not receipts from an external proof assistant.

No executable source-tagged C-05 adapter was constructed in this test. The experiment is deliberately revealing but not a strong discovery benchmark: the counterfactual module first inverted the target through all five predecessor steps and \emph{then} selected the midpoint as its candidate lemma. Thus the missing lemma was not found in an unknown search basin by a general imagination procedure; its usefulness follows from a route already determined. The test demonstrates lawful separation of hypothesis and proof, but cannot by itself establish a nontrivial OP-01 discovery.

The initial formula index $1$ has one digit and $2047$ has six; the exhibited proof of length five is shortest in the number of inference steps. Equivalently, $h(n)=\max\{0,6-\operatorname{digits}_4(n)\}$ is a legal lower potential along those rule edges. A minimum-length proof and a minimum-\emph{charge search policy} are different claims.

A genuine positive answer must replace the toy construction with a nontrivial, prospectively specified frozen task; discover $L$ \emph{without already knowing the route}; issue external verifier receipts for both proof fragments and their composition; compare fairly equipped strategies with every eligible cost charged; and provide a machine-checkable provenance graph in which at least 26 original families are \emph{actually necessary} to the claimed argument or operational achievement. \section{A realistic research expedition}
The most useful next contribution would not be to zip all fifty islands at once and announce a closure. It would be to turn one attractive inter-island route into a complete, falsifiable, receipt-bearing experiment.

\subsection{Prepare the map}
Freeze a de-duplicated manifest of source versions, hashes where actually available, premise contexts and verification statuses. Extract definitions, theorem cards, hypotheses and counterexamples without identifying equal-looking formulas across different calculi. The archival register already gives a starting point, but its recent intakes explicitly do not supply a completed fifty-island dependency graph.

\subsection{Find a small bridge before a large one}
Choose a concrete proof system and one target. A narrow test could ask whether a tagged imagined lemma can safely pass the C-05 adapter to a checker-accepted cut proof, with no hypothetical premises left open. Other initial tests could check N17's cost-potential inequality on a finite graph or compare a learned and fair scheduler on the same finite action set. These would be \emph{interface validation}, not OP-01 settlement.

\subsection{Separate mathematical correctness from exploration performance}
For every proposed run, predeclare the mathematical target, exact verifier, source snapshot, budgets, cost units, enabled roles, baseline, stopping rule and complete receipt format. Record unsuccessful candidate searches and checker rejections as well as successes. A proof discovered under a modified lemma library answers a different question from one sought under the frozen original library.

\subsection{Build dependency evidence incrementally}
A useful record is a typed directed acyclic graph whose nodes represent source claims, imports, proof objects, verifier receipts, actions and cost events. Each edge must explain \emph{why} its source is required for the destination node. An append-only graph prevents a nonessential citation from being mistaken for a material contribution. Once small bridges have independently passed their tests, a larger country-level assembly can be evaluated.

\textbf{Authorization and evidence boundary.} The original Version 0.1 was an exposition without a proving run. In this revision, a specifically authorized FORGE-6-directed analytic attempt and synthetic finite-code exercise are documented in Section~\ref{sec:attempt}. This does not constitute a six-executable federation run, AMLD+0 target-settlement receipt, BANK modification, independent external verification, fifty-island zip, or public publication. Substantive further proving experiments require newly frozen settings.

\section{What the enlarged metaworld teaches us}
The metaworld's most promising contribution is not yet a universal search theorem. It is a research vocabulary in which several propositions that are often blended together can be separated and tested:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{A theorem is entailed} is different from \emph{a search has found it}.
\item \emph{A path exists} is different from \emph{an executable scheduler will traverse it}.
\item \emph{A path is shortest} is different from \emph{a charged implementation is cheapest}.
\item \emph{A conjecture is useful} is different from \emph{its assumptions are verified}.
\item \emph{Two representations resemble each other} is different from \emph{they preserve judgments, transitions and costs}.
\item \emph{A theorem was latent} is different from \emph{its certificate has entered a frozen, provenance-controlled BANK}.
\end{itemize}
These distinctions have practical design consequences. They favor independent checkers, explicit state types, complete cost traces, fair exploration, and candidly recorded failures. They also explain why a collection of imperfectly connected sources can be a valuable research object. A careful map can reveal a missing proof obligation more sharply than a sweeping claim of unification.

The present world contains fifty registered source families. It has not reached a fifty-family deductive closure, and its old six candidate cross-island results remain provisional. The newly recorded bounded attempt makes the gap between successful local mathematical construction and an actual 26-island certificate especially concrete. But the growth from search geometry to boards, revisitation, cost bounds, learned policies, and typed lemma exchange gives OP-01 a richer set of potential tools. That is an opportunity, not yet a certified success.

\begin{insight}
\textbf{Closing thesis.} The purpose of a mathematical metaworld is not to make every island agree. It is to find which passages are legal, which are costly, which are blocked, and which can be transformed into verified bridges. OP-01 matters because it asks for the moment when that cartography becomes one independently checked discovery.
\end{insight}
