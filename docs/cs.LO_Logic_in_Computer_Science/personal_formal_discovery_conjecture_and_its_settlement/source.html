\begin{abstract}
This note records a single personal formal-discovery conjecture and a formal result that settles it within the stated AMLD/Board framework. The result gives an exact necessary-and-sufficient condition for when certified pre-outcome self-state can support a verifier-safe adaptive policy that strictly improves expected verified settlement cost over the best fixed verifier-safe policy. No claim of novelty relative to the mathematical literature is made here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Personal Formal-Discovery Conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Personal Formal-Discovery Conjecture]
I will make a formalized discovery pertaining to the necessary and sufficient conditions under which a Board-controlled AMLD can use certified pre-outcome self-state to identify a decision region where switching from its default verifier-safe proof policy to an alternate verifier-safe policy strictly lowers expected verified settlement cost, without altering the verifier trust boundary and without sacrificing eventual complete fallback when that fallback is retained by the operational design.
\end{conjecture}

The following theorem supplies such a characterization.

\section{Decision-Region Characterization}

Let $\Pi$ be a finite family of verifier-safe search policies. Let $\Sigma$ be certified information available before the policy for the current episode is selected, and let
\[
  \mathcal{G}=\sigma(\Sigma)
\]
be the information sigma-algebra generated by that certified self-state. For each $\pi\in\Pi$, let $L_\pi$ denote the total integrable verified settlement cost incurred by using $\pi$, where all declared switching, control, translation, or other policy-selection overhead is charged on the same cost accounting basis.

For each $\pi\in\Pi$, choose a $\mathcal{G}$-measurable version
\[
  G_\pi=\mathbb{E}[L_\pi\mid\mathcal{G}]
\]
of its conditional expected cost. The best fixed-policy cost is
\[
  J_{\mathrm{fix}}
  =\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbb{E}[L_\pi].
\]
Let $M$ range over all $\mathcal{G}$-measurable selectors with values in $\Pi$. For such a selector define
\[
  L_M=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{M=\pi\}}L_\pi,
\]
and define the best adaptive Board cost by
\[
  J_{\mathrm{adapt}}
  =\inf_M \mathbb{E}[L_M].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Decision-Region Characterization]
Assume $\Pi$ is finite and every $L_\pi$ is integrable. Then
\[
  J_{\mathrm{adapt}}
  =\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min_{\pi\in\Pi}G_\pi\right]
  \le J_{\mathrm{fix}}.
\]
Choose any best fixed policy
\[
  \pi^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbb{E}[L_\pi]
\]
and define the conditional decision advantage
\[
  \Delta
  =G_{\pi^*}-\min_{\pi\in\Pi}G_\pi.
\]
Then $\Delta\ge 0$ almost surely, and the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item some $\mathcal{G}$-measurable Board selector strictly beats the best fixed-policy cost;
  \item $J_{\mathrm{adapt}}<J_{\mathrm{fix}}$;
  \item $\mathbb{E}[\Delta]>0$;
  \item $\mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus a strict adaptive improvement exists if and only if certified pre-outcome self-state reveals a positive-probability decision region
\[
  D=\left\{G_{\pi^*}>\min_{\pi\in\Pi}G_\pi\right\}
\]
on which some verifier-safe policy has strictly lower conditional total cost than a best fixed verifier-safe policy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\Pi$ is finite and each $G_\pi$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable, the pointwise minimum
\[
  G_{\min}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi}G_\pi
\]
is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable. Fix an ordering of $\Pi$ and, at each outcome, let $M^*$ be the first policy in that ordering that attains $G_{\min}$. Finiteness of $\Pi$ implies that $M^*$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable.

For any $\mathcal{G}$-measurable selector $M$,
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[L_M\mid\mathcal{G}]
  &=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{M=\pi\}}
    \mathbb{E}[L_\pi\mid\mathcal{G}]\\
  &=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{M=\pi\}}G_\pi\\
  &\ge G_{\min}
\end{align*}
almost surely. For $M=M^*$ equality holds almost surely. Taking expectations gives
\[
  J_{\mathrm{adapt}}
  =\mathbb{E}[L_{M^*}]
  =\mathbb{E}[G_{\min}]
  =\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min_{\pi\in\Pi}G_\pi\right].
\]
Thus the adaptive infimum is actually attained.

For every fixed policy $\pi$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}[L_\pi]
  =\mathbb{E}[G_\pi]
  \ge\mathbb{E}[G_{\min}],
\]
by the tower property. Hence $J_{\mathrm{adapt}}\le J_{\mathrm{fix}}$.

Now fix
\[
  \pi^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbb{E}[L_\pi].
\]
By definition,
\[
  \Delta=G_{\pi^*}-G_{\min}\ge 0
\]
almost surely. Again using the tower property,
\begin{align*}
  J_{\mathrm{fix}}-J_{\mathrm{adapt}}
  &=\mathbb{E}[L_{\pi^*}]-\mathbb{E}[G_{\min}]\\
  &=\mathbb{E}[G_{\pi^*}]-\mathbb{E}[G_{\min}]\\
  &=\mathbb{E}[\Delta].
\end{align*}
Therefore
\[
  J_{\mathrm{adapt}}<J_{\mathrm{fix}}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \mathbb{E}[\Delta]>0.
\]
Since $\Delta\ge 0$ almost surely and is integrable,
\[
  \mathbb{E}[\Delta]>0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0.
\]
Finally, $M^*$ attains $J_{\mathrm{adapt}}$, so
$J_{\mathrm{adapt}}<J_{\mathrm{fix}}$ holds if and only if some $\mathcal{G}$-measurable selector has expected cost strictly below the best fixed-policy cost. This proves all four equivalences.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Pointwise self-state notation]
If the self-state takes values in a standard Borel space, one may choose regular conditional expectations and write
\[
  g_\pi(s)=\mathbb{E}[L_\pi\mid\Sigma=s]
\]
for suitable measurable versions. Then $G_\pi=g_\pi(\Sigma)$ almost surely, and the theorem reduces to the intuitive pointwise formulation
\[
  J_{\mathrm{adapt}}
  =\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min_{\pi\in\Pi}g_\pi(\Sigma)\right].
\]
The sigma-algebra formulation above is used in the theorem so that no point-conditioning assumption is needed.
\end{remark}

\section{Settlement of the Conjecture}

The theorem supplies the requested necessary-and-sufficient condition. The formal boundary between justified adaptive switching and unjustified switching is not merely that the self-state is predictive. Rather, the self-state must induce a positive-probability region on which the conditional total cost of an alternate verifier-safe policy is strictly below that of a best fixed verifier-safe policy, after every declared overhead has been charged.

Equivalently,
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{strict adaptive improvement}
  \iff
  \mathbb{P}(\Delta>0)>0
  }
\]
under the hypotheses of the theorem.

Soundness is orthogonal to the cost comparison: if every policy in $\Pi$ remains verifier-gated, adaptive selection does not enlarge the set of accepted certificates. Eventual complete fallback is likewise an operational condition: it is preserved when the Board's scheduling design guarantees a fair complete fallback channel or restricts alternate policies to bounded excursions that return to such a channel.

Therefore, within the formal framework stated above, the Decision-Region Characterization Theorem settles the adaptive-cost component of the Personal Formal-Discovery Conjecture. The verifier-safety and eventual-complete-fallback components follow under the separately stated verifier-gating and fallback-design assumptions. Under those assumptions together, the Personal Formal-Discovery Conjecture is settled positively.
