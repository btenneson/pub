ALD Implementation Policies: Creativity,
Shared Memory, and the
Excluded-Middle Benchmark
Working policies for moving the Automatic Logic Decider from
mathematical framework to benchmarkable code

1. Purpose
The immediate engineering goal is to implement an Automatic Logic Decider (ALD) that
receives a formal environment A and a conjecture C, runs distinct Prover, Refuter, and
Independence searches over a shared verifier-certified mathematical memory, and returns a
settlement only when it possesses an appropriate certificate. The first benchmark target is the
law of excluded middle, C := φ ∨ ¬φ.
The implementation must preserve the central separation already established in the research
framework: verification determines correctness; search determines attention. Creativity therefore
changes which legal possibilities receive computational resources, not the axioms, inference
rules, side conditions, or acceptance standard.


2. Benchmark semantics: every answer is relative to
a formal system

Policy 2.1 — Freeze the formal environment before search
Every benchmark run must identify the formal system A, the target C, the representation of ¬C,
the admissible inference rules, the verifier, and any background assumptions before the run
begins. The ALD must never report that excluded middle is simply 'true' or 'false' without
specifying the formal environment relative to which the certificate was produced.
Rationale. The current ALD framework explicitly makes settlement relative to A. Its
excluded-middle example notes that φ ∨ ¬φ is valid in classical propositional logic but is not
generally derivable in intuitionistic logic. The benchmark is therefore partly a test that the
software respects its formal environment rather than silently importing classical assumptions.


Policy 2.2 — Use certificate-based settlement only
The public settlement labels are PROVED, REFUTED, and INDEPENDENT. PROVED requires
a verifier-accepted certificate for A ⊢ C. REFUTED requires a verifier-accepted certificate for A ⊢

¬C. INDEPENDENT requires an appropriate metamathematical certificate establishing the
required non-derivability or relative-consistency/model conditions for both sides.
Rationale. Failure to discover a proof of C or ¬C is not a certificate of independence. The code
must embody this distinction rather than relying on operator judgment after a long search.


Policy 2.3 — Add non-settlement execution states
The implementation must also expose at least BOUNDED_UNKNOWN and
IMPLEMENTATION_FAILURE as run statuses. BOUNDED_UNKNOWN means that the frozen
resource limit was reached without a settlement certificate. IMPLEMENTATION_FAILURE
means that a crash, verifier error, corrupted checkpoint, incompatible formal environment, or
other software failure prevented a valid conclusion.
Rationale. The mathematical CSA function is partial, and the creativity protocol already requires
a clean distinction among verified success, bounded unknown, frontier exhaustion, and
implementation failure. These execution states prevent a finite benchmark from pretending to
have solved an undecided mathematical search.


3. Agent architecture

Policy 3.1 — Keep three logically distinct objectives
The initial ALD shall contain three named objective roles: Prover P searches for A ⊢ C; Refuter
R searches for A ⊢ ¬C; Independence I searches for an appropriate independence certificate.
The roles may use different internal algorithms and do not need identical inference mechanisms.
Rationale. The architecture is intended to combine competing settlement objectives with
cooperation over verified mathematical knowledge. Treating the three roles as merely three
copies of one search policy would discard the principal design advantage.


Policy 3.2 — Preserve private search state
Each agent shall maintain its own frontier, ranking state, random state, learned-policy state,
restart history, local hypotheses, and search-control parameters unless a particular experiment
explicitly declares some of these shared.
Rationale. The shared bank should transmit mathematical discoveries without forcing all agents
into the same attentional trajectory. Sharing full frontier state by default risks converting a
heterogeneous population into one effectively duplicated search.


Policy 3.3 — Make agent profiles genuinely heterogeneous
At least during creativity experiments, P, R, and I should be allowed different configurations of
action-selection temperature, candidate width, breadth-versus-depth allocation, novelty
pressure, restart behavior, lemma-construction willingness, and proof-length tolerance.
Heterogeneity must be logged and reproducible.

4. Shared verified lemma bank

Policy 4.1 — The bank is append-only and monotonic
Let L_t be the verified mathematical bank after round t. A verified deposit D_t updates the bank
by L_{t+1} = L_t ∪ D_t. Accepted mathematical knowledge is not deleted during a run.
Rationale. Monotonic shared memory is the central cooperation mechanism in the ALD
framework. A result produced for one settlement objective becomes available to all later
searches.


Policy 4.2 — Nothing enters the shared bank without verification
Agents may propose speculative, stochastic, learned, high-temperature, or otherwise risky
candidate statements, but a candidate becomes shared mathematical infrastructure only after
the trusted verifier accepts it.
Rationale. This keeps aggressive creative search compatible with logical integrity. Search may
be wrong frequently; the shared bank must not be.


Policy 4.3 — Share verified lemmas, not mandatory attention
Every verified lemma becomes available to every agent, but no agent is required to immediately
expand from it. Each agent's ranking policy decides whether and when the new lemma
deserves attention.
Rationale. This is the main defense against bank-induced convergence. Shared knowledge
should enlarge each agent's legal opportunity set without collapsing the population's diversity.


Policy 4.4 — Record provenance and cross-objective reuse
Each LemmaRecord should contain at least: lemma_id, formal statement, normalized form or
hash, producing agent, producing objective, parent premises, verification result and verifier
version, production cost, deposit time or expansion, novelty features, reuse count, and
cross-objective reuse count.
Rationale. We want to measure whether the bank itself creates value. A Prover lemma later
used productively by Refuter or Independence is stronger evidence for inter-agent cooperation
than a lemma used only by its producer.

5. Creativity policy

Policy 5.1 — Optimize verified creative yield, not raw exploration
For a fixed target and frozen protocol, use the existing creativity objective C(u) = D(u)N(u),
where D is the probability of verified success and N is expected normalized structural novelty
conditional on success. Do not equate maximum temperature, entropy, randomization, or
novelty pressure with maximum creativity.
Rationale. The creativity manuscript proves that increasing exploration can strictly reduce
success and creative yield, that equal entropy can correspond to different creative yield, and
that novelty alone can select a worse prover.


Policy 5.2 — Permit controlled counterfactual admission
Every proof-oriented agent should retain a predeclared mechanism for occasionally admitting a
legal candidate outside the ordinary learned or heuristic candidate cap. The probability or
budget for such admissions must be bounded, logged, and included in total cost.
Rationale. The counterfactual-rescue result gives a precise case where a necessary
proof-enabling action lies outside a deterministic cap: the capped policy has zero success
probability, while a counterfactual policy can restore positive success probability.


Policy 5.3 — Use adaptive creativity only through explicit signals
A later ALD controller may increase exploration after predeclared stagnation signals and
decrease it after verified progress. Candidate signals include repeated frontier states, prolonged
absence of new verified lemmas, repeated failure of the highest-ranked branch family, low
novelty of admitted actions, or prolonged lack of goal reduction.
Rationale. Adaptive creativity is theoretically worth testing because the adaptive policy class can
include fixed policies, but empirical superiority is not guaranteed. Controller overhead, bad
signals, or overfitting can make adaptation worse.
